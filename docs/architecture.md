## 1. Purpose and bounds

**Does:**
- gives the canvas kinds. Each kind is the platform's code, shared by all modules, and a module chooses the kind for each task and sets its settings (concepts 8 spec; R02 spec). `canvas` has built both kinds of the first slice (R41 spec; product 10 spec): the handwriting sheet — for olympiad math and short essays in English —, and the workbook — for school math of grade 3. The workbook `canvas` detailed itself — the page, the answer slots, the slot's crop and the template layer (architecture 3.2 spec) —, by epic E6 (R30): the decision is R34, how it is built section 3.8. There is no text canvas in the first slice (R41 spec), and `canvas` does not build it (R21). The other kinds — the sandbox and the creative canvas (concepts 8 spec) — are not in the first slice. The code canvas is open question О-20;
- puts the canvas together from layers, bottom to top: the author's template, the module's widget, the author's demo, the learner's work, the tutor's layer. The tutor's sketch is drawn under the learner's writing, the frames and the marks above it (concepts 8 spec; architecture 3.2 spec). On the sheet's template is the task's grid, on the workbook's template its item lines with boxes (R36), and the task statement stands above the sheet or the workbook page, in the element itself (R23, R34);
- gives the tools the task allows: pen, eraser, ruler, protractor, calculator, keyboard, microphone. The eraser removes a stroke whole: the tutor's marks are tied to the learner's strokes, and an erased half of a stroke would break them (concepts 8 spec). There is no microphone in the first slice: voice input is not in it (product 10 spec). The learner's voice is not stored and lives only in the memory of the request (privacy 4 spec), and children's voice input waits for the author's decision on Claude's analysis (П-5; R112 spec; the recommendation of О-6 — for adults from the beta);
- captures the learner's strokes with time and pressure (architecture 3.2 spec);
- shows the lesson's worked example — the segment `worked_example` — on a sheet the learner does not write on: the statement above the sheet, and the parts of the author's demo the learner opens one at a time and can hide the whole demo. Paragraphs and pictures beside the sheet stand as a list above the sheet, and pictures in sheet areas on the sheet itself, on the demo layer (architecture 3.2 spec; R58 spec; R42, section 3.9). The lesson and the player of its segments are `app` (architecture 3.1 spec);
- shows the module's content — the statement, formulas, media files — by the task's open view, that is, without the key, the solution and the traps (architecture 3.2 spec; R05 spec). The statement is text and pictures, and the tasks do not need formulas yet (R23);
- draws the crop of the work for the check — by A3, on a sheet of 1000 × 1400 logical units: it is the crop that goes to the check, not the strokes (architecture 3.2 spec; R09 spec; R06, section 3.2). On the workbook, a crop of each filled box by its strokes, and the draft outside the boxes does not go to the check (R34, R38);
- shows the tutor's marks on the tutor's layer, tied to the learner's strokes (architecture 3.2 spec; R43 canvas-api). On the workbook a mark stands on an answer slot and holds on to the strokes of its box (R39). The marks do not change or cover the learner's work (R05 spec);
- is built as a separate build — the samples page for eval (architecture 3.2 spec; R25 spec):
  - a sample is made with the same canvas as the learners have, and is saved as a file on the device;
  - an adult's sample is for a measurement, and then it goes to the measurement's organizer, outside git, or for a module's eval set, and then it is a pull request to `modules`. The purpose is chosen on the page, and the page takes no consent: the written consent is a signed form outside it (R11, section 3.6);
  - a child's sample is with a separate consent and without a pull request: it does not get into the repositories, and where the file goes and who has access to it is decided by П-10 and П-12;
  - nothing goes to the platform's server meanwhile;
  - for the first measurement, without hosting: the build is downloaded from the release of `canvas` and opened on the device, and the tasks and media files are put into it at build time, from the release of `modules` by the CLI of `module-kit`. While it runs the page goes to no network.

  Samples are needed for the measurement of AI reading at stage 2 — including samples where adults imitate children's handwriting (repositories 6 spec; П-10);
- from stage 4, the widget host: the application's side of the widget protocol (architecture 3.2 spec; repositories 3 spec). The widget itself runs in an iframe sandbox without `allow-same-origin`, on a separate registrable domain, and talks to the application only by the protocol from `spec` (architecture 3.4 spec; R16 spec).

**Does not:**
- knows no accounts, no `api` and no subjects (architecture 3.2 spec). It calls no one over the network (repositories 2.1 spec): the picture of the work is sent to `api` by `app` (architecture 4.1 spec);
- does not change the learner's work: the tutor's layer does not touch or cover the writing (R05 spec; concepts 8 spec);
- does not store the strokes or send them to the server. The strokes with time and pressure are on the device, and what is stored there and how much is decided by `app` (architecture 3.1 spec; R09 spec);
- does not read the work and does not give a verdict: the work is read by AI through `tutor`, the verdict is given by `module-kit` (architecture 1 spec; R03 spec);
- does not receive the key, the solution and the traps of an unsolved task: it is given only the open view (R05 spec; architecture 3.1 spec);
- runs no foreign code in the page: the code of the modules' widgets runs only in an iframe sandbox, and in the first slice there are no widgets at all (R16 spec; architecture 3.4, 6 spec);
- the samples page does not take learners' work into `modules`: it is not part of the modules, and eval takes it only with a separate consent and keeps it outside the repositories (concepts 3 spec; П-10). So the page makes a child's sample without a pull request (R25 spec).

**Data and links:**
- it has no data of its own (architecture 3.2 spec). The learner's strokes and artifacts are on the device — `app` holds them (architecture 3.1, 5 spec). An artifact is work that is a result in itself: a drawing, an essay, a recording. The work on a task with a key — strokes, answers, the submission context — is not an artifact, and the device erases it at the end of the attempt (concepts 8 spec; R47). The repository has no learners' data and no secrets (RULES.md, Hard rules): the strokes in tests and examples are only synthetic;
- the consumers are `app` and the samples page; later the module preview for the author and the card of the MCP channel (architecture 3.2 spec). `app` builds the package in at an exact version, and during development uses its working copy. The second consumer — a site or the card of the MCP channel — gets a second build of the same package (architecture 6 spec; R16 spec). And `tutor`'s CI takes synthetic samples from the release of `canvas` — by exact tag and sum (section 3.4; R38 spec);
- at build time it depends on nothing but `spec`, and over the network it calls no one; `app` depends on it at build time (repositories 2.1, 3 spec). The CI of the samples page's build takes the tasks and media files by exact tag: until the build has moved to `modules` (Д4 in RUN.md), the file of open views of the measurement's tasks from the release of `tutor`, and after that the CLI of `module-kit` and the modules' packages in the release of `modules`, without the versions that the index of the newest release marked withdrawn (R62 spec, R63 spec). CI takes each such release file only once `spec`'s recipe `release-verify` has accepted its signature (R77 spec). This is a link of the kind "CI tool", not a dependency at build time: so the tasks get into the build, and the package itself knows no subjects (the 2026-10-21 amendment to R25 spec; repositories 1 spec; architecture 3.2 spec). The reverse link: `tutor`'s CI takes `canvas`'s synthetic samples (R38 spec). The code takes `spec` from its releases, not from the submodule (R21 spec; R64 spec): the format's types as the npm package `@altnovatrail/spec` in `dependencies`, at the same version as the submodule's tag (R73 spec; R41), and the signature of each package of `@altnovatrail` in the lock file is checked in CI by `spec`'s recipe `release-verify-npm` (R77 spec; R44). The module format is JSON Schema 2020-12 (R45 spec), and the task's schema is R51 spec. Since `spec` v0.6.0 the format is of version 0.2 (the 2026-11-01 amendment to R44 spec), and since T68 the element takes a manifest of this version only (the 2026-11-02 amendment to R32). The element takes the task and the manifest by `spec`'s types, and its own are only the narrowed types of the task for the check and the temporary field `canvas.tools` (R32, R41; Д5 in table 4 of [RUN.md](../RUN.md)). The types of the agreement with `app` are also `spec`'s, `@altnovatrail/spec/canvas`, since T69 (R74 spec; the 2026-11-02 amendment to R07). The task's open fields `givens` and `unknown` (R57 spec) the element takes since T55 and does not show: `app` shows them;
- how the package docks with the application and where it is published was decided by `canvas`'s answers to Т-21 and Т-26 — R36 and R37 spec (section 2.3; R16). The docking is the web component `<novatrail-canvas>` with the data types from `spec`'s releases (R07, section 3.3). The package is on npm, and the build of the samples page and the synthetic samples are files of a GitHub release (R08, section 3.4);
- adults' samples from the samples page go to the measurement's organizer or, for an eval set, through a pull request to `modules`, and children's samples without one, to where П-10 and П-12 decide (R25 spec; R11). The format of the samples is set by the eval protocol from `spec` (R15 spec; architecture 3.2 spec), and what was in the sample file before it is section 3.5 (R09). The volunteers' consent is open question П-16 (section 2.4), and the page's steps are section 3.6 (R11);

### 2.1. The drawing engine

- **Gist:** what the canvas draws the sheet and the layers with and takes the input of pen, finger and mouse with: a ready library — canvas-api planned Konva — or our own code on the browser's API. It is decided here: it is a point of the card (architecture 3.2 spec), and a component's technologies are decided by its repository (R11 spec).
- **What is known:**
  - what the drawing engine must be able to do the card and the vocabulary say: five layers, the tutor's sketch under the learner's writing, and the frames and marks above it; the task's tools; an eraser that removes a stroke whole; strokes with time and pressure; the picture of the work for the check; marks tied to the learner's strokes (architecture 3.2 spec; concepts 8 spec);
  - in the first slice there are two kinds — the handwriting sheet and the text canvas (product 10 spec). The text canvas is sentences and words: strike out, insert, reorder, highlight, listen (concepts 8 spec). Whether it is drawn by the same drawing engine as the sheet is not decided yet;
  - canvas-api planned Konva together with `react-konva`, the binding for React: in its spec 3.1, `konva` 10.7.x and `react-konva` 19.3.x for the layers of writing and hints. Its client `web/` still has neither — only React 19.3.0, so MathTrail has no actual experience with Konva (sources 1.1). Konva came there with the decision to keep the client in one repository with the API (R13 canvas-api), and there is no argument for Konva itself there; the argument about the repository was removed by R10 spec (repositories 1 spec);
  - standalone compared drawing libraries for the pictures of tasks in its widget — still drawings, not a handwriting sheet — and rejected Konva and the other libraries on `<canvas>`: such a drawing surface is one element, which a screen reader will not read, and a change of theme needs a redraw; `react-konva` moreover needs React (R269 standalone; sources 1.2). Accessibility is a point of `app`'s card (architecture 3.1 spec);
  - the prototype in `spec/draft/` draws the sheet straight on `<canvas>`, and its eraser erases pixels, not a whole stroke. Where the prototype differs from the documents, the documents are right (sources 1.3);
  - input in canvas-api is Pointer Events: `pointerType` tells the pen from the finger, `pressure` gives the pressure. The pressure changes only the line's width on screen and does not get into the picture for the check; a palm's touch is rejected (spec 3.1 canvas-api, sources 1.1);
  - how the package docks with the application is still open (Т-21; section 2.3). Т-21's recommendation is web components: the framework of each consumer is decided by its repository. So a drawing engine that holds on to the consumer's framework is checked against both answers to Т-21;
  - the layer of the module's widget is from stage 4, and the widget runs in an iframe sandbox (R16 spec; architecture 3.4 spec);
  - the dependencies are at exact versions and only with licenses compatible with Apache-2.0 (RULES.md, Hard rules; R12 spec);
  - the research «Рукопись ребёнка на канве и ИИ-наставник» is not a decision but an input of T01 and T02 (sources 1.4): for the check the canvas gives a crop — by A3 and H3; the group boxes of stroke groups — by A6 — go into the samples and into the tying of marks on the device; the canvas receives the marks of the tutor's layer and holds them to the strokes — by C2 (section 2.2);
  - tldraw is a whiteboard SDK under a license of its own. Without a trial or commercial key it requires "Not to use the Software in Production Environments". Other licenses tldraw gives separately — "Alternative licenses are available from tldraw for commercial and non-commercial use" —, and their terms were not read. And of the code itself the license says: "The Software may collect and transmit usage data to tldraw for license compliance purposes" (sources 2.1). The license of `LICENSE.md` is not ASF's category A, to which R12 spec limits what ships with the package, and the exception for development tools does not fit tldraw: it would ship inside `canvas`. The data collection also conflicts with `canvas` calling no one over the network (repositories 2.1 spec), and with the samples page going to no network while it runs (R25 spec). Its Agent starter kit is built on `tldraw`, goes to AI providers by itself and changes the shapes on the board (sources 2.2) — against R03, R05 and R15 spec. The research refers to tldraw's bindings by shape ID as a model, but its labels on strokes (C2) do not need tldraw;
  - the candidates the author named 2026-10-21 after the discussion are not a decision: Konva 10 for the scene and the layers, perfect-freehand 1.2 for the stroke's outline and rbush 4 for the index of strokes, all under MIT; input is Pointer Events (sources 2.3 and 3.1).

### 2.5. Input on the text canvas — as typed

- **Gist:** the text canvas gives to the check what the learner typed. But the browser and the device's keyboard change what was typed by themselves: they correct words, put capital letters, suggest continuations and mark errors. Then the check kind "text" or "edit" would compare with the key not the learner's answer but the device's correction — for example, a stress mark that autocorrect put where the module counts stress marks as significant (concepts 9.2, 16.2 spec). And a marked error is a hint even before the check, that "red pen" the platform does without (product 7 spec). The card has no such point: the author's analysis found it 2026-10-21 (sources 1.5) — «нормализация, автокоррекция и „угадывание“ нужного слова в канве запрещены».
- **What is known:**
  - HTML Standard (sources 3.5): with `autocorrect="off"` the browser may not correct words while typing, and without the attribute `on` applies; with `spellcheck="false"` spelling is not checked, and with the check on the browser marks errors — exactly how, the standard does not set; with `writingsuggestions="false"` the browser does not suggest continuations;
  - the attributes are a request, not a guarantee. A check the user has turned on always `spellcheck="false"` does not turn off, and `autocapitalize` the browser or the keyboard "may choose to ignore or override", and the standard does not tell one to rely on it (sources 3.5). How the keyboards of iPad and Android behave is seen only on devices — the same way T22 checks the pen;
  - turning off has a price — accessibility (concepts 15 spec): for a learner with motor impairments or with dyslexia, autocorrect and suggestions can help in typing. Accessibility is a point of `app`'s card (architecture 3.1 spec);
  - normalization is a parameter of the check kind, and the module sets it: case, spaces, punctuation, stress marks (concepts 9.2 spec). The canvas has nothing to normalize;
  - this subsection's recommendation is taken as an input by T29 in `spec`'s RUN.md — the check kinds and the normalization of the module format v0 — with the same argument: otherwise normalization would check not the learner's answer but the device's correction;
  - input on the text canvas is "a keyboard with stress marks" (section 3.1). Whether it is a panel of the canvas's own or the device's keyboard is decided by the epic of the text canvas.
- **Options:** turn everything off — `autocorrect="off"`, `autocapitalize="none"`, `spellcheck="false"`, `writingsuggestions="false"` — and check the keyboards on devices; leave it as set on the device; let the module decide for each task.
- **Recommendation:** turn everything off in the answer field and check on devices: no check needs autocorrect, so the module needs no setting either. What to replace the typing help with for whoever needs it is to be decided in the same epic, together with `app`'s accessibility. There too: what to do if the device's keyboard does not obey the attributes — for example, an on-screen keyboard of the canvas's own.
- **Task:** epic E3 «Текстовая канва», which T05 proposed. It was dropped 2026-10-25 (R21): `canvas` builds no text canvas, and the subsection is not decided. The subsection's finding concerns any typed answer — the answer slots with keyboard input too, which `app` shows (concepts 6 spec). The «Вход» of T29 in `spec`'s RUN.md refers to the subsection — in case the text canvas enters format v0. It is not in the first slice (R41 spec), and В32 is closed so (R30).

### 2.6. Scrolling the sheet with one pointer

- **Gist:** the drawing surface covers the visible part of the sheet, and it has `touch-action: none`: the browser does not take a touch that writes for scrolling (R18). Since T35 two fingers scroll the sheet on the drawing surface, by our own code (R29). WCAG 2.2 requires that what is done by a multipoint or path-based gesture be done by a single pointer without a path too (2.5.1, level A), and what is done by dragging, by a single pointer without it (2.5.7, AA). By Understanding 2.5.7 the criterion "does apply if content actively suppresses the user agent's own scrolling functionality and/or implements its own scrolling mechanism", and the keyboard alone is not enough. The card has no such point: the planning of T35 found it (sources 9.10).
- **What is known:**
  - on a screen no wider than 1000 CSS px — on a tablet — the drawing surface covers the sheet's whole width. On an element with a height the sheet then cannot be scrolled with one finger: the row of buttons scrolls the page, not the container, and the statement goes up together with the sheet;
  - on an element without a height the sheet scrolls together with the page, and a finger scrolls the page on the statement, on the row and outside the element by the browser's native scrolling. The criteria do not apply to it: 2.5.7 "does not apply to scrolling and dragging gestures enabled by the user agent";
  - the scrollbar on a tablet stands on top of the content, and a tablet usually has no wheel and no keyboard;
  - what WCAG level is required of UI packages `spec` has not decided — it is О-24. The shared accessibility standard of UI packages will be passed «в реестр spec» by T07 `app`, and the row of buttons and `canvas`'s table of labels are its input (the first 2026-10-26 amendment to R36 spec);
  - by `spec`'s addition to О-24, scrolling of our own by two fingers falls under 2.5.1 of level A too: Understanding 2.5.1 — "This success criterion applies to gestures in the author-provided content". Dragging with one finger would also do as the replacement at level A — 2.5.1 "does not apply to gestures that involve dragging in any direction" —, but on the sheet the finger writes, and in pen mode it does nothing (R29). So on an element with a height on a narrow tablet, without one of the options below, the sheet does not meet even level A.
- **Options:**
  - the buttons «Выше» and «Ниже» in the element's row, which scroll by the visible part with the same scrolling as two fingers (R29);
  - `app` gives the element no height on devices with touch, and the sheet scrolls together with the page;
  - an exception for the sheet in the accessibility standard.
- **Recommendation:** the buttons «Выше» and «Ниже», if the standard requires level AA — so `canvas` advised in В41, and so it is written in О-24. By the addition to О-24 they are needed at level A too, while `app` gives the element a height on devices with touch: two buttons and two labels, and the scrolling is already there. To be decided together with the standard T07 `app`.
- **Task:** open — О-24 in `spec` (R31), decided together with the accessibility standard T07 `app`. R29 refers to the subsection. Added 2026-10-26: by the author's decision in the planning of E6, the buttons «Выше» and «Ниже» are built by T45 in [RUN.md](../RUN.md), for the sheet and the workbook, without waiting for the standard.
- **Decided** (R33): the buttons «Выше» and «Ниже» in the element's row, on any device. A press scrolls what two fingers would scroll, by ⅘ of what the scrolled container shows, and at the edge the button fades and keeps the focus. On a computer the buttons let the keyboard scroll an element with a height in Safari too, where a scroll container without focusable content cannot be scrolled from the keyboard (sources 9.11). The accessibility level still waits for the standard T07 `app` (О-24), and `canvas`'s answer to О-24 is В44 in table 3 of [RUN.md](../RUN.md). How it is built: section 3.3. T45 — done. Added 2026-11-11: the keyboard scrolls the element's container itself too — a region with `tabindex="0"`, while its content overflows it, in Safari too (T93; the T93 amendment to R33; section 3.1). The buttons stay for a single pointer.

### 2.7. The workbook

- **Gist:** how the canvas shows a task of the kind `workbook` and what it gives from it to the check. The format sets only the item line — pieces of text and boxes in order, at least one box —, and leaves the layout to the canvas: "How the lines are laid out on the page is the canvas's" (the format's README, §14; R51 spec). Since 2026-10-22 the card gives `canvas` the detailing of the workbook: the page, the answer slots, the slot's crop and the template layer (architecture 3.2 spec; R41 spec). It is decided here. And what concerns the agreement `app` ↔ `api`, the eval protocol, the agreement of `canvas` and `app` and the card itself goes «в реестр spec» (RULES.md, Across repositories).
- **What is known:**
  - the format (the format's README, §11 and §14; `task.schema.json`; R51 spec):
    - the workbook is "a page of lines, one for each item, that the learner does not erase, with a box in a line for each answer". An item is a line, `line`: pieces of text and boxes `binding: box` in order, and a box anywhere — "7 × [ ] = 56", "56 [ ] 65", "[ ] apples". The boxes of a line are its answer slots, and a slot is named by its place among the item's boxes, from 1: items and slots have no IDs;

    - there is no limit: `items` has no `maxItems`, and a piece of text only `minLength: 1`, so a space is a piece too;

    - a piece of text is in the module's explanation language, like the statement, and the digit height is the manifest's `canvas.digit_height`. The format's README, §11, says of it — "on the crop of a handwriting sheet";
  - concepts spec:
    - each item has its own region on the canvas, its own key, its own skills and its own state — not started, started, checked —, and the task's result is made up of the items (concepts 6 spec);
    - the exit condition of a step is, among others, "a filled answer slot" (concepts 7 spec);

  - the agreement and the place:

### 3.1. The drawing engine and the layers

The drawing engine is our own code on the browser's API (R05). Of others' code it holds two packages: perfect-freehand builds a stroke's outline, and rbush the index of strokes. The options and their comparison are section 2.1, the sources are sources 2.1, 2.3, 2.4, 2.5, 2.6, 2.7 and 3.1.

**The canvas's two parts:**
- **DOM-free**: plain TypeScript, the same in the browser and in Node: the strokes and the writing history, the sheet's clock, the stack of actions for undo, clearing and erasing, the index of strokes and the eraser (T33, T10), the stroke groups and their rectangles on the sheet (T12; R24), the snapping of the tutor's marks to strokes and their places (T14; R26), the crop with the group boxes in its fractions — its numbers in integers, its own raster and its own PNG encoder (T13; R25) —, and also the check and restoring of an assigned writing history, the work for checking and the digest of its submission (T15; R27). The browser's drawing surface does not draw the crop: the HTML Standard does not fix the bytes of a PNG, in Node a drawing surface needs a native package with pixels of its own, and browsers add noise to what is read from a drawing surface. Brave adds it by default, Firefox on the desktop in standard protection too, and Safari in Private Browsing and possibly in all browsing (sources 2.7 and 3.1). The tests of this part run without a browser, and the same code draws T18's synthetic samples. The picture of the whole sheet, which the browser's drawing surface drew for `tutor`'s board (R20), is gone (R27);
- **on the page**: the stack of layers and input. Each layer is an element of the page of its own; all are of one size, lie one over another and count in the same units of the sheet (section 3.2).

**What draws each layer**, from the bottom up (concepts 8 spec):

| Layer | Handwriting sheet and workbook |
|---|---|
| the author's template | on the sheet, the grid, SVG: squares, lines or dots (T11; R23). On the workbook, the item lines, SVG: the numbers and the pieces of text `<text>`, fitted by `textLength` to the layout, and the boxes `<rect role="img">` (T51; R35, R36). The border of a filled box is 3 CSS px (T48; R37). The statement is not on the canvas but above it: HTML, text and pictures |
| the module's widget | its own place in the stack: empty until stage 4, from stage 4 an iframe sandbox (R16 spec) |
| the demo | SVG: the example's pictures in sheet areas, an `<image>` stretched over its area, and of those laid over one another, the one opened later on top (T61; R42). The demo's parts beside the sheet stand not on it but above it, as a list under the statement (T60) |
| the tutor's layer: the sketch "how to begin", under the learner's work | SVG; empty |
| the learner's work | Canvas 2D: a stroke is a perfect-freehand outline by its points with pressure; the eraser finds a stroke by the index and removes it whole |
| the tutor's layer: boxes and marks, over the learner's work | SVG: the box of each mark around its strokes (T14; R26). A mark has `role="img"` and `aria-label`, and the layer `role="none"` |

The tutor's layer is one layer in two elements: the sketch lies under the learner's work, the boxes and marks over it. Neither intercepts the pen's input (`pointer-events: none`) or covers the learner's work (R05 spec). The statement, the tools and the marks are HTML and SVG elements with accessible names, and a screen reader can read them, while the content of the drawing surface is not visible to it (sources 3.1). So the drawing surface holds only ink (concepts 15 spec; R269 standalone). T11 checked the statement by roles and names in Chromium: the paragraphs are text, the pictures come with their descriptions, and a screen reader does not see the grid, the drawing surface or the tutor's layer (R23). T51 checked the workbook's lines, in the DOM and in Chromium's own tree: a list of the items in order, each with its number, its text and its boxes by name (R36). T14 checked the mark: a screen reader reads it by its name, and the layer of marks itself does not count as a picture (R26). T41 checked the buttons: the row is a `toolbar`, and a screen reader finds its buttons by roles and names in the element's language (R28). T60 checked the parts of the example's demo, in the DOM and in Chromium's own tree: the row, the statement and the list of opened parts come in order, and the screen reader finds a picture in a sheet area by its description in the item of the list (R42). The picture itself on the sheet it does not see: the demo layer is `aria-hidden` (T61).

**The scene** (R23) is the visible part of the sheet, `position: sticky` in its native scrolling. It holds all the layers, of one size and `position: absolute`, in the order of the table:
- the SVGs show the visible part by their `viewBox`, `vx vy W/k H/k`, where W × H is the visible part on the screen, and `vx` its left edge, while a zoomed sheet is scrolled sideways (T94; R73). One layout sets it, in the same frame in which the ink is drawn, on scrolling and on a new size. So the layers do not drift apart: a layer that scrolled by itself would run ahead of the ink while scrolling (sources 9.4);
- the HTML layer, the widget's place, is empty for now and does not move. HTML on the sheet, when it comes, is a block of 1000 × 1400 units with the matrix (k, 0, 0, k, 0, −vy·k) in the same layout. The demo layer is SVG: it moves the pictures in areas by its `viewBox`, like the other SVGs, not by a matrix (T61; R42);
- only the drawing surface takes input. The other layers have `pointer-events: none` and `aria-hidden`, and the layer of marks and the workbook's template have `role="none"` instead of `aria-hidden`: the marks are read by their names (R26), and the workbook's lines as a list (R36). No `pointer-events` is set on the drawing surface and its ancestors, so the consumer's `none` on the element reaches the drawing surface (R20). There is one exception, the sheet of a worked example: there the drawing surface takes no input, `pointer-events: none`, by a rule on the sheet's class, and without the class the drawing surface again inherits the consumer's `none` (T59; R42). A layer that becomes active will drop its `none` but will not get `auto`. Above the scene, outside the scrolling, stands the element's row of buttons, and its buttons take the pointer just as the drawing surface does: the row has no `pointer-events` of its own (R28).

**The dependencies** are at exact versions, in the package's `dependencies` and in the lock file (R12 spec; R12, section 3.7):
- perfect-freehand 1.2.3: MIT, with no dependencies: a stroke's outline on the screen. It does not go into the crop: there the line is of one width, with no pressure (spec 3.1 canvas-api);
- rbush 4.0.1: MIT, with `quickselect` 3.0.0, ISC: the index of strokes for the eraser, the groups and the crop's bundles. The snapping of marks does not need it: a pass over the submission's strokes is enough for it (R26). The package is ESM only; its types are `@types/rbush` 4.0.0, MIT, for development only.

**Input** is Pointer Events: `pointerType` tells the pen, the finger and the mouse apart, `pressure` gives the pressure, and the intermediate points come from `getCoalescedEvents`, where it exists. T09 made input with one pointer (R18), and T35 input on tablets (R29):
- the drawing surface of the learner's work has `touch-action: none` and pointer capture: a stroke goes on beyond the edge of the drawing surface too, pressed to the edge of the sheet. Every pointer that came down on the drawing surface it holds by capture until lift-off, whether that pointer writes or not;
- one pointer writes at a time, with the primary button — the pen's touch, a finger, the left mouse button —, and with `tool = "eraser"` it erases. The pen's eraser erases with any `tool`, if the task allows the eraser (R22, R28). The pen's button and the right and middle mouse buttons neither write nor erase;
- which pointer writes is decided by the touch rule (R29). The pen removes the stroke of a touch in progress and writes itself, and while the pen or the mouse is writing, touches do nothing;
- a stroke ends on `pointerup`, on a move without the primary button, on a loss of capture and when the element leaves the page;
- `pointercancel` cancels the stroke: this is how the device reports a touch that it judged accidental;
- on the sheet of a worked example the learner does not write, and the drawing surface takes no input: `pointer-events: none` and `touch-action: auto` (T59; R42). A press lands in the scene, where a finger and the pen scroll the container or the page by native scrolling, and a pinch zooms, as over the statement (R23). Such a sheet neither turns the pen mode on nor turns it off, and an event that a script sends straight to the drawing surface the handler drops at once. Whatever the hands held when the example came — a palm, two fingers — goes;
- a point closer than 0.1 unit to the last one recorded is not written, while the place and time of lift-off always are. The time is from the sheet's first event, by the sheet's clock, shared by strokes and actions (R22), the pressure as it came, and for the last point, that of the previous move: on `pointerup` it is 0.

T35 made the palm rejection (R29), as in canvas-api (spec 3.1 canvas-api), with a way out of the pen mode:
- after the pen's first touch on any sheet of the page, the pen mode, one for the whole page until it reloads. In it a finger neither writes nor erases, and only presses buttons and scrolls with two fingers;
- a touch wider or taller than 100 CSS px is a palm, on `pointerdown` and on any coalesced `pointermove`. It does nothing until lift-off, and the stroke it was writing is removed. In canvas-api the threshold is 30 px, but on iPadOS the width of a touch is twice the radius, and a finger is wider there (sources 1.1 and 9.10);
- the button «Рисовать пальцем» in the element's row turns the mode off — for instance, when the pen's battery is flat —, and the pen's next touch turns it on again.

**The element's scroll container** is `.viewport`, which holds the statement, the demo's parts and the sheet (T93; the T93 amendment to R33; R90 spec):
- while its content overflows it — vertically, `scrollHeight − clientHeight > 0,5` px, or sideways while the sheet is zoomed (T94; R73) —, it is a region: `role="region"`, `tabindex="0"` and the name of the canvas kind, «Лист», «Тетрадь» or «Пример», in the language of the labels. The row is then named the kind's buttons, «Кнопки листа» and so on (the T93 amendment to R28);
- Tab enters it after the row and leaves it for what follows the element, and the arrows, Page Up and Page Down, Home, End and Space scroll it, as the browser scrolls a focused container. It has no `keydown` of its own. Without an overflow — in an element with no height or with a task that fitted — there is no role, no name and no Tab stop, and a focused container keeps them until `blur`;
- the focus ring — 2 px of the ink's color inside the edge, Highlight in forced colors — is on the sibling `.ring` in the wrapper `.frame`, over the sheet: the paper and the scene would cover the `outline` of the container itself.

On the drawing surface two fingers scroll the sheet, by our own code: `touch-action` acts on the pen too, and native scrolling cannot be given to the finger while writing is given to the pen (section 3.2). Without the pen mode a second finger that touched no later than 150 ms after the first, while the first had moved no more than 8 px, removes the first one's stroke, and both scroll, and a second finger later than that does nothing. In the pen mode two fingers that touched no more than 150 ms apart scroll. A third finger does nothing. With one pointer the sheet is scrolled by the buttons «Выше» and «Ниже» of the element's row — the same as two fingers would scroll (T45; R33; section 3.3). A zoomed sheet two fingers scroll sideways too, and one pointer does so with «Левее» and «Правее»; a gesture that at the threshold has gone twice as far along one axis as along the other goes on to its end along that axis only (T94; R73). In a low window the row does not stick, and in an element with no height they are at hand while the row is visible (T92; section 3.3).

The thresholds are parameters of T22: it checks them on devices. There are no tablets, and T22 checked in the emulators what they give (R65): `pointercancel` removes a stroke, two fingers scroll and do not write, the row's eraser and undo work — on Android with Chrome's touches, on the iPad with the page's events. The thresholds themselves, the palm, the pressure and the pen are for the hardware (Д7). Where the hardware does not measure a touch, `width` and `height` equal 1, and a palm is not rejected by its area — then the pen mode holds it (sources 3.1). The canvas will not fix WebKit bug 269535: on the iPad the pen is silent while a finger or the edge of a palm touches the screen.

**The ink** (R18) is a perfect-freehand 1.2.3 outline, with every parameter set explicitly: a line of 3 units of the sheet, as on the board, with round ends. Only the pen's pressure changes the width — from half the line to one and a half —, and for the mouse and the finger the line is even. There is one drawing surface, over the visible part of the sheet and in device pixels; the path of a finished stroke is built once, and anew on each frame only for the stroke being written. Strokes outside the visible part are not drawn, and a stroke whose points are far beyond it is not even traced until it comes into it: an assigned history may hold thousands of strokes (R27). On a new size of the drawing surface the ink comes back in the same frame. There is no context menu and no selection over the sheet: the scene forbids them, on the example's sheet too (R42). The pressure does not get into the crop for checking: its line is of one width (spec 3.1 canvas-api; R25).

**Undo and clearing** (R19) are the work on the sheet, in the DOM-free part: the strokes that are visible, in the order in which they were laid, and the stack of actions. An action is a written stroke, an erasing or a clearing (R22). Undo removes the last one: for an erasing it returns the erased strokes to their former places, and for a clearing its strokes; there is no redo. Undo and clearing do not touch the stroke being written: it will lie on the sheet when it ends. The element's commands are section 3.3.

**The eraser** (R22) erases a stroke whole, along the path of a press:
- a press takes every stroke whose line comes no farther from the path than the reach: 10 CSS px on the screen, whatever the zoom at the moment of the press, plus 2.25 units, half of the pen's widest line. A tap is a path of one point;
- outside the visible part of the sheet — where a captured pointer goes — the eraser takes nothing, and the path goes on from its last place on the screen;
- the rbush index finds strokes by the bounding boxes of their raw points, and our own code computes the exact distance from a segment of the path to the segments of a stroke. The index is built anew when the sheet's array of strokes has changed, and only when it is queried;
- the strokes under the eraser vanish from the screen at once, and leave the sheet when it is lifted, as one action and one event `historychange` (R27). `pointercancel` returns them, and a press with no hits is not an action;
- on the lift, what goes of the strokes taken is what is on the sheet then: what undo or a clearing removed during the press and did not return is no longer there to erase.

**The writing history** (R22) is events in the order in which the actions were laid: "stroke", the time it was laid and the stroke itself with its points; "erasing", the time of the lift and the IDs of the erased strokes; "clearing", the time and the IDs of everything that was on the sheet; "undo", the time, the IDs it removed and the IDs it returned. The time at which an action was laid does not decrease along the history, and folding the history gives the sheet without the rules of the stack. The sheet's clock is milliseconds from the first call to it. The recording of input takes the points' time from it, and the work on the sheet the actions' time, and only when an action changed something. The page's time — the `timeStamp` of events and `performance.now()` for the methods — is counted from one origin (sources 9.3). The history goes out by the property `history` and the event `historychange`, and comes in by the same property: another history replaces the sheet, and the clock runs from its last event + 2001 ms, more than the groups' pause (T15; R27; section 3.3).

**The widget layer from stage 4.** The iframe sandbox takes its place in the stack. The chosen tool distributes input: the widget's tool removes `pointer-events` from the layers above the widget, and touches reach the iframe, while the pen and the eraser take input on the layer of the learner's work. The iframe has its own document: the widget receives the input in it, and the page's `touch-action` is not inherited in the iframe — the widget sets its own (sources 3.1). The widget tells of its state only by the protocol from `spec` (R16 spec; Т-24). What the protocol has to decide beyond R16 spec is named by architecture 3.4 spec, and decided by epic E5 in RUN.md of `spec`, by stage 4. The protocol's side in the app is `canvas`'s widget host (architecture 3.2 spec; section 1), and for it this is the input:
- **the channel** is a `MessageChannel` port: the host passes it to the widget once, on the iframe's first load, and from then on listens only to it. The widget's origin without `allow-same-origin` is opaque, and the iframe's window is one for all its documents, so neither by origin nor by `event.source` can a document that the widget later navigated to be told apart. How the host notices such a navigation the protocol decides;
- **the state's version** in each message of the widget. A mark on a part of the widget that has changed since then the host does not draw — just as a mark of the sheet whose strokes are erased (section 3.2);

The parts' boxes are converted to the screen by the same conversion as all the layers (section 3.2); in what units the widget reports them the protocol decides;

The host gives no verdict, nor does the canvas at all (section 1);
- **the conformance tests** are in `spec`, beside the protocol, and the test host in the development environment `widget-template` (architecture 3.13 spec). Both sides pass them — the widget and `canvas`'s widget host, each in its own CI —, and "`app`" in architecture 3.4 spec is this host. This is how `spec` answered В22 (R16).

Whether a version of the widget is withdrawn (R63 spec) the host does not learn by itself: it knows neither `api` nor the index, and over the network it calls no one (section 1). This is settled before it, at `app`, which gives the canvas the task.

### 3.2. The sheet's logical coordinates

The handwriting sheet counts in logical units, as canvas-api does: they do not depend on the screen (R06; R15 canvas-api). The options are section 2.2, the sources are sources 1.1, 1.4 and 5.2.

**The sheet** is W × H = 1000 × 1400 units:
- the origin is the top left corner, x grows to the right, y downward, one scale on both axes;
- the size is a constant of the canvas kind, not a setting of the module. The sheet is one, with no pages.

In these units:
- the points of strokes, as fractional numbers, as they came: only the recording of a sample rounds them, to 0.1 unit (section 3.5; R18). A point outside the sheet input presses to the edge, as in spec 4.2 canvas-api;
- the grid: its kind — squares, lines or dots — and its step are set by the task, and the default step is 50 units, like canvas-api's square;
- the zone of an answer slot, a rectangle in the sheet's units: `left` and `top` from the left and top edges, `width` and `height` (R51 spec);
- all the layers of the stack (section 3.1).

**Who counts in what:**

| What | In what units | Who converts |
|---|---|---|
| strokes, the grid, a slot's zone, the area of a demo picture, the layers | the sheet's units | — |
| the workbook's lines and boxes | the page's units, 1000 × 1400, as for the sheet, integers (R35) | — |
| the sheet on the screen | CSS px: `k` per unit | `canvas` |
| the statement above the sheet | CSS px, without `k` | — |
| the crop | the crop's px: `s` per unit; its place on the sheet is `sheet_box`, in the sheet's units (R25) | `canvas` |
| a group's rectangle | the sheet's units, `[ymin, xmin, ymax, xmax]` | — |
| the group boxes and the place in the observation | fractions 0–1000 of the crop, `[ymin, xmin, ymax, xmax]`, integers | into fractions, `canvas` and `tutor`'s adapter; from fractions into the sheet, `canvas` |
| a mark of the sheet | `{stroke_ids, snapped_box}`, the box in the sheet's units (R26) | `canvas` |

**The screen** has one conversion for all the layers, as in spec 4.1 canvas-api. The sheet is fit to the width of its area — A CSS px —, but no wider than 1000 CSS px: on a wide screen it stands in the center, with margins, as in canvas-api (R23). There is no lower limit: there are no phones in the first slice (product 10 spec). The page's zoom z enlarges the fitted sheet — from 100 to 200 %, in steps of a quarter, by the row's buttons «Крупнее» and «Мельче» (T94; R73). What does not fit in height, and for a zoomed sheet in width too, scrolls; `vx` and `vy` are the left edge and the top of the visible part of the sheet, in units:

```
k     = min(A / W, 1) × z
x_css = (x − vx) × k             y_css = (y − vy) × k
```

- the limit is the layout's: `fitScale` in `screen.ts`, with the page's zoom. The `k` for input, for the eraser's reach and for drawing is by the width of the sheet on the screen, `scaleFor`, without the limit: in an ancestor with a `transform` the sheet on the screen can be wider than 1000 px too;
- the drawing surface of the learner's work is only the visible part, on both axes, and draws in device pixels: the scale is `k × devicePixelRatio`, the offset `−vx × k × devicePixelRatio` and `−vy × k × devicePixelRatio`. Its buffer does not grow with the zoom, in an element with a height; in an element with no height the drawing surface is still as tall as the whole sheet (R73);
- for the SVG of the template — the grid or the workbook's lines —, of the demo layer and of the tutor's layer, `viewBox` sets the visible part of the sheet, `vx vy W/k H/k`, and the browser converts by itself. A workbook item that `scrollIntoView` or a screen reader scrolls to comes into the visible part two frames later: the container scrolls at once, and the next layout sets the new `viewBox` (R36);
- a workbook box holds a stroke if the middle of the bounding box of its points is in the box widened by 12 units, and that bounding box itself is no bigger than 160 × 160 (R37). The rule counts in the page's units, in whole tenths, for each stroke separately, and does not know the screen;
- HTML on the sheet, when it comes, takes the same `k`, as a block of 1000 × 1400 units with the matrix (k, 0, 0, k, 0, −vy·k). The statement stands above the sheet and does not take `k`;
- input is converted back: `x = (clientX − r.left) / k + vx`, `y = (clientY − r.top) / k + vy`, where `r` is the rectangle of the visible part of the sheet, `getBoundingClientRect()`.

The offsets `vx` and `vy` are the same for all the layers too. T09 decided the way of scrolling (R18; the 2026-10-22 amendment to R06): the sheet scrolls by the native scrolling of a container in the element's shadow root — by the wheel, the scroll bar and the keyboard, as everywhere on the page:
- the scene with all the layers stands in the visible part of the container (`position: sticky`) — section 3.1 —, and `vy` is the drawing surface's offset from the top of the sheet, divided by `k`;
- above the sheet, in the same scrolling, is the task's statement (R23), and above the statement, outside the scrolling, the element's row of buttons (R28). By default the element is as tall as the row, the statement and the sheet, and the sheet is seen whole. If the consumer gave the element a smaller height, the statement and the sheet scroll in it, under the row;
- while the statement is visible, the drawing surface goes below the visible part of the container, and the visible part of the sheet is the drawing surface clipped by it. The eraser takes strokes in it (R22). A row that stuck above the sheet when the page scrolls the element clips the visible part from the top: under the row the eraser takes nothing (R28);
- on a new width and on a new height of the statement `vy` stays as it was, and on a new height of the container itself the element does not touch the scrolling: a scrolling in progress is not cut off. A new zoom keeps the top left corner of the visible part, `vx` and `vy`, at its place on the sheet (R73);
- while the sheet is zoomed, the container scrolls sideways too, left to right whatever the page's direction, and the scene sticks to its left edge too. The statement and the example's parts are of the fitted width, `min(A, 1000)`, at the left edge of the container: they wrap as at 100 % and do not move sideways (T94; R73);
- with one finger the sheet on the drawing surface does not scroll, nor does the page over it: the drawing surface covers the visible part, and it has `touch-action: none`. One finger scrolls them on the statement above the sheet and on the sheet's margins on a wide screen, and on the row of buttons the page, by native scrolling;
- on the drawing surface two fingers scroll the sheet, by our own code (T35; R29). The sheet moves with the midpoint of the fingers, after the first 8 px and without a jump. What to scroll the gesture's first step chooses, and the choice does not change until the gesture's end: the container, if it can move in that direction, otherwise the element's nearest scrollable ancestor — across shadow boundaries —, otherwise the page, if its overflow is not hidden. The movement is divided by the container's scale, and there is no inertia;
- with one pointer the same is scrolled by the row's buttons «Выше» and «Ниже» (T45; R33): a press chooses what to scroll as the gesture's first step does, and moves it by ⅘ of what it shows on the screen — under the row, if the row stuck above it.

T11 put the other layers into the same scene (R23): they do not scroll with the content but move by themselves, in the ink's frame, the SVGs by their `viewBox`. A layer that scrolled by itself would run ahead of the ink while scrolling (sources 9.4). T35 made the scrolling with two fingers (section 3.1; R29).

**The crop** is drawn by A3, from the visible strokes only: without the grid, the statement and the tutor's layer (spec 4.3 canvas-api; H3). Its rule is R25. In the formulas:
- D is the digit height in px: a canvas setting of the module in its manifest, `canvas.digit_height`, an integer from 1 to 256, by default 24 since format 0.2, and 32 before it (the 2026-10-28 amendment to R51 spec). Without a manifest it is 24 too (the 2026-11-02 amendment to R32). How the app gets it with the task is decided by T34 in RUN.md of `spec`, and the element gets it from the module manifest, the property `manifest` (section 3.3; R32);
- B is the common rectangle of the visible points. All places are in tenths of a unit, `round(10 × v)`, as for the groups (R24);
- `h_med` is the median height of the bundles:
  - a bundle is strokes no shorter than 15 units whose bounding boxes touch or overlap, by a chain and without time;
  - a shorter stroke is a dash: a dot, a minus, a fraction bar, an underline. It does not count and binds nothing;
  - the median is the lower one, the ⌊(n − 1) / 2⌋-th from the lowest; with no bundles, 15 units;
  - the scale takes it no higher than 100 units: H = min(`h_med`, 100);
- `m` is the margin, 12 px;
- D′ is the height at which the digits are drawn: the largest integer no greater than D at which the crop fits within the limit.

```
s         = D′ / H                              crop px per unit of the sheet
w₀        = ⌈(B.xmax − B.xmin) × s⌉ + 2m        h₀ likewise
w         = max(w₀, 200)                        the crop's width, px; h likewise
mx        = m + ⌊(w − w₀) / 2⌋                  the left margin, px; my the top one, likewise
limit:      max(w, h) ≤ 1568 and ⌈w / 28⌉ × ⌈h / 28⌉ ≤ 1568
sheet_box = [B.ymin − my/s, B.xmin − mx/s, B.ymin − my/s + h/s, B.xmin − mx/s + w/s]
```

`sheet_box` (`sb`) is where the crop, with its margins, lies on the sheet. The coordinates are converted by it:

```
sheet → crop:     X   = (x − sb.xmin) × s                Y = (y − sb.ymin) × s
group box:        fx₀ = ⌊1000 × (X₀ − r) / w⌋            fx₁ = ⌈1000 × (X₁ + r) / w⌉, y likewise
fraction → sheet: x   = sb.xmin + fx / 1000 × (sb.xmax − sb.xmin)
                  y   = sb.ymin + fy / 1000 × (sb.ymax − sb.ymin)
```

Here X₀ and X₁ are the edges of the group's points on the crop, and r is half the line and the pixel of its edge.
- **The line** on the crop is of one width, with no pressure (spec 3.1 canvas-api): max(D′, 12) / 12 px: at 24 px, 2, at 32 px, 2.67.
- **A group box** is its ink together with the edge pixel that the raster still paints, rounded outward. Every pixel of the group's ink is inside the box, and min < max for a dot too (R53 spec).
- **The limit** is Claude's standard level: any model of Claude and of OpenAI at `high` takes such a picture without downscaling (sources 9.6).
  - A crop that does not fit is drawn with digits smaller than D, by whole px, and D′ says so.
  - A side shorter than 200 px gets a wider margin, and the scale stays the same.
- **An empty sheet** gives no crop: it is cut off before the AI call (H3).

The numbers are computed in integers. The tenths of the points, H, D′, the margins and the sizes are integers below 2^53, and `s`, `sheet_box` and the line are each one division of two integers. So `tutor`'s runner will repeat them to the bit, as with the rule of groups (R24). The formulas in integers are in the header of the test vectors (section 3.5).

**The crop of a workbook box** is the same rule over the strokes of one filled box, as one group of them, at the manifest's digit height (R34, R38). The draft goes into no crop. At the eval heights a box's crop is at the lower limit, 200 × 200 px, that is 64 tokens, and a page of 10 items with one box each is 640 tokens and about 11 KB of PNG. An empty box gives no crop.

**The raster and the PNG** are our own DOM-free code (section 3.1; R05, R25):
- the pixels are gray: white is 255, ink 0;
- a segment of a stroke is a line of one width with round ends, a stroke of one point is a dot. A pixel takes the share that the line covers, by a slope of one pixel at the edge;
- only +, −, ×, ÷ and `Math.sqrt` go into a pixel, and the bytes are the same in any JavaScript engine;
- the PNG is gray, 8 bits, one IDAT with a zlib stream of one fixed-Huffman block, with no ancillary chunks. A crop of the whole sheet weighs about 10 KB.

**The groups and their boxes** (R24). The groups are built by space and time, as in `tutor` (R08 tutor):
- two strokes are neighbors if there are no more than 2 s between them, and the bounding boxes of their points come closer than 50 units horizontally and vertically, closer than a square of the default grid. The pause is from the end of the earlier stroke to the start of the later one, and for strokes that overlap in time it is 0;
- a group is strokes joined by a chain of neighbors. What is added later beside old writing is a group of its own, and lines written one after another do not merge: under a stroke of a line lies a stroke written a line later;
- the rule takes the visible strokes of the final sheet with the time of their points. It counts in integers — in tenths of a unit, `round(10 × v)`, and in milliseconds, `round(t)`, rounding a half up — and only by subtraction, so `tutor`'s runner will repeat it to the bit;
- the group labels are A, B, C…, after Z AA, AB…, by place: by the top of the group's rectangle, then by the left edge;
- a group's rectangle is the common rectangle of the points of its strokes in the sheet's units, `[ymin, xmin, ymax, xmax]`. A group box is its ink in fractions of the crop, its own for each crop (R25).

The table "label → stroke IDs" stays on the device and is valid only for its own submission (C2). The split by time is a rough sign of the process: on the research's scale A4 the group boxes are then closer to (b) than to (a) (Т-31). The crop's scale does not depend on the groups: `h_med` is the median of the bundles, and they are built without time (R25). So the same final strokes give one crop, however they were written, and only the boxes and the labels depend on the order of writing.

**The place from the model:**
- `tutor`'s adapter converts its provider's convention into fractions of the crop that the device sent. The provider's downscaling of the picture and the margins with labels, if the server draws them (Т-31), it takes into account itself;
- `canvas` converts the fractions into the sheet's units by the `sheet_box` of its submission and snaps the box to the strokes of that submission whose points are mostly inside (C2; R43 canvas-api). The model did not see the strokes added after the submission, and they do not go into the mark. The rule is R26:
  - the box is widened by a quarter of the digit height: D′/4 px on the crop, H/4 on the sheet, at D = 24, 6 px, and at 32, 8 px;
  - a stroke is taken if no fewer than two thirds of its points are in the box, and at least one;
  - it is computed in integers, on the crop itself, as the raster of R25 is. A point in tenths stands on it at X = 1000 × ((px − B.xmin) × D′ + mx × H), and the box holds it if (xmin × w − μ × D′) × H ≤ X ≤ (xmax × w + μ × D′) × H, the same for y, with μ = 250 thousandths of D′. The edges are inclusive;
  - the box is first widened, then clipped by the sheet's bounds, and the clipping changes nothing: the points lie on the sheet;
  - the margin and the threshold are parameters of eval, and the search by which they were chosen is in R26;
- no strokes inside, no box, and the question is asked without it (R04 spec; R43 canvas-api);
- `tutor` does not snap the box to strokes: the board draws it as it is (the T45 amendment to R19 tutor), and the measurement counts it by the line of the first error: "the box with a margin of 8 px holds the most visible points of exactly this line" (R24 tutor; sources 9.7).

**The marks:**
- on the sheet a mark is `{stroke_ids, snapped_box}`. It is drawn by the current geometry of its strokes; new strokes do not move it, and when all its strokes are erased, it vanishes too. Only a sketch on an empty place needs `snapped_box` (C2);
- from `api` a mark comes with a group label, with an answer slot or with a box in fractions of the crop, not in the sheet's units (R53 spec);
- where a mark is `canvas` decides when the marks have come (R26). All the strokes that the sheet knew go into the reckoning, the erased ones too, but only from the submission's table:
  - a group label: the strokes of its row of the table;
  - a box of the model: the snapping above;
  - an answer slot: by place, `{ item, slot }`, from 1. On the sheet it has no strokes: the sheet takes no zones (R32). On the workbook, the strokes of the table's row of its box; an empty box, which is not in the table, the mark circles itself, without strokes, and a box not of the page gives no mark's box (R39 and the 2026-11-08 amendment to it). A group label and a box of the model on the workbook are a `TypeError`.

  What is visible is checked at drawing. So a mark that came after a clearing comes back with an undo, together with its strokes;
- the mark's box is around the points of those of its strokes that are visible now and not under an eraser's press, 10 units from them and within the sheet. On the workbook, around the box and these points together (R39). The line is 2 CSS px at any `k`. While new points of the stroke being written come into the box, it fades to a quarter, and when the stroke is laid it comes back (R26).

**What does not leave the device** is `sheet_box`, the table of groups, the strokes and their time (R09 spec). From the workbook only the crops of the filled boxes leave, with their places, item and box, and the table "box → stroke IDs" and the digest stay with `app` (R38). How `canvas` gives them to the consumer together with the crop and takes them back with the marks is section 3.3. This is the rule of checking, and a sample for eval has its own path and its own composition, section 3.5.

**The sketch "how to begin" on an empty place** is not addressed by the crop: the crop covers only the work, and an empty sheet has none. Where it stands is decided together with it, with the first slice of `app` and `api` (the archive of E2, [docs/plan/E2.md](plan/E2.md), «Не входит»).

### 3.3. Docking with the app

The package enters its consumer as a web component, the element `<novatrail-canvas>`, with no framework (R07; Т-21). The options are section 2.3, the sources are sources 3.3 and 5.1.

**The element:**
- one for all the canvas kinds: it takes the kind from the task's open view. The module chooses the kind, and a new kind is the platform's code (R02 spec), so a new kind comes with a new version of `canvas`, with no change to `app`;
- the markup is in an open Shadow DOM: the page's styles do not touch the canvas's layers, nor the canvas's styles the page. The element sets its own styles by `adoptedStyleSheets` — one `CSSStyleSheet` per document, filled by `replaceSync()` —, not by a `<style>` element in the tree, and writes no `style` attribute in the markup: it works under a content security policy with no inline styles, as `app`'s is (R51; the second 2026-11-02 amendment to R36 spec). An element moved into another document takes that document's sheet, `adoptedCallback()`;
- the element is registered on the import of its entry point, and only once. The element's name on a page is one, and `app` builds in one exact version of the package (R16 spec);
- the package has two entry points. The DOM-free part is imported in Node too, for the tests and T18's synthetic samples (section 3.1), and registers no element. The element is registered by a separate entry point. These are `@altnovatrail/canvas`, the element, and `@altnovatrail/canvas/core`, the DOM-free part (R12; section 3.7).

**It takes** by properties, not by attributes. The properties are declared in the element's class: so React 19 and Preact pass their values to the element as properties (sources 3.3).

  - `cells` — the crop of every filled box, `{ item, slot, png }`, in the task's order, by the box's strokes (section 3.2);
  - `submission` — the context `{ boxes, digest }`: the table "box → stroke IDs" and the digest of its strokes, of all the page's boxes and of the table's rows;
  - if no box is filled — `null`, even with a draft;
  - the kind of the work is named by its key — `png` or `cells` —, and the boxes in `cells` are exactly those filled by `filled`;
- the property `history`, read-only — the whole writing history, frozen, the same array until the next action. The measurement sample and the synthetic samples take it (section 3.5), and so does `app`'s export (R09 spec);
- the property `filled`, read-only — which answer slots are filled: by item, by slot, in the task's order. A slot is filled by strokes among those `exportWork()` would take: not by the stroke in progress, but by the strokes under the eraser before it is lifted:
  - on a workbook, since T48 (R37), — a box with at least one such stroke in it;
  - on a sheet, since T57 (R42), — the only slot, `anywhere` or `sheet`: `[[true]]` if the sheet has such a stroke, otherwise `[[false]]`. A sheet with only `choice` and a sheet with no task have `null`: `app` marks the options. A worked example has `null` too (R42).

  The value is frozen and stays the same array until a slot changes, and on a sheet both values are the same for all elements. An action changes it before `historychange`, and assigning `task` and `history` changes it with no event. By it `app` learns the exit condition of the step `answer_slots_filled`: for `choice`, its own choice of an option; for the other slots, `filled` (the format's README, §18; concepts 7 spec);

There is no event `speak`. It was the request to voice the "listen" of the text canvas, and `canvas` does not build that canvas (R21). R36 spec and architecture 3.1 spec leave this event until the text canvas, "later": the first slice has none (R41 spec).

**Commands** — methods the consumer calls (R19):
- `undo()` takes back the last action on the sheet — a written stroke, an erasing or a clearing — and gives `true` if the sheet changed (R22);
- `clear()` removes all the strokes in one action, which undo takes back, and on an empty sheet gives `false`.

The element's own buttons, «Отменить» and «Очистить», call them (R28), and the consumer may call them itself.

**The element's buttons** (R28, R29, R33) — a row above the statement and the sheet:
- as wide as the sheet's column and outside the element's scrolling. In an element with a height, the statement and the sheet scroll under it; without a height, it keeps to the top of the ancestor's scrolling while the element is on screen, below the top by `--novatrail-canvas-top`;
- in a low window it does not stick (T92; R28; WCAG 1.4.10, 2.4.11): the row sticks while it is no taller than a quarter of the scrolling in which it sticks — of the nearest ancestor with overflow `auto`, `scroll`, `overlay` or `hidden`, across shadow boundaries, or of the window — below `--novatrail-canvas-top`. Otherwise it stands in its place, above the statement, and goes away with the scrolling, as W3C's technique C34 takes the stickiness off (sources 9.19). In a window of 320 × 256 CSS px the row wraps onto two to four lines and stands in place, and in a wide low window a row of one line, 60 px, still sticks. The element computes the threshold anew when the size of the row, of the window or of that ancestor changes and when the page or the ancestor scrolls: a new `--novatrail-canvas-top` changes no size, and the threshold hears it with the next scroll;
- a button in focus (`:focus-visible`) shows a ring in the ink's color, 2 px, 2 px outside the button (2.4.7, 1.4.11). The row's side margins are 4 px, so the ring of an end button stands on the row's paper even at the edge of the window. A disabled button is paler in color — 40 % ink on paper — not in opacity: opacity would pale the ring too;
- a panel `toolbar` named by the canvas kind's buttons, «Кнопки листа» or «Кнопки тетради» (R36; the T93 amendment to R28), and the kind's name is on the scroll container (section 3.1): «Перо» and «Ластик» are a group of radio buttons «Инструмент», and «Отменить» and «Очистить» are buttons;
- the keyboard:
  - one Tab stop;
  - the arrows move the focus between the buttons without selecting, and Home and End to the first and the last. The arrow along the row leads to the next button: in right to left, ArrowLeft (R46);
  - Space and Enter press;
- «Отменить», while there is nothing to undo, and «Очистить» on an empty sheet are `aria-disabled`: they grow pale and keep the focus;
- a button is no smaller than 44 × 44 CSS px;
- every button has, before its label, an icon of the set Material Symbols by the table of architecture 6 spec: an SVG without a name, in the color of the button's text, and the button's name is still its label. The selected tool is filled, «Показ» is an arrow down or along the row, and «Отменить», «Дальше» and a hidden «Показ» are mirrored in right to left. The script `scripts/icons.ts` writes the icons from the set's package, and `ci-lint` checks its output (T73; R46; R78 spec);
- the tools are those the task allows, and for a task without the eraser «Перо» stays alone;
- «Выше» and «Ниже» — a pair after «Очистить», on any device (T45; R33):
  - a press scrolls what two fingers would scroll, by ⅘ of what it shows on screen;
  - while any sheet of the page is being written on, a press does nothing;
  - at the edge the button is `aria-disabled`, like «Отменить»;
- «Крупнее» and «Мельче», "Zoom in" and "Zoom out", — a pair after «Выше» and «Ниже» (T94; R73): a step of the page zoom, from fit to the width up to 200 %, on every sheet of the page, but not while any of them is being written on; at the limit the button is `aria-disabled` and keeps the focus. While the sheet is enlarged, «Левее» and «Правее», "Left" and "Right", follow them: sideways, as «Выше» and «Ниже» go down. This pair comes after the pair of the zoom: the last «Мельче» hides it and does not move the buttons under the finger. The four words and their icons are in the tables of architecture 6 spec: `spec` recorded В76 by recommendation (a) (Д219 spec; R77);
- for a worked example the row is called «Кнопки примера», "Worked example buttons", and holds only «Дальше», "Next", «Показ», "Demo", «Выше», «Ниже» and the pair of the zoom, and with an enlarged sheet «Левее» and «Правее» too (T59, T60; R42; R73). «Дальше» is `aria-disabled` while the demo is hidden or all its parts are open, and refers by `aria-controls` to the region `status`. «Показ» is a disclosure button: `aria-expanded`, `aria-controls` on the list, and a triangle — ▾ while the demo is visible and ▸ when it is hidden — as an SVG without a name, and the label does not change. The row hides the other buttons with the attribute `hidden`, and the arrows skip them. A button that has hidden gives the Tab stop and the focus to the first visible one, not to the pen's button, and while no button has been in focus, the stop is on the first visible one, whichever it becomes;
- in pen mode on a device with touch — the last button, «Рисовать пальцем». It gives drawing back to the finger on the whole page, appears and goes away between the strokes of the whole page, and when it disappears it gives the focus to the pen's button (T35; R29 and its amendments): a row that wraps would move the sheets under it too.

The element sends its events from itself — from the host, not from inside the shadow tree — and they bubble (`bubbles: true`). **The submission context.** The canvas has no data of its own (architecture 3.2 spec), so it does not keep the submission context but gives it along with the crop:
- `app` keeps the context and returns it together with the marks of this submission. It keeps it while marks may come; whether to store it on the device longer is for `app` to decide (architecture 3.1 spec, "what is stored on the device and how much"). The context is derived from the learner's work — like the strokes, it is data on the device (privacy 4 spec);
- `sheet_box` and the table "label → stroke IDs" do not go to the server (R06), and the group boxes will go only if Т-31 so decides;
- by the context the canvas finds the strokes of the group labels and converts the boxes into the sheet's units, and it does not show on the sheet the strokes erased since (section 3.2);
- the context holds the crop's numbers, as `cropOf` gives them, with `sheet_box` and the group boxes, and the groups with the table "label → stroke IDs" (R26). The snapping rule needs the crop's integers, and `sheet_box` and the group boxes are checked against them;
- the context carries a digest: CRC-32 and Adler-32 of a text of the table's strokes — points in tenths of a unit and milliseconds — and of the context's integers (R27). It catches an accidental mismatch, not a forgery. The context returns to a sheet whose history holds its strokes — the same sheet, or one opened anew with the same history. Every sheet numbers its strokes from 1, and another sheet's context with the same IDs, or a changed one, is a `TypeError`;
- on a workbook the context is `{ boxes, digest }`: the table "box → stroke IDs" and the digest of its strokes, of all the page's boxes and of the table's rows (R38). It holds no crop numbers and no groups. It returns to a workbook whose history holds its strokes, and only to a page of the same layout: a context of another layout is a `TypeError` (R39). The table does not go to the server, like the table of labels;
- `app` stores the history and the context as they are, through JSON: a store that rounds numbers changes the digest, and the sheet's own context becomes another's.

**Theme** — by CSS variables: they inherit from the parent (sources 3.3), and the element reads them inside its tree (R23):
- `--novatrail-canvas-paper` — the sheet, `--novatrail-canvas-ink` — the ink on screen, `--novatrail-canvas-grid` — the grid; the defaults are light: `#fff`, `#111` and a light gray grid;

  - the selected tool is filled with ink, and its icon has a fill in the paper's color (R46);
  - the focus ring is in the ink's color: its contrast to the paper is the same as that of the buttons' labels, and it has no variable of its own (T92). The same ring, inside the edge, marks the scroll container in focus too (T93; section 3.1). A disabled button is a `color-mix` of 40 % ink and paper;
  - `--novatrail-canvas-top` — the sticky row's offset from the top, 0 by default: an `app` with a sticky header of its own sets it to the header's height;

**What `app` does:**
- gives the element `task`, `manifest` — the open view of the manifest of the task's module (R32) —, `media`, `history` and `marks` — `task` and `history` before `marks` —, and the language by the attribute `lang` on the element itself: the element does not read an ancestor's language (R27, R28, R39);
- gives a lesson's worked example by the property `workedExample`, and a new element to every segment of the lesson, in React by a new `key` (R20, R42). React 19 takes away a property that disappeared by assigning `undefined`, but assigns the changed ones in the JSX order, so changing the task and the example on one element would give a `TypeError` with explicit `undefined` in one of the two directions. It imports the entry point before the first render: before the element is registered, React writes an attribute instead of a property. It does not write `null` into the element's properties (sources 3.3);
- keeps the demo state as `demochange` tells it, and gives it to the segment's element after the example, or may not give it at all (T60; R42). In JSX `workedExample` stands before `demoState`, as `history` before `marks`: React assigns the changed properties in the JSX order, and a state before the example is a `TypeError`. React listens to the event by its exact name — `ondemochange`. As with `tool`, `app` gives the state that `demochange` told, not a new literal on every re-render: that would bring the demo back to its start;
- does not give `tool` at all, or listens to `toolchange`: the learner chooses the tool with the element's buttons, and a re-render of `app` must not bring back the former one. A task without the eraser brings back the pen with no event, so after a new task `app` takes `tool` from the element: otherwise its `tool = "eraser"` is a `TypeError` (R28);
- sets `--novatrail-canvas-top` on the element if it has a sticky header of its own — otherwise the row of buttons will go under it (R28). The element reads the offset even when the row does not stick: a step of «Выше» and «Ниже» and an opened part of the example do not go under the header (T92);
- remembers that in a low window the row of an element without a height does not stick and goes away with the scrolling: «Выше» and «Ниже» are then at hand only while the row is visible. If they are always needed, `app` gives the element a height by the window — then the page does not scroll it and the row does not go away (T92; R33);
- does nothing for pen mode and the sheet's zoom: they are one for the whole page and hold until a reload, and a new task does not reset them (R29, R73). An element without a height scrolls together with the page — with one finger off the drawing surface and with two on it, and with the buttons «Выше» and «Ниже» by one pointer. `app` may also give the element a height on a tablet: the buttons scroll the sheet in it in the same way (R33);
- remembers that the element is one Tab stop on the page, and an element with a height, which scrolls its own sheet, is two: the row and the sheet itself (T93; section 3.1). `pointer-events: none` for the time of the check stops its buttons only for the pointer: from the keyboard, undo, clearing and scrolling by the buttons work even then (R20, R28, R33);
- allows `blob:` pictures in the content security policy, `img-src blob:`: the element shows the pictures of the statement and of the demo at such addresses (R23, R42). The element needs nothing else from the policy: it asks for no inline styles, fonts or network, and it works under the policy `default-src 'none'; style-src 'self'; img-src blob:` (R51);
- listens to `historychange` on the element itself and appends the events to the history on the device, and stores the history and the context as they are, through JSON (R27);
- reads `filled` — which boxes of the workbook, or the sheet's slot `anywhere` and `sheet`, are filled — for the exit condition of the step `answer_slots_filled` (R37, R42; concepts 7 spec);

**The probe and publishing.** Before the first release the repository's own `release.yml` published nothing: `publish: false` and `npm-package: "@altnovatrail/canvas"` (T66). ### 3.5. The sample file

Since `spec` v0.9.8 the sample file is the eval protocol: the message `Sample` of the package `novatrail.eval.v1`, its markup is the field `markup`, and the rules of the groups, the crop, the snapping and the boxes are those the device and the runner share (R93–R95 spec; [the text of the contracts](../spec/contracts/README.md), the sections "The sample file" and "The rules the device and the runner share"). Below is what `canvas` proposed to the protocol by R09. Where the protocol decided otherwise — without pressure, the markup by parts and truth rather than by lines, the versions `0.0.0` and `none` for synthetic samples, a license on an eval-set sample — is named by the 2026-11-08 amendment to R09. The options are section 2.4, the sources — sources 1.1, 1.4 and 5.2. What the page asks a person and shows them was decided by T28 — section 3.6 (R11) —, whether the build opened from a file works was checked by the probe T29 (R52), and on Android by T74 (R54).

**How the file is built** (R48). The module `src/eval/` — outside the package, without the DOM, in Node and in the browser — builds `Sample` with the code of `@altnovatrail/contracts`: `sampleOf` from the purpose, "who wrote", the provenance, the writer code, the reference to the task, the sheet or the workbook with the writing history, and the markup in the sheet's stroke numbers. `sampleText` writes the file with every field, zeros too, by the proto names, `readSample` reads it strictly, and `personId`, `writerCode` and `syntheticId` give the ID and the code. The order of computation is below, with what T17 decided:
- the device builds the groups and the table of boxes by the raw history;
- the sample's history — time in whole milliseconds, positions to a tenth, without pressure;
- the final geometry — the strokes in the canonical direction, with the start higher, and when equal, further left; points along the stroke's length every 0.25 units, while the ends and the points that hold the stroke's box stay; the numbers by place;
- the crops — by the file's strokes, at 16, 24, 32 and 48 px and at the module's height, with h_med in `bundle_median`. Their numbers for the geometry are the same as for the history, and the pixels differ by no more than a quarter of the gray scale — that is how the step was chosen.

Examples of each composition are in `examples/`: a test writes them, and `ci-licenses` checks them by `spec`'s recipe `sample-check` (section 3.7).

**Synthetic samples** (R49). The generator `src/eval/synthetic.ts` draws 16 samples from the seeds 101–116 on the tasks of `tutor`'s file of open views from `tasks/` — since T99 v0.1.22, of format 0.4, whose banks and tasks are byte for byte those of v0.1.10 (the T99 amendment to R49): sheets of lines and of free writing and workbook pages, without essays. The hand is `src/eval/hand.ts`, with its own shapes of digits and signs, through `Recorder` into `Work`, with erasing, clearing and undo. The scenarios are a correct solution, one with an error, an erased one, a cleared one and an undone one, a crossed-out one, a mirrored digit, an illegible answer, two answers and none, an empty sheet, a number line, and pages full, with empty and crossed-out boxes, with a mirrored digit, and empty; the markup says what is drawn, and the seed chooses `first_error`. The set is not in git: `just synthetic <folder>` builds the generator with `tsc` into `build/` and writes the files `<ID>.json`, and prints the rows "ID — purpose — writer — scenario" for the release's description (T24).

**Synthetic samples of a module's eval set** (R63; R110 spec). `just synthetic-module <package> <tag> <folder> <task>:<seed>...` writes samples of a module's eval set on its package — the folder that `module-kit build` wrote at the commit the samples go into. The recipe runs the same file that every release carries, `synthetic-module_v<X.Y.Z>.mjs`, built from the tree with version 0.0.0, and `modules`'s CI takes the release's file, once `release-verify` has checked it, and calls it with the same arguments after `--module` (R68). Samples that will match what the release's file writes in `modules`'s CI are written by that release's file, or by the recipe in a clone of `canvas` at its tag: the recipe at another commit builds other code. A second mode of the same file, `--digest <package> <task>...`, prints a row per task — its ID and the digest of its open view by the same projection as the reference in a sample —, and by it `modules`'s CI checks people's samples against the commit's package (R95 spec). Every task with an open view has a digest, and so do choice, essay and zone, which the generator does not draw; a template has no open view, and a task that is not in the package has no digest: the call prints the rows of the others, names such tasks after them and exits with code 1 (the T97 amendment to R63). On all 773 tasks without templates of the packages of `modules` v0.2.1 — of format 0.4 — the digests matched those of `module-kit open` v0.3.0, and the tests hold the digests of the essays and choices of `english-essays` 0.2.0, copies in `tasks/` (the T98 amendment to R63). From the package `inventory.json` and `server.json` are taken: the inventory must be of format 0.4 and name `server.json` by its digest and size, and the package digest is the sha256 of the inventory. `moduleTasksOf` builds a task's open view from the canonical view by the same tables of open fields by which T21 checks `tutor`'s file, only here the closed fields are dropped. On all 773 tasks without templates of the release `modules` v0.2.0, the view and its digest matched byte for byte what `module-kit open` v0.3.0 gives. The sample is an eval set's, by an adult in their own handwriting or imitating a child's, without a writer code, under Apache-2.0, with the source `modules`: the tag the call named, the module's ID and version, and the package digest. The seed chooses the scenario and the writer, and the ID is `synthetic-<seed>`. The generator draws a sheet with one place `anywhere` and a workbook page; a template, a choice, an essay and a zone it rejects and names. A file of the same seed in the folder is written anew only if it is a sample of the same task of the same module; otherwise the call writes nothing.

**A sample is not a submission for checking.** Section 3.2 says that `sheet_box`, the table of groups, the strokes and their time do not leave the device: that is the rule of checking (R09 spec). A sample has a path of its own. It is made for eval, with consent, and the file goes where its purpose directs (R25 spec; П-10, П-16). So it holds what a submission does not — as much as the eval of this purpose needs.

**Three fields** set what lies in the file and where it may go:

| Field | Values | Why |
|---|---|---|
| purpose | measurement; eval set | a measurement sample from an adult — outside git, at `tutor` (R05 tutor); an eval-set sample — a pull request into `modules`, Apache-2.0, cannot be withdrawn (R20 spec; concepts 3 spec) |
| who wrote | an adult in their own handwriting; an adult imitating a child's; a child | a child — only with the purpose "measurement", but their sample does not go to `tutor` together with the adults' samples: it lies outside the repositories, without a pull request, and where the file goes and who has access to it is for П-10 and П-12 to decide. It does not go to AI until П-3 has given a contract that admits children (R25 spec; R24 spec) |
| provenance | person; synthetic — with a seed | does not change the composition. For synthetic samples the sample's ID is derived from the seed: one seed gives the same file byte for byte (T18). For a person the ID is random |

By these fields `modules`'s CI accepts only an eval-set sample from an adult (T02 modules), and `tutor`'s runner does not send a child sample to AI. Synthetic samples come out in each of the two compositions below, and `tutor`'s CI checks both (T18; R38 spec).

**Composition:**

| What | measurement by an adult | eval set and child |
|---|---|---|
| strokes | the writing history, as the element gives it (section 3.3): a stroke is an ID, a pointer type and points of x, y, time and pressure, in the order of writing; erasings, clearings and undos with the time and the IDs they removed or brought back (section 3.1; R22) | the final geometry of the visible strokes: the points go along the stroke's length, the stroke's direction is canonical, the strokes are numbered by place; no time, no pressure, no order of writing, nothing erased |
| crops | at every digit height of the protocol; the working one is the module's height | the same |
| groups | the boxes in the fractions of every crop and the table "label → stroke IDs" | the same |
| crop parameters | for every crop — D, D′, `s`, `sheet_box`, width, height and margins; shared — `h_med` and the sheet's size (R25) | the same |
| markup and reference to the task | below | the same |
| about the person | purpose, who wrote, the version of the consent text — the version of the page's text set (R11), writer code | for an eval set — without a writer code, for a child — with it |
| versions | of the format and of `canvas` | the same |

Time is milliseconds from the first event of the sample, without a date, and coordinates are the sheet's units with a precision of 0.1 (section 3.2). By the scale A4 of the study "A child's handwriting on the canvas and the AI tutor" («Рукопись ребёнка на канве и ИИ-наставник»), the writing history is class (c), and the final geometry with groups is (b): the division into strokes and the split into groups by time remain in it (sources 1.4; П-18).

**What the file does not hold:** name, email, GitHub account, device, browser, screen, language, date, age and grade. A name written on the sheet itself would get into the strokes and the crops. The page says that it is not written on the sheet (T28). For an eval-set sample the name on the sample is looked for by T02 modules, and for measurement and child samples only by whoever collects them: who does it, and when before a run, is the question П-18.

**The writer code** is random, the page makes it, and it is linked to no one. The lines of one sheet are «связаны писавшим» ("linked by the writer"), and the measurement's boundary is now counted by sheets (architecture 3.1 tutor): by the code the runner will also take into account the link between the sheets of one writer. For a child, the adult writes the code down beside the consent, and by the code the samples are withdrawn and deleted when their period ends (П-10). The page does not remember the code: a person enters their code themselves (R11; section 3.6).

**The order of computation** is such that the runner, from what is recorded, repeats what is recorded:
1. the groups are built by the raw history, by space and time (R06; R24). The labels are placed by position, top to bottom, so as not to give away the order of writing. The rule computes in tenths of a unit and in whole milliseconds, as the sample will record them, so by the recorded history it gives the same groups;
2. in eval-set and child samples the strokes are numbered anew, by place, and the table of groups and the markup move to the new numbers: sequential numbers would give away the order of writing;
3. the points are rounded, and in eval-set and child samples also resampled;
4. by the recorded geometry `h_med` is computed, and for every crop D, D′, `s`, `sheet_box`, width, height, margins and, by the groups, the boxes, by the formulas of section 3.2 (R25).

The crop computes in tenths of a unit, as the sample writes, so the crop of a measurement sample from an adult matches `app`'s crop byte for byte. In eval-set and child samples the points are also resampled. The step is taken such that no difference is visible on the crop, and the end points of a stroke stay: then the crop's numbers are the same too (R25). T17 chooses the step.

The rule of the groups and the rules of the crop are one for the device and the runner. Their test vectors go from the writing history to the groups and from the geometry with groups to `h_med`, `s`, `sheet_box` and the boxes, not to PNG bytes. Samples without a history carry the groups the device built, and the runner does not recompute them (the epic E3 in `spec`'s RUN.md).

The test vectors of these rules are `spec`'s: four files of `vectors/eval/v1/` of the npm package `@altnovatrail/contracts`, as messages of the protocol — the writing history to the groups, the geometry with groups to the stroke bundles and the crop's numbers, a model's box to the strokes it takes, and a page's strokes to the boxes and the table of the filled ones (R94 spec). These are the vectors `canvas` wrote before the tag v0.1.2, turned into the protocol's form: the history in them is without pressure and in whole milliseconds, and the groups also have groups of an erased stroke. They hold no PNG pixels: two implementations may differ by a gray level. `canvas`'s tests check its code against them (section 3.7), and the rule changes only in `spec` — `canvas` brings an edit as a row «в реестр spec» (R47; the 2026-11-08 amendments to R24–R26 and R37).

**The crops** are drawn by `canvas` at saving — by the same DOM-free code as the crop for checking (section 3.1), at every digit height the protocol names. Now these are 16, 24, 32 and 48 px (A6 of the study; R08 tutor). The working one is the module's digit height: at it `app` sends the crop. The runner compares the heights «парно с 32 px на тех же строках» (architecture 3.1 tutor), and so the comparison runs on one render. The states — after a line and a pause within a line — are drawn by the runner, from the writing history, by the same rules. So only measurement samples from adults have them.

**A workbook sample** (R34, R38; section 3.8) carries:
- the crops of the filled boxes at the eval heights, with the item and the box, — by the same `cellsForCheck` as the work for checking;
- the table "box → stroke IDs", which the device built by the raw strokes. The runner does not recompute it, as it does not the groups, and in eval-set and child samples it moves to the new stroke numbers, as the table of groups does;
- the rectangles of all the page's boxes in the page's units — those the workbook's digest holds. The runner does not need `canvas`'s layout;
- in eval-set and child samples only the boxes' strokes, and in a measurement sample from an adult the whole writing history (R09).

A workbook sample has no groups and no group boxes: the table of boxes takes their place (R34). So the rows "groups" in the composition above and in the comparison with `tutor`'s format below are about the sheet. The workbook's markup is the value in every box, and the table of boxes replaces "the strokes of every line" on it. The eval protocol took the rule of the boxes as it is, with its vectors (В47; R94 spec), and the sample is built by the page — since T20 (R58) — and by the synthetic samples of T18 (the 2026-10-27 amendment to R34).

**The markup** is what the sample must say: the field `markup` of the protocol (R95 spec; [the text of the contracts](../spec/contracts/README.md), the section "The sample file", "The markup"). `canvas` proposed a markup by lines, as in `tutor`'s format (T12 tutor) — with the sheet's scenario, the intended text of a line and the expected outcome —, and the protocol decided otherwise (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R09):
- the truth is an observation: every answer slot that AI reads, with its value as it is written, or with the reason why there is none. It holds no outcome and no trap: the runner gets them by the key;
- the notes of the answer slots are "mirrored digit" and "crossed out and written anew";
- on a sheet — the parts of the solution: kind, text, strokes, "crossed out", "illegible" and "final answer", and the number of the first error; for an essay — instead of parts, the assessments of the criteria, which the organizer gives;
- on a workbook — neither parts nor assessments: its places are the boxes.

The page marks up a sample after the writing, before saving, and saves it only when the markup is enough (R56; section 3.6). The parts of a sheet are the device's groups, which the writer merges, and the sheet's truth is derived from the parts with the result. After saving, the markup does not change. The page calls no AI, so the markup does not adapt to the model's answer (R65 canvas-api). A child sample is marked up by an adult, from the child's words, after the writing (T20).

**The reference to the task** carries no key: the canvas does not get it (R05 spec), and the key lies with the runner, in the task bank or in the module package. The reference holds:
- the source: the release tag and the sha256 of the file from which the task came into the build (the 2026-10-21 amendment to R25 spec);
- the module or the task bank, and its version;
- the task's ID and the seed of a template task (concepts 9.3 spec);
- the digest of its open view: it checks the seed. Whether it will be the same for a task from `tutor`'s file and from the `modules` release is not known: the view of an open task in `tutor`'s file is temporary, until the file moves to the task schema of R51 spec (the 2026-10-21 and 2026-10-26 amendments to R25 spec). `canvas` does not match the samples of the two sources: the reference names its source, and whether to match them, and by what, is for `tutor`'s runner to decide, row В71 of table 3 of [RUN.md](../RUN.md) (R62).

**What the page shows and writes with each source**, by the 2026-10-21 amendment to R25 spec, with which `spec` answered В6 (R16):

| Source | What it shows | What goes into the reference |
|---|---|---|
| the open views of the measurement's tasks from a `tutor` release, while `modules` has no first content | the statement, the items, the answer slots and the media files, from the open view | `tutor`: the release's tag and sha256, the bank and its version, the task's ID, the digest |
| a `modules` release through the CLI `module-kit`, from its first content on | the same | `modules`: the release's tag and sha256, the module and its version, the task's ID, the seed, the digest |
| a file of tasks that the adult opens on the page, a fallback if `tutor` does not come to release the file | the same | the file's sha256 instead of a tag, the bank and its version, the task's ID, the digest |

The amendment does not accept a page without tasks: a task's number from the volunteer's words is not the sheet the learner has.

**What the build needs from the CLI `module-kit`**, for T05 module-kit (its section 2.5; R25 spec):
- the open views of specific instances of tasks: for a template task, with the seed, since the page does not compute templates;
- the media files they refer to;
- the module's ID and version and the digest of its package;
- the digest of each task's open view.

Whether this is a command of the CLI's own or the card's commands are enough, T05 module-kit decides.

**Modules' tasks in the build** (T101; R71). A command of its own came out: `module-kit open` writes `open-views.json` — the open view of each task with its digest, for a template an instance for each seed — and the files of the device part (R43 module-kit). The release's build runs without the network, so the page's build takes a copy of this output in `tasks/modules-v0.2.4/`: the `open-views.json` of the `modules` release v0.2.4 with the seeds 1–5, the release's index, and for each module `LICENSE`, `NOTICE` and the files of `media/` that the statements of the taken tasks name. `ci-licenses` writes the output anew — with the binary of `module-kit` v0.3.0 and the archives of the packages that `release-verify` accepted — and compares it with the copy byte for byte (`just modules-open`):

- only the eval set takes the modules' tasks, and the measurement and the child sample session take the tasks of `tutor`'s file (the author's decision, RUN.md, T101; Д4);
- the element gets the module manifest — the digit height (R32) — and the media from the build's data, and the file's notices name each module with its `NOTICE`.

**Signatures.** CI takes the CLI `module-kit` as the binary of its release by exact tag, not by `go install`, and before the first run checks its signature with `spec`'s recipe `release-verify`. So too the `modules` release and `tutor`'s file (R77 spec; Д54 in `spec`'s RUN.md). From then on their digest holds the files.

**Comparison with `tutor`'s format** (T12 tutor; В7 tutor):

**The writer code** is one per person, not per device. The page makes it but does not remember it: a person enters their code themselves or asks for a new one. Measurement samples and child samples have it, and an eval-set sample does not (section 3.5).

**The adult's steps:**
1. **Opening.** The page says what it is: a person writes the solution of a task by hand, and the page saves the sample as a file on this device. The first measurement is on computers and Android, and the page does not move to hosting yet (the 2026-11-08 amendments to R25 spec; the T85 amendment to R55).
2. **Purpose**: the measurement or the eval set. It is chosen at every opening, before the writing, and the page does not remember it. The child sample session is a separate button, below.
3. **Code**: for the measurement, and of the adult's purposes only for it: «ввести свой код» or «новый писавший». The code step of a child's session is its own, below. The page shows a new code and asks that it be written down: consent to the measurement samples is withdrawn by it.

   - who will see the file:
     - a measurement sample: the organizer and the AI providers that the consent text names (В9 tutor);
     - an eval-set sample: everyone. It goes into the open `modules` forever, under Apache-2.0, and everyone gets a "perpetual, worldwide … irrevocable" right to copy and change it (sources 8.1). It is also run at AI providers in `modules`' CI (R24 spec). How the sample gets linked to a GitHub account through the DCO sign-off depends on who opens the pull request (T02 modules; П-18);
   - the term and how to withdraw consent. Consent to a measurement sample is withdrawn by the writer code, and the notice names honestly what will remain: the AI providers' logs, the summary report without handwriting and the copy of the file on the device (privacy 1 spec, "Honest retention periods"). An eval-set sample cannot be withdrawn (concepts 3 spec);
   - that nothing personal is written on the sheet: no name, signature, school or contacts.

   The notice has two statements without which one cannot go on. The first is «мне 18 лет или больше, и по законам места, где я живу, я вправе заключить этот договор» (П-6; R124 spec; the T88 amendment to R11). The second is «форма согласия версии … подписана и принята организатором». The statements are the page's words, not the set's: in the part `legal` they stand in the forms (R69).
5. **Writing and markup**, by section 3.5 and R56 (T81). «Кто писал» is set for every sample: in one's own handwriting or imitating a child's. After the writing, «Разметить»: the page shows the crop that the model will see, and the markup form.
   - On a sheet, the parts of the solution come first, one device group per part. The writer merges neighboring ones, splits and reorders them, and for each enters the text — or a drawing in words — and the flags «зачёркнута», «написана неразборчиво нарочно», «итоговый ответ задачи». «Показать на листе» outlines the part's groups with the element's boxes. Below come the first error, the final answer as written, and the marks «зеркальная цифра» and «зачёркнуто и написано заново».
   - On an essay — a sheet whose only answer place is bound to `sheet` — there are no parts: the writer enters the essay's text as written, with its errors and line breaks, or marks that it cannot be read whole, and a sheet without strokes is an empty essay. The organizer gives the criteria's scores after the writing, and the page writes them empty (T21; R62).
   - On a workbook, each box of the page: an empty one is named so, and a filled one has its crop, its value as written, «неразборчиво» or «можно прочесть по-разному», and the same marks.
   - A new stroke after «Разметить» removes the form: the markup is asked for anew. The page names what is missing, and saving is possible only when the markup is enough and it is confirmed that the sheet holds nothing personal.
6. **Saving:**
   - the page shows the crops — what the model will see — and, briefly, what else lies in the file;
   - the file is named by the sample's ID, `<id>.json`, for any purpose: so it goes into a module's `eval/` without renaming (the T101 amendment to R58). The ID of a person's sample is 12 characters of `a`–`z` and `2`–`7`, so it will not start with `synthetic-`;
   - the person confirms that the sheet holds nothing personal;
   - the file goes where the browser puts downloads. Where that is on each device, and whether that folder syncs with a cloud, was checked by T29 and, on tablets, by T74 (the 2026-11-02 amendment to R17). On Linux, Firefox and Chrome by default put the file straight into the downloads folder, asking nothing; for the author it is `~/Downloads`, and it does not sync with a cloud (T29; measurements 1.1). Chrome on Android 16 in the emulator puts a download in `/sdcard/Download`, also without asking (T74). Safari on iPadOS 26.5 and 27 asks "Do you want to download “…”?" with "Download" and "Close", and the download goes into «На iPad» → "Downloads"; it can be opened there, but the page's script does not run in Quick Look, and the page can save nothing on the iPad (T80). For Safari on the iPad this is "iCloud Drive" or "On My iPad", as chosen in the settings (sources 3.6), and with "iCloud Drive" the sample file, a child's too, goes to Apple's cloud. Where the page proposes to put the file was chosen by the planning of T84 (В30): in a child's session, before saving, it asks to check that Safari on the iPad saves downloads «На iPad», not in iCloud Drive; the page does not see the setting (R59). Whether this is enough is decided by the author on Claude's analysis in epic E4 of `spec`'s RUN.md (the 2026-10-22 addition to П-10; R112 spec);
   - where to pass the file: a measurement sample and an eval-set sample go to the organizer, by an upload link that is valid for a day, and then the file is erased from the device (R125 spec, R126 spec); the organizer brings an eval-set sample into `modules`. By the author's draft, as agreed with the measurement's organizer.

**The child sample session** is led by an adult, and the child writes. While the set `child-sample` has no current version, the session is closed.
- **Before the first session** the adult opens the page without the child and asks for a new code. They write the code into the consent form, a parent or guardian signs it, and the organizer accepts it with the code already in it (П-10). By the code, consent is later withdrawn and the samples are deleted by their term.
- **The session's notice:**
  - the file holds the final geometry without time and pressure, crops, markup, «кто писал» — the child — and the child's code, which links all their samples (section 3.5);
  - the file does not go into a repository, and does not go to an AI while П-3 has given no agreement that admits children (R24 spec);
  - where to pass the file and who is admitted to it, by П-10 and П-12;
  - GitHub saw the IP address of the adult who downloaded the page (the amendment to R25 spec). The release's description says the same before the download (R66).
- **The adult's statements:** «мне 18 лет или больше, и по законам места, где я живу, я вправе заключить этот договор» and «отдельная письменная форма родителя или опекуна этого ребёнка версии … с этим кодом подписана и принята организатором» (П-10). The form is accepted before the session, as in R26 canvas-api.
- **The code** the adult enters at the start of each session: the one written into the form.
- **Writing and markup.** The child writes, and the adult marks up, from the child's words, after the writing (section 3.5).
- **Saving** is as for an adult, but without a pull request. The notice advises passing the file on and deleting it from the device, and after saving the page says that the file goes to the organizer by their upload link, which is valid for a day, and then the adult erases it from the device (R125 spec; the T88 amendment to R59).

This is how the session is made (T84; R59):
- in «Начале» it is the last choice, in the nested group «Сеанс образца ребёнка» below the adult's purposes; without a current set `child-sample` it is closed, with the reason;
- the code step is shared with the measurement, but has words of its own: «ввести код из формы согласия» or «новый код для формы — без ребёнка». The page shows a new code with the version of the text set, and «Начать» does not open with it: a session runs only with the code of an accepted form. On a switch between the measurement and the session, the code step is cleared;
- «Кто писал» is not asked in the session, and the part's flag «написана неразборчиво нарочно» is labeled «не прочесть и со слов ребёнка»: only the writer knows whether it was on purpose, and without the flag a child's unreadable part cannot be marked. What the flag means for a child sample is row В68 «в реестр spec»;
- before saving, the phrase about Safari's downloads on the iPad, and after it, «куда передать файл» from the session's texts.

**«Открыть образец»** is a section under «Начало» (T87; R64). An eval-set organizer, a module author, the measurement's organizer or the adult who led a child's session opens a sample file in it and, before a push or a handover, looks for a name, a signature, a school and contacts, both in the strokes and in the texts of the markup, which are published with the sample (R110 spec; П-12). Neither an AI nor an agent sees the file: a person looks at it on their own device.
- A sample of any purpose opens. `readSample` reads it, and only `sample-check` checks it by the rules (R93 spec): a file the page did not read is named an error, and nothing is shown.
- The page draws the strokes from the file alone: a sheet by its sizes, a workbook each box with its place. No task is needed for this: the file opens even when its task is not in the build. Of a writing history, what the sheet showed at the end is seen, and the checkbox «показать стёртое» adds, dashed, everything written and not visible at the end — the erased, the cleared and what undo took off: it too lies in the file. The picture does not cut a stroke beyond the sheet's edge. Below come the crops from the file, what the model will see, and the markup in words: the purpose, who wrote, the license, the task, the truth by places, the parts with their texts, the first error and the criteria's scores.
- The file is not changed, not saved and goes nowhere: no network, no downloads, no storage. «К началу» is a new opening of the page. Once writing has begun, the section is not there.

**What the page remembers** of people: nothing, no codes, no purpose, no declarations. All this is asked at every opening. It does not remember an unfinished sheet either — so T81 decided (R56) —, and whether storage works on a page opened from a file was checked by T29 and T74 (the 2026-11-02 amendment to R17). By the browsers' sources it behaves differently: in Chromium all pages from `file:` share one storage, and in Firefox each file has its own (sources 3.6). The probe of T29 confirmed it. In Chromium a second file and a copy in another folder see the first one's record, and it survives a restart. In Firefox 159 the storage is per path: it takes a copy with the same name in another folder for another file. For the author, Firefox's record did not survive a restart; by Firefox's sources this happens only if clearing site data on close or the private mode is on, and by default both are off (measurements 1.1). Chrome on Android opens a page from the downloads as `content://media/external/downloads/<number>`, and all such pages have one storage, as `file:` has in Chromium, and it survives a restart of Chrome (T74). So the storage of a page from a file can be trusted with nothing that would be a pity to lose: a new build, a file in another folder or a browser setting loses it. Were the page to keep an unfinished sheet, in Chromium any local HTML file would read it.

### 3.8. The workbook

The second canvas kind of the first slice is the workbook for school math of grade 3 (R41 spec). A task's item is a line of pieces of text and boxes, and the answer in a box is a number or a fraction by hand (R51 spec). T46 decided the workbook (R34), and T47, T51, T48, T49 and T53 built it (R35–R39). The options are subsection 2.7, the sources sources 9.12 and 9.13, the probes measurements 1.4. Here is the workbook whole. Where it is a part of the shared design, the details stand there: the layers are section 3.1, the units, the box rule and the crop 3.2, the element's properties, context and types 3.3, the sample 3.5, the code and the tests 3.7.

**The page** is 1000 × 1400 units, like the sheet: a constant of the kind, without a grid (R34). The statement is above it, HTML, in the same scrolling as on the sheet (R23). The items go in lines, in order, with numbers on the left, and what the lines did not take is the draft.

**The layout**: `pageOf` in the DOM-free part, a function of the task alone, in integers (R35):
- the font size is 30 units, a box 100 × 100, the margins 40, two columns of 440 with a gap of 40, rows 32 apart. A line with a box is 100 high, one without a box 46;
- a short item — a line of one row no wider than 384 — stands two to a row, by lines. A long one takes the whole width, 864, and wraps: by pieces; a piece wider than the line, by words and boxes; a word wider than the line, by clusters. A box holds on to the word after it;
- the number "n." is right-aligned in a column the width of "00.", on the baseline of the item's first line, and the line begins at 56 from the item's left edge;
- a page holds up to 10 rows: up to 20 short items or up to 10 long ones of one line. A row that would go below 1360 is a `TypeError` on `task.items[i]` of its first item: the boxes and the font size do not shrink (R34). The limit of lines for authors is В49 «в реестр spec»;
- the widths of characters are a table of our own, in hundredths of the font size, the mean of Liberation Sans and Liberation Serif (`widths.ts`), and characters differ only by explicit ranges of codes. So the layout is one in any engine and in Node, and the device's font does not move it: a piece is one `<text>`, fitted to its width by `textLength` and `lengthAdjust="spacingAndGlyphs"`;
- the entry point `core` does not give the layout, and its numbers and rule since format 0.4 are `spec`'s: `format/page.yaml` and its test vectors, which the package `@altnovatrail/spec` carries and to which the tests hold the layout (the T85 amendment to R35). `canvas`'s former vectors, [vectors/pages.json](../vectors/pages.json), are no longer written.

**The template layer** (R36): SVG with the same `viewBox` as the sheet's grid: numbers and pieces of text, boxes `<rect role="img">` with a frame of 1 CSS px on whole device pixels. The print is in the ink's color and the consumer's font, and it resets the rest of what is inherited. A screen reader reads the lines as a list, in order, and the boxes by the names from the table of labels: «Окошко», «Окошко 1», in the element's language. The lines' language is from the module manifest, and a value that the example's demo wrote into a box is voiced in it, by a print node of its own right after the box, not in its name (T107; section 3.9). The element is horizontal under any writing mode of the page. The print grows with the sheet's zoom: the buttons «Крупнее» and «Мельче» take it to 200 % at any width, and the workbook's page is not laid out anew and scrolls sideways: 1.4.10 allows it a two-dimensional layout. So the lines pass 1.4.4, and the nonconformance that `app` declared goes away (T94; R73).

**Boxes and the draft** (R37): the rule `cellOf`, in integers, without DOM and without time, for each stroke separately:
- a stroke is a box's if the middle of the bounding box of its points lies in the box widened by 120 thousandths of its size, and the bounding box itself is no wider and no higher than 1600 thousandths of the box. With a box of 100 this is a margin of 12 and a limit of 160;
- of two boxes, the one with more of the stroke's points, then the earlier by (item, box). On the page of R35 the widened boxes do not overlap, and there is no disputed case;
- the rest is the draft: it is on the page and in the writing history, but does not go to checking;
- the rule takes the same strokes as `exportWork()`: the stroke under the pen is not there, and the strokes under the eraser before lift-off are;
- the test vectors are `vectors/eval/v1/boxes.json` of the package `@altnovatrail/contracts` (R94 spec), and the set of the exhaustive search by which the margin and the limit were chosen is `src/core/workbooks.fixture.ts`.

There are no groups of R24 on a workbook: the boxes "47 ÷ 6 = [ ] r [ ]" stand closer than their threshold, and one group would take both. Their place is taken by the table "box → stroke IDs" (R34).

**Filled state**: the property `filled` (R37): per item, per box, whether it is filled, in the task's order. On a sheet, the only answer slot `anywhere` or `sheet`, and for a sheet with only `choice` and without a task, `null` (R42; section 3.3). A box is filled if it holds at least one stroke. The frame of a filled box is 3 CSS px, its name «Окошко, заполнено», and in forced colors the frame is Highlight. By `filled`, `app` learns of a "filled answer slot" (concepts 7 spec).

**To checking** (R38) the whole page goes at once. `exportWork()` gives `{ cells, submission }`:
- `cells`: the R25 crop of each filled box by its strokes, as one group, at the manifest's digit height, with the item and the box, in the task's order. At eval's heights this is 200 × 200 px, 64 tokens (section 3.2);
- `submission`: the context `{ boxes, digest }`: the table of boxes and the digest of its strokes, of all the page's boxes and of the table's rows;
- not a single filled box: `null`, even with a draft.

An empty box gives no crop: its verdict is "unreadable" with the reason `blank` (R56 spec), and `filled` says which boxes are empty. Only the crops with their places leave the device, and the table and the digest stay with `app` (R09 spec).

**Marks** (R39) come with the answer slot by place, `{ item, slot }`, from 1, and hold on to the strokes of their submission's table row. The frame stands around the box and the visible ink of its strokes together, with a margin of 10. A group label and a model's box on a workbook are a `TypeError`. A mark outlines an empty box by itself, without strokes, and a box not from the page gives no frame (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R39). The consumer gives `task` and `history` before `marks`, and a new page sets the marks anew or takes them off, without an event.

**The workbook's sample** (R34, R38; section 3.5) carries the boxes' crops at eval's heights, the table of boxes that the device built, and the boxes' rectangles in the page's units. It is built by the samples page, since T20 (R58), and by the synthetic samples of T18 (R49).

**What is still open:**
- `spec`'s answers: what goes to checking (В45), the contract of `canvas` and `app` (В46), boxes in the eval protocol (В47), the card and the vocabulary (В48) and the limit of lines (В49): rows «в реестр spec» of table 3 of [RUN.md](../RUN.md);
- devices, T22: the emulators passed its checklist (R65), and R65 did not check the items below, which are in the debt Д7: the font size and the fonts of the lines on children's tablets, the text, its reset and the frames in Safari, VoiceOver and TalkBack by lines and by touch, a fraction by finger and by pen in a box, the margin and the limit of the box rule ("Revisit" R35–R37). Until the first release the layout's numbers and rule are changed by an amendment with new vectors, and after it only by a decision of their own (R34; section 3.4). The limit of lines will enter the format as a rule of the version 0.y that the modules' requests bring, and then the numbers, the table of widths and `vectors/pages.json` move to `format/`. Until then `canvas` brings a change of the numbers as a row «в реестр spec» too: by it `spec` corrects the guide for authors in the format's README, §14 (Д57 in `spec`'s RUN.md; the 2026-11-02 amendment to R35);
- the place and the look of the answer slot's field in the contract and the mark of an empty box were decided by `spec` (R97 spec; the 2026-11-08 amendment to R39), and the color and the look of the frame by the first slice of `app` and `api` (R39);
- a printed two-level fraction, column arithmetic (T38 in `spec`'s RUN.md) and input into the boxes from the keyboard (R51 spec): later.

### 3.9. The task's fields, filled answer slots and the worked example

What `spec` v0.2.40–v0.2.42 gave `canvas` (R40): the task's open fields, the step's exit condition "there is something in each answer slot" and the lesson's worked example on the author's demo layer (R57 spec, R58 spec). The decision is R42 and subsection 2.8, and epic E7 built it all. The details are sections 3.1 and 3.3.

**The task's fields** `givens` and `unknown` — "givens and unknown" — the element takes and checks strictly, like the schema, and does not show: the chips are shown and marked by `app` (T55; the 2026-11-01 amendment to R32). The types of the task and of the manifest are the package `@altnovatrail/spec`'s (R41).

**`filled` on a sheet** (T57; R42): for a task with a single answer slot `anywhere` or `sheet`, `[[true]]` if the sheet holds a stroke that `exportWork()` would take, otherwise `[[false]]`; for a sheet with only `choice`, for a sheet without a task and for an example, `null`. The values are two frozen arrays for all the elements, and the time of change is as on the workbook (R37). By the slot binding, `app` reads the exit condition `answer_slots_filled`: for `choice`, its own choice of an option, for the rest, `filled`.

**The worked example**: the property `workedExample`, the open view of the segment `worked_example`, of the type `WorkedExample` of `spec`'s package (T58, T59; R42):
- **the check** is strict, as for the task, with a path from `workedExample`: the canvas is a sheet with a grid or, since format 0.4, a workbook with the example's lines (R61), a demo part is a paragraph, a picture beside the sheet with a size, a picture on an area of the sheet, or a workbook's box, `fill`. An area lies on the sheet by the rule `lesson.areas`, and the workbook's lines lie on the page, each value in its own box, and the demo fills each box once (`lesson.workbook-page`, `lesson.example-fills`). Equality is by content;
- **on a workbook** (T85; R61) the example's lines stand in the task's layout with empty boxes, and an opened step `fill` writes the box's value: in the middle of the box, on the line, as wide as its text by the table of widths. The frame names the box without the value, «Окошко 1», and the value is a print node of its own right after the frame, in the lines' language, and a screen reader reads it after the box. The list item and the announcement of the region `status` are «Окошко 1.1: 5»: the label's words are in the labels' language, and the value is a `<span>` of its own in the parts' language (T107; the T107 amendment to R61); a hidden demo hides the values too;
- **a task or an example**, not both. An example with `task` set, a non-empty history or a stroke in progress is a `TypeError`, and `app` gives each segment of a lesson a new element (R20);
- **the example mode**: the learner does not write: the input's drawing surface takes nothing, and a finger and a pen scroll the sheet with native scrolling. The history can only be empty, assigning marks is a `TypeError`, `exportWork()` and `filled` are `null`, and `tool` is the pen. The row of buttons is «Пример»: «Дальше», «Показ», «Выше» and «Ниже»;
- **the demo's parts** (T60) the learner opens one at a time with the button «Дальше», as a numbered list above the sheet, under the statement, and «Показ» hides and shows the whole demo. For `app`: the property `demoState`, `{ opened, hidden }`, and the event `demochange`, only from the learner's press, like `tool` and `toolchange` (R28). A polite region `status` announces the opened part, and the focus stays on «Дальше»;
- **pictures** (T64, T61): one loading per element: the element requests all the example's pictures when the example has arrived, and decodes their addresses before the demo, so that an opened part shows its picture in the same press. A picture beside the sheet is a frame of its own size in the list, and a picture on an area is an `<image>` on the demo layer, an SVG with the `viewBox` of the other layers: stretched over the area, and of overlapping ones, the one opened later on top. Its description is read in the list item, and the layer is `aria-hidden`. An opened part with an area scrolls the sheet to the area, and the others to their item;
- **accessibility**: the reading order is the row of buttons, the statement, the list of parts. Checked in the DOM and in Chromium's own tree; in forced colors the buttons and the frames are in system colors, the picture in its own (section 3.3).

`app` gives `workedExample` before `demoState`, as `history` before `marks`, listens to `ondemochange` and gives back the state that `demochange` told, not a new literal (section 3.3).

**What is still open:**
- the element's types from `@altnovatrail/spec/canvas`, with `workedExample`, `DemoState` and `demochange`, the element takes since T69 (the 2026-11-02 amendment to R07), and `Media` since T73 (R45). `spec`'s answers to В51–В55 — `canvas`'s types in the card, `filled` on a sheet, the example's property and mode, who opens the demo's parts, and transparent pictures on dark paper — came with v0.6.2, all by `canvas`'s recommendations (R44; the 2026-11-02 amendment to R42);
- devices: T22, by the 2026-11-01 amendment to R42 on the tasks of E5; the emulators passed its checklist (R65), and R65 did not check the items below, which are in the debt Д7: VoiceOver on the polite region `status` in a shadow root and on the hidden demo layer; scrolling the example's sheet by finger and by pen, and a pinch over it, on the iPad; a sheet that shifts in an element without a height when the list of parts grows; the picture of an opened part in the same press, the fallback decoding of the frame and the picture on an area by a `blob:` address in Safari; scrolling to the area's box with `visibility: hidden`;
- `app`'s lesson player may want to lead the parts itself, and the buttons and the announcement follow the standard of UI packages, R90 spec (section 3.10). The sketch "how to begin" and the learner's writing on the example's sheet come with minor versions of the format (R58 spec), and the example on a workbook came with format 0.4 (R61), while format 0.2 did not change the lesson: its only change is the digit height without a field, 24 px, and the element takes it since T68 (the 2026-11-01 amendment to R44 spec; the 2026-11-02 amendment to R32). Nor did format 0.3 change the lesson: the element read it since T77 (the 2026-11-08 amendment to R32), and since T85 it reads only 0.4 (R60).

### 3.10. Accessibility

The standard of UI packages is R90 spec: WCAG 2.2 level AA and, beyond it, two AAA criteria: 2.5.5, targets no smaller than 44 × 44 CSS px, and 2.3.3, motion by `prefers-reduced-motion`. What a package does not yet meet it names in its documents — the criterion, what stands instead and when it goes —, and `app` collects this on its page «Доступность» (R90 spec). Here is `canvas`'s list, the outcome of epic E10 (T95; R74): what the element holds and by what, what it declares, what is declared not by it but by `app`, and what is not checked. It is true for the release that carries T93 and T94 (R73); before it, `app` also keeps the nonconformance 1.4.4. The sources are sources 9.18, 9.19 and 9.20.

**Declared by `app`, not by `canvas`** (R90 spec; R11 app):
- an answer in handwriting: 2.1.1, 2.5.1 and 2.5.7, until a second way of answering (О-25). The exception of 2.1.1 does not exempt handwriting an answer: the path in it is the input technique, not what is input: "if using handwriting to enter text, the input technique (handwriting) requires path-dependent input but the underlying function (text input) does not" (Understanding 2.1.1). The element gives no second way, neither on the sheet nor in the workbook's boxes, and input into the boxes from the keyboard waits for `spec`'s decision (section 3.8). The element's eraser, undo, clearing and scrolling work from the keyboard and with a single pointer (R28, R33);
- submission on «Проверить» without confirmation or undo: 3.3.4; the button is `app`'s, and `exportWork()` submits nothing.

**Not nonconformances but limits**, which the consumer knows of:
- in an element without a height, in a low window, the row of buttons does not stick and goes away with the scrolling, so «Выше» and «Ниже» are at hand while it is visible (T92). Scrolling with a single pointer, 2.5.1, in a low window is reliable only in an element with a height: its row does not go away. So `app`, which needs the buttons always, gives the element a height by the window (section 3.3, "What `app` does"), and this is not declared as a nonconformance (T92, «Открыто»);
- in a right-to-left row — the element takes its direction from the page, `:dir(rtl)` — the arrows go by the DOM: in the pair «Левее» and «Правее», `ArrowLeft` leads to «Левее» through «Правее» (T94). `app` never has this: its languages, `en` and `ru`, are written left to right (R92 spec). A right-to-left page of another consumer — a module preview or a card of the MCP channel with a module in such a language — will get this order;
- the samples page is `canvas`'s build for adult volunteers, not a screen of `app`, and its page «Доступность» does not hold it. Handwriting there is the sample itself: the strokes with time and pressure are what is input, and this is the exception of 2.1.1: "Free hand drawing, or watercolor painting require path dependent input" (Understanding 2.1.1). axe goes through the page's own screens without violations (T90; R70).

**Not checked:**
- the keyboard in the sheet's region in Safari: only by WebKit bug 277290 and R90 spec; there is no WebKit in the image (T93; section 3.7). It can be taken in iPad Simulator, like `main`'s emulators (R54, R65);
- VoiceOver and TalkBack on a sheet, a workbook and an example: on tablets, the debt Д7 ([DEBT.md](../DEBT.md), table 4);
- Firefox: the element's tests run only in Chromium (section 3.7).

**A new nonconformance** that a task or a review finds goes here — with the criterion, what stands instead and the term — and as a row «в реестр spec» for `app`'s page. One that goes away leaves here the same way.
