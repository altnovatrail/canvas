## 1. Purpose and bounds

**Does:**
- gives the canvas kinds. Each kind is the platform's code, shared by all modules, and a module chooses the kind for each task and sets its settings. `canvas` has both kinds of the first slice: the handwriting sheet — for olympiad math and short essays in English —, and the workbook — for school math of grade 3, whose page, answer slots, slot's crop and template layer are `canvas`'s own (below, under «The workbook»). There is no text canvas in the first slice, and `canvas` does not build it. The other kinds — the sandbox and the creative canvas — are not in the first slice. The code canvas is open question О-20;
- puts the canvas together from layers, bottom to top: the author's template, the module's widget, the author's demo, the learner's work, the tutor's layer. The tutor's sketch is drawn under the learner's writing, the frames and the marks above it. On the sheet's template is the task's grid, on the workbook's template its item lines with boxes, and the task statement stands above the sheet or the workbook page, in the element itself;
- gives the tools the task allows: pen, eraser, ruler, protractor, calculator, keyboard, microphone. The eraser removes a stroke whole: the tutor's marks are tied to the learner's strokes, and an erased half of a stroke would break them. There is no microphone in the first slice: voice input is not in it. The learner's voice is not stored and lives only in the memory of the request, and children's voice input waits for the author's decision on Claude's analysis (П-5; the recommendation of О-6 — for adults from the beta);
- captures the learner's strokes with time and pressure;
- shows the lesson's worked example — the segment `worked_example` — on a sheet the learner does not write on: the statement above the sheet, and the parts of the author's demo the learner opens one at a time and can hide the whole demo. Paragraphs and pictures beside the sheet stand as a list above the sheet, and pictures in sheet areas on the sheet itself, on the demo layer (below, under «The task's fields, filled answer slots and the worked example»). The lesson and the player of its segments are `app`;
- shows the module's content — the statement, formulas, media files — by the task's open view, that is, without the key, the solution and the traps. The statement is text and pictures, and the tasks do not need formulas yet;
- draws the crop of the work for the check — by A3, on a sheet of 1000 × 1400 logical units: it is the crop that goes to the check, not the strokes (below, under «The sheet's logical coordinates»). On the workbook, a crop of each filled box by its strokes, and the draft outside the boxes does not go to the check;
- shows the tutor's marks on the tutor's layer, tied to the learner's strokes. On the workbook a mark stands on an answer slot and holds on to the strokes of its box. The marks do not change or cover the learner's work;
- is built as a separate build — the samples page for eval:
  - a sample is made with the same canvas as the learners have, and is saved as a file on the device;
  - an adult's sample is for a measurement, and then it goes to the measurement's organizer, outside git, or for a module's eval set, and then it is a pull request to `modules`. The purpose is chosen on the page, and the page takes no consent: the written consent is a signed form outside it (below, under «The samples page's steps»);
  - a child's sample is with a separate consent and without a pull request: it does not get into the repositories, and its form, where the file goes and who has access to it are decided in `spec`;
  - nothing goes to the platform's server meanwhile;
  - for the first measurement, without hosting: the build is downloaded from the release of `canvas` and opened on the device, and the tasks and media files are put into it at build time, from files of NovaTrail releases (`.claude/rules/tasks.md`). While it runs the page goes to no network.

  Samples are needed for the measurement of AI reading at stage 2 — including samples where adults imitate children's handwriting;
- from stage 4, the widget host: the application's side of the widget protocol. The widget itself runs in an iframe sandbox without `allow-same-origin`, on a separate registrable domain, and talks to the application only by the protocol from `spec`.

**Does not:**
- knows no accounts, no `api` and no subjects. It calls no one over the network: the picture of the work is sent to `api` by `app`;
- does not change the learner's work: the tutor's layer does not touch or cover the writing. The tutor's act in grown-up mode on a task from paper does, as the grown-up asks, and only by `drawTutor`, which `app` calls (below, under «The drawing engine and the layers»);
- does not store the strokes or send them to the server. The strokes with time and pressure are on the device, and what is stored there and how much is decided by `app`;
- does not read the work and does not give a verdict: the work is read by AI through `tutor`, the verdict is given by `module-kit`;
- does not receive the key, the solution and the traps of an unsolved task: it is given only the open view;
- runs no foreign code in the page: the code of the modules' widgets runs only in an iframe sandbox, and in the first slice there are no widgets at all;
- the samples page does not take learners' work into `modules`: it is not part of the modules, and eval takes it only with a separate consent and keeps it outside the repositories. So the page makes a child's sample without a pull request.

**Data and links:**
- it has no data of its own. The learner's strokes and artifacts are on the device — `app` holds them. An artifact is work that is a result in itself: a drawing, an essay, a recording. The work on a task with a key — strokes, answers, the submission context — is not an artifact, and the device erases it at the end of the attempt. The repository has no learners' data and no secrets: the strokes in tests and examples are only synthetic;
- the consumers are `app` and the samples page; later the module preview for the author and the card of the MCP channel. `app` builds the package in at an exact version, and during development uses its working copy. The second consumer — a site or the card of the MCP channel — gets a second build of the same package. And `tutor`'s CI takes synthetic samples from the release of `canvas` — by exact tag and sum (below, under «Publication and release»);
- at build time it depends on nothing but `spec`, and over the network it calls no one; `app` depends on it at build time. The CI of the samples page's build takes the page's tasks and text sets from files of NovaTrail releases by exact tag — the measurement's tasks from `tutor`'s file of open views (Д4 in [run/DEBT.md](../run/DEBT.md)), a module's from the release of `modules` by the CLI of `module-kit`, without the versions that the index of the newest release marked withdrawn, and the text sets from `spec`'s legal part —, each only once `spec`'s recipe `release-verify` has accepted its signature (`.claude/rules/tasks.md`). This is a link of the kind "CI tool", not a dependency at build time: so the tasks get into the build, and the package itself knows no subjects. The code takes `spec` from its releases, not from the submodule: the format's types as the npm package `@altnovatrail/spec` in `dependencies`, at the version of the submodule's tag, and the signature of each package of `@altnovatrail` in the lock file is checked in CI by `spec`'s recipe `release-verify-npm`. The module format is JSON Schema 2020-12, and the task's schema is `spec`'s. The element reads a manifest of the format version of that package alone. It takes the task, the manifest and the agreement with `app`, `@altnovatrail/spec/canvas`, by `spec`'s types, and its own are only the narrowed types of the task for the check and the temporary field `canvas.tools` (Д5 in [run/DEBT.md](../run/DEBT.md)). The task's open fields `givens` and `unknown` the element takes and does not show: `app` shows them;
- the package docks with the application as the web component `<novatrail-canvas>`, with the data types from `spec`'s releases (below, under «Docking with the app»), and comes out on npm, while the build of the samples page and the synthetic samples are files of its GitHub release (below, under «Publication and release»);
- adults' samples from the samples page go to the measurement's organizer or, for an eval set, through a pull request to `modules`, and children's samples without one, as `spec` decides. The sample file is the eval protocol's, built below under «The sample file»; the volunteers' consent is decided in `spec`, and the page's steps are below, under «The samples page's steps»;
- the license is Apache-2.0, and the dependencies only with licenses compatible with it;
- stage 2: with `tutor` it is the stage's first code. The order of the repositories' work is `spec`'s cross-repository plan, COORDINATION.md, and this repository's work is [RUN.md](../RUN.md).

Decided:

### 2.5. Input on the text canvas — as typed

Not decided: `canvas` builds no text canvas, and the first slice has none. The point concerns any typed answer, the answer slots with keyboard input that `app` shows too.
- **Gist:** the browser and the device's keyboard change what was typed by themselves — they correct words, put capital letters, suggest continuations and mark errors. Then the check kind "text" or "edit" would compare with the key the device's correction, not the learner's answer — a stress mark that autocorrect put, where the module counts stress marks —, and a marked error is a hint before the check, the "red pen" the platform does without (sources 1.5).
- **What is known:** `autocorrect="off"`, `spellcheck="false"` and `writingsuggestions="false"` ask the browser not to correct, check or suggest, and `autocapitalize` the browser or the keyboard "may choose to ignore or override": the attributes are a request, not a guarantee, and how the keyboards of iPad and Android behave only a device shows (sources 3.5). Turning them off has a price in accessibility: autocorrect and suggestions help a learner with motor impairments or dyslexia type. Normalization — case, spaces, punctuation, stress marks — is a parameter of the check kind, which the module sets: the canvas has nothing to normalize.
- **Options:** turn everything off — `autocorrect="off"`, `autocapitalize="none"`, `spellcheck="false"`, `writingsuggestions="false"` — and check the keyboards on devices; leave what the device has; let the module decide for each task.
- **Recommendation:** turn everything off in the answer field and check on devices: no check needs autocorrect, so the module needs no setting. What replaces the typing help for whoever needs it, and what to do with a keyboard that does not obey the attributes — an on-screen keyboard of the canvas's own, say —, are decided with `app`'s accessibility.

### 2.6. Scrolling the sheet with one pointer

Decided: no button scrolls the sheet. The hand drags it with any pointer, one finger in pen mode and two fingers always, which meets 2.5.1 of [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/), and 2.5.7 is a declared nonconformance (sources 9.10; below, under «Accessibility»); the wheel and the keys scroll the element's container, a region with `tabindex="0"` while its content overflows it. Built: below, under «The drawing engine and the layers» and «Docking with the app».

### 2.8. The task's fields, filled slots and the example's demo

Decided: the element takes the task's open fields `givens` and `unknown` and does not show them, `app` does; it gives `filled` on the sheet as on the workbook; a lesson's worked example comes by the property `workedExample`, on a sheet the learner does not write on, and the element opens its demo part by part with its own buttons, telling `app` by `demoState` and `demochange`. Built: below, under «The task's fields, filled answer slots and the worked example».

### 3.1. The drawing engine and the layers

The drawing engine is our own code on the browser's API. Of others' code it holds two packages, at the versions of `package.json` and its lock file: perfect-freehand, MIT, with no dependencies, for a stroke's outline on the screen, which does not go into the crop; and rbush, MIT, with `quickselect`, ISC, ESM only, for the index of strokes of the eraser, the groups and the crop's bundles — the snapping of marks needs no index, a pass over the submission's strokes. Its types, `@types/rbush`, MIT, are for development only. The sources are the READMEs of [perfect-freehand 1.2.3](https://github.com/steveruizok/perfect-freehand/blob/f56f097e0e211fffa1601b93883e4d9f9dccf122/packages/perfect-freehand/README.md) and [rbush 4.0.1](https://github.com/mourner/rbush/blob/c3f6ad06f1e47ccec58998461f3383aa90066ecd/README.md), sources 2.1, 2.7 and 3.1, [Pointer Events Level 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/REC-pointerevents3-20260630/) and measurements 4.1, and `ci-licenses` checks the licenses.

**The canvas's two parts:**
- **DOM-free**: plain TypeScript, the same in the browser and in Node: the strokes and the writing history, the sheet's clock, the stack of actions for undo, clearing and erasing, the index of strokes and the eraser, the stroke groups and their rectangles on the sheet, the snapping of the tutor's marks to strokes and their places, the crop with the group boxes in its fractions — its numbers in integers, its own raster and its own PNG encoder —, the check and restoring of an assigned writing history, the work for checking and the digest of its submission. The browser's drawing surface does not draw the crop: the HTML Standard does not fix the bytes of a PNG, in Node a drawing surface needs a native package with pixels of its own, and browsers add noise to what is read from a drawing surface — Brave by default, Firefox on the desktop in standard protection, Safari in Private Browsing and possibly in all browsing (sources 3.1; the READMEs of [Konva 10.9.0](https://github.com/konvajs/konva/blob/d95eed6d46e785efba6ab5e9eaf5a1d6bbb528c6/README.md), [Fabric.js 7.4.0](https://github.com/fabricjs/fabric.js/blob/v740/README.md) and [Paper.js 0.14.0](https://github.com/paperjs/paper.js/blob/v0.14.0/README.md); [`RFPTargetsDefaultBaseline.inc` of Firefox 158.0.2](https://github.com/mozilla-firefox/firefox/blob/cc4ebb2adcb7/toolkit/components/resistfingerprinting/RFPTargetsDefaultBaseline.inc)). Its tests run without a browser, and the same code draws the synthetic samples (below, under «The sample file»);
- **on the page**: the stack of layers and input. Each layer is an element of the page of its own; all are of one size, lie one over another and count in the same units of the sheet (below, under «The sheet's logical coordinates»).

**What draws each layer**, from the bottom up, in the order of spec's `docs/concepts.md`:

| Layer | Handwriting sheet and workbook |
|---|---|
| the author's template | on the sheet, the grid, SVG: squares or lines as a path, dots as the tiles of a pattern. On the workbook, the item lines, SVG: the numbers and the pieces of text `<text>`, fitted by `textLength` to the layout, and the boxes `<rect role="img">`; a filled box's border is 3 CSS px. The statement is not on the canvas but above it: HTML, text and pictures |
| the module's widget | its own place in the stack: empty until stage 4, from stage 4 an iframe sandbox |
| the demo | SVG: the example's pictures in sheet areas, an `<image>` stretched over its area, and of those laid over one another, the one opened later on top. The demo's parts beside the sheet stand above it, as a list under the statement (below, under «Docking with the app») |
| the tutor's layer: the sketch "how to begin", under the learner's work | SVG; empty |
| the learner's work | Canvas 2D: a stroke is a perfect-freehand outline by its points with pressure, in its style; the eraser finds a stroke by the index and removes it whole or cuts it into pieces |
| the tutor's layer: boxes and marks, over the learner's work | SVG: the box of each mark around its strokes. A mark has `role="img"` and `aria-label`, and the layer `role="none"` |

**A task from paper** has its crop as tracing paper: an SVG layer between the demo's and the learner's work, in sheet units, multiplied over a sheet of the learner's grid — dots as it comes, lines, blank or squares, which the bar's last button turns —, with a grip and its strokes, `on: "tracing"`, from its corner, in its own units by its scale. A press begun on the shown paper writes and erases on it alone. Chosen by its grip, it shows a frame with four corners that scale it and a bar under it — Smaller, the percent, Larger, Fit and Recognise —, and Ctrl or ⌘ with the wheel scales it too; the statement's text the consumer read by Recognise lies over its photo on a layer of its own, its fractions and powers laid out by the browser's MathML, under the ink, with a sign above it that says which it shows. The verdict marks of its tasks lie on the marks' layer, by the learner's strokes nearest the boxes on the crop of the work, or by the boxes, and so does the wavy line of "Show where", under the strokes its box takes, or under the box.

The tutor's layer is one layer in two elements: the sketch lies under the learner's work, the boxes and marks over it. Neither intercepts the pen's input (`pointer-events: none`) or covers the learner's work. The statement, the tools and the marks are HTML and SVG elements with accessible names, which a screen reader reads, while the content of the drawing surface is not visible to it (sources 3.1). So the drawing surface holds only ink. A screen reader finds the statement's paragraphs and pictures with their descriptions, the workbook's items as a list, each with its number, text and boxes, a mark by its name, the bar's buttons by roles and names, and the example's parts in order, a picture in a sheet area by its description in its item. It does not see the grid, the drawing surface, the sketch or the demo layer, which are `aria-hidden`. What the tests check of it is below, under «Accessibility».

**The scene** is the visible part of the sheet, `position: sticky` in its native scrolling. It holds all the layers, of one size and `position: absolute`, in the order of the table:
- the SVGs show the visible part by their `viewBox`, `vx vy W/k H/k`, where W × H is the visible part on the screen, and `vx` its left edge, while the sheet is scrolled sideways. One layout sets it, in the same frame in which the ink is drawn, on scrolling and on a new size, so the layers do not drift apart: a layer that scrolled by itself would run ahead of the ink while scrolling (sources 9.4);
- the HTML layer, the widget's place, is empty and does not move. HTML on the sheet, when it comes, is a block of the page, 1000 × 1400 units, with the matrix (k, 0, 0, k, −vx·k, −vy·k) in the same layout. The demo layer is SVG and moves its pictures by its `viewBox`, like the other SVGs;
- only the drawing surface takes input. The other layers have `pointer-events: none` and `aria-hidden`, but the layer of marks and the workbook's template have `role="none"` instead of `aria-hidden`: the marks are read by their names, and the workbook's lines as a list. No `pointer-events` is set on the drawing surface and its ancestors, so the consumer's `none` on the element reaches it. The one exception is the sheet of a worked example: a rule on the sheet's class gives its drawing surface `pointer-events: none`, and without the class it inherits the consumer's `none` again. A layer that becomes active drops its `none` but gets no `auto`. Above the scene, outside the scrolling, stands the element's bar of buttons, which takes the pointer as the drawing surface does: it has no `pointer-events` of its own.

**Input** is Pointer Events: `pointerType` tells the pen, the finger and the mouse apart, `pressure` gives the pressure, and the intermediate points come from `getCoalescedEvents`, where it exists ([browser-compat-data v8.1.6](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/v8.1.6/api/PointerEvent.json)):
- the drawing surface of the learner's work has `touch-action: none` and pointer capture: a stroke goes on beyond the edge of the drawing surface too, pressed to the edge of the sheet. Every pointer that came down on the drawing surface it holds by capture until lift-off, whether that pointer writes or not;
- one pointer writes at a time, with the primary button — the pen's touch, a finger, the left mouse button —, and with `tool = "eraser"` it erases. The pen's eraser erases with any `tool`, if the task allows the eraser. The pen's button and the right and middle mouse buttons neither write nor erase;
- which pointer writes the touch rule decides: the pen removes the stroke of a touch in progress and writes itself, and while the pen or the mouse is writing, touches do nothing. A pen that comes down while another pen is down ends that one's stroke or erasing where it was, as a loss of capture does, and writes: a screen has one pen, and on an iPad the system's selection took a pen's touch and the page heard nothing of its lift (measurements 1.8);
- with `tool = "hand"` a press of any pointer, by any button, moves the sheet and writes nothing, the first pointer alone, and the touch rule is not asked: a pen there does not turn the pen mode on. The canvas shows `cursor: grab`, `grabbing` while it drags;
- a stroke ends on `pointerup`, on a move without the primary button, on a loss of capture, when a pen comes down while it is down and when the element leaves the page;
- `pointercancel` cancels the stroke: this is how the device reports a touch that it judged accidental;
- on the sheet of a worked example the learner does not write, and the drawing surface takes no input: `pointer-events: none` and `touch-action: auto`. A press lands in the scene, where a finger and the pen scroll the container or the page by native scrolling, and a pinch zooms, as over the statement. Such a sheet turns the pen mode neither on nor off, the handler drops at once an event that a script sends straight to the drawing surface, and whatever the hands held when the example came — a palm, two fingers — goes;
- a point closer than 0.1 unit to the last one recorded is not written, while the place and time of lift-off always are. The time is by the sheet's clock, shared by strokes and actions, the pressure as it came, and for the last point that of the previous move: on `pointerup` it is 0.

**The palm rejection**, as in MathTrail's canvas-api ([canvas-api@0901af0, `docs/spec.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/spec.md)):
- after the pen's first touch on any sheet of the page, the pen mode, one for the whole page until it reloads, which nothing turns off. In it a finger neither writes nor erases, and it moves the sheet (below);
- a touch wider or taller than 100 CSS px is a palm, on `pointerdown` and on any coalesced `pointermove`. It does nothing until lift-off, and the stroke it was writing is removed. canvas-api's threshold is 30 px, but on iPadOS the width of a touch is twice the radius, and a finger is wider there ([canvas-api@0901af0, `docs/spec.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/spec.md); WebKit's [`PointerEventIOS.cpp`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/dfaf47f/Source/WebCore/dom/ios/PointerEventIOS.cpp) and [`NativeWebTouchEventIOS.mm`](https://github.com/WebKit/WebKit/blob/dfaf47f/Source/WebKit/Shared/ios/NativeWebTouchEventIOS.mm) at dfaf47f; sources 9.10). Where the hardware does not measure a touch, `width` and `height` equal 1, and only the pen mode holds the palm ([Pointer Events Level 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/REC-pointerevents3-20260630/)).

**The element's scroll container** is `.viewport`, which holds the statement, the demo's parts and the sheet:
- while its content overflows it — vertically, `scrollHeight − clientHeight > 0,5` px, or sideways, the sheet wider than the container —, it is a region: `role="region"`, `tabindex="0"` and the name of the canvas kind, «Лист», «Тетрадь» or «Пример», in the language of the labels. The bar is then named by the kind's buttons, «Кнопки листа» and so on;
- Tab enters it after the bar and leaves it for what follows the element, and the arrows, Page Up and Page Down, Home, End and Space scroll it, as the browser scrolls a focused container; it has no `keydown` of its own. Without an overflow — in an element with no height or with a task that fitted — there is no role, no name and no Tab stop, and a focused container keeps them until `blur`;
- the focus ring — 2 px of the ink's color inside the edge, Highlight in forced colors — is on the sibling `.ring` in the wrapper `.frame`, over the sheet: the paper and the scene would cover the `outline` of the container itself.

**The fingers**, and the hand, move the sheet on the drawing surface by our own code: `touch-action` acts on the pen too, and native scrolling cannot be given to the finger while writing is given to the pen ([Pointer Events Level 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/REC-pointerevents3-20260630/); sources 9.10):
- without the pen mode, a second finger that touched no later than 150 ms after the first, while the first had moved no more than 8 px, removes the first one's stroke, and both scroll; a second finger later than that does nothing;
- in the pen mode, two fingers that touched no more than 150 ms apart scroll, and one finger that has gone more than 8 px before a second came drags the sheet alone, by the same rules; a second finger then does nothing, nor one that came down earlier and waited. No finger drags while a sheet of the page is written on, and a pen never drags;
- a third finger does nothing. The fingers scroll the sheet sideways too, once it is wider than the container, and a gesture that at the threshold has gone twice as far along one axis as along the other goes on to its end along that axis only.

The thresholds are to be checked on devices. In the emulators the element's styles forbid selecting its text and the menu of a long press, `pointercancel` removes a stroke, two fingers scroll and do not write, and the bar's eraser and undo work — on Android with Chrome's touches, on the iPad with the page's events; the thresholds themselves, the palm, the pressure and the pen wait for hardware (Д7). The canvas does not work around WebKit bug 269535: on the iPad the pen is silent while a finger or the edge of a palm touches the screen.

**The ink** is a perfect-freehand outline, with every parameter set explicitly: a line of 3 units of the sheet, as on the board, with round ends. Only the pen's pressure changes the width — from half the line at no pressure to twice it at full, `0.5 + 1.5 × p^0.8` of it, each point's pressure eased toward the one before it —, and for the mouse and the finger the line is even. Apple Pencil in WebKit gives writing a pressure of about 0–0.2 (measurements 1.8), where the power below 1 widens the line more than a straight width would; no tool of its own, a brush, stands beside the pen for it. There is one drawing surface, over the visible part of the sheet and in device pixels; the path of a finished stroke is built once, and anew on each frame only for the stroke being written. While the view stays, the drawing surface keeps the finished strokes, and a frame paints only the rectangle where something changed: while a stroke is written, the rectangles of its last frame and this one come back from a buffer of the finished ink under it, and the stroke is filled there; a stroke that lands has its rectangle painted anew. A scroll, a new size or scale, an undo and the like paint the strokes in sight anew, and so does each frame while an eraser is down (measurements 1.17). For `app`'s debug view, `paintStats` reads what the drawing holds and costs, and `paintFlash` shows each rectangle a frame paints in red on a canvas of its own. Strokes outside the visible part are not drawn, and a stroke whose points are far beyond it is not even traced until it comes into it: an assigned history may hold thousands of strokes. On a new size of the drawing surface the ink comes back in the same frame. No text of the element is selected and no long press opens a menu over it — the bar, the statement, the parts and the sheet, the example's sheet too: `user-select: none` and `-webkit-touch-callout: none` on the host, repeated on the workbook's print, which `all: initial` takes them off, and `selectstart` stopped in the shadow tree. A pen's `touchstart` and `touchmove` on the drawing surface are cancelled, a finger's are left as they are: on an iPad a pen's double tap or long press, the way a dot or an equals sign is written, selected the nearest text — by the bar, where a button's label stands out of sight — and opened the system's menu of the selection over the sheet (measurements 1.8). Screen readers read the labels and names as before: the selection is no part of what they read. The pressure does not get into the crop for checking: its line is of one width, as in MathTrail's canvas-api ([canvas-api@0901af0, `docs/spec.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/spec.md)).

**The pen's settings**, as in Apple's Notes, by spec's mockup of them: every stroke the element writes has spec's `style` — its kind of pen, colour, width and opacity —, and the settings are the property `pens` (below, under «Docking with the app»). A second press of the chosen pen opens its popover beside the bar — «Ручка», «Линер» or «Маркер», five widths, the opacity, the palette's five colours and «Другие цвета», each kind its own —, and a second press of the chosen eraser its own — «Штрих целиком» or «Частично», five sizes; in an element narrower than 600 CSS px a popover is a sheet along its bottom. A press on the sheet closes it and writes. «Ручка» widens with a pen's pressure by the curve above, and under a finger or a mouse with the speed of its points, which goes into no `p`, its ends a little finer; «Линер» is even; «Маркер», four times as wide, multiplies the ink under it. A stroke is filled once, in its colour at its opacity: a translucent one is no darker where it crosses itself. The palette's black follows the theme's ink, and in forced colours every stroke is the ink colour. A pen's hover with no button held shows a ring of its width and colour, or of the eraser's circle. The attribute `unstyled`, the samples page's, writes strokes with no style and opens no popover. The steps, the palette and the defaults are `src/core/style.ts`.

**Undo and clearing** are the work on the sheet, in the DOM-free part: the strokes that are visible, in the order in which they were laid, and the stack of actions. An action is a written stroke, an erasing, a cut, a clearing or the tutor's act. Undo removes the last one: for an erasing it returns the erased strokes to their former places, for a cut it takes its pieces off and returns the strokes it cut, for a clearing its strokes, and for the tutor's act its strokes go and those it took off return; there is no redo. Undo and clearing do not touch the stroke being written: it will lie on the sheet when it ends. The element's commands are under «Docking with the app».

**The eraser** erases along the path of a press, whole strokes or, chosen in its popover, a part of them:
- its size is a diameter in units, of the pen's settings. Taking whole strokes, a press takes every stroke whose line comes no farther from the path than the reach: the eraser's radius, but no less than 10 CSS px on the screen, whatever `k` at the moment of the press, plus half the stroke's widest line — 3 units for a stroke with no style. A tap is a path of one point;
- taking a part, it cuts each stroke it crosses where its radius, widened by half the stroke's widest line, crosses the line of the stroke's points, computed exactly by the circles and strips of its path: each piece a new stroke, spec's `Piece`, a run of the stroke's points with at most one point of its own at each end, at the place, time and pressure between its neighbours, and a stretch under 0.1 unit no piece. While it is down the strokes it crossed show as their pieces; on the lift the pieces take the next IDs, and the cut is one action. A piece takes its stroke's place on the sheet, and one of the tracing paper's stroke is the paper's;
- outside the visible part of the sheet — where a captured pointer goes — the eraser takes nothing, and the path goes on from its last place on the screen;
- the rbush index finds strokes by the bounding boxes of their raw points, and our own code computes the exact distance from a segment of the path to the segments of a stroke. The index is built anew when the sheet's array of strokes has changed, and only when it is queried;
- the strokes under the eraser vanish from the screen at once, and leave the sheet when it is lifted, as one action and one event `historychange`. `pointercancel` returns them, and a press with no hits is not an action;
- on the lift, what goes of the strokes taken is what is on the sheet then: what undo or a clearing removed during the press and did not return is no longer there to erase.

**The tutor's act** in grown-up mode, on a task from paper alone: `cropStrokes` gives the strokes whose ink reaches into the crop, in the sheet's order, the tracing paper's if shown, where it lay, in the crop's pixels by its `sheetBox` and size, thinned to 128 points at most; `drawTutor` lays the act back by the same numbers, onto the tracing paper from its corner when the context's paper was shown and holds the whole act, else onto the sheet, as liners of the pointer "tutor", one action, the event "tutor". Its strokes are drawn in turn along their length, 0.45 units a ms, 90 ms at least, 40 ms apart, shortened alike to end within 3 s; a frame paints only the rectangles of the parts that grew. An undo, a clearing, an eraser, a new history, a scroll or another act paint the rest at once; reduced motion, all of it.

**The writing history** is events in the order in which the actions were laid: "stroke", the time it was laid and the stroke itself with its points and its style; "erasing", the time of the lift and the IDs of the erased strokes; "cut", the time of the lift, the IDs of the strokes it crossed and the pieces it left; "clearing", the time and the IDs of everything that was on the sheet; "tutor", the time, the tutor's strokes and the IDs it took off; "undo", the time, the IDs it removed and the IDs it returned. The time at which an action was laid does not decrease along the history, and folding the history gives the sheet without the rules of the stack. The sheet's clock is milliseconds from the first call to it: the recording of input takes the points' time from it, and the work on the sheet the actions' time, only when an action changed something. The page's time — the `timeStamp` of events and `performance.now()` for the methods — is counted from one origin (sources 9.3). The history goes out by the property `history` and the event `historychange`, and comes in by the same property: another history replaces the sheet, and the clock runs from its last event + 2001 ms, more than the groups' pause (below, under «Docking with the app»).

**The widget layer from stage 4.** The iframe sandbox takes its place in the stack. The chosen tool distributes input: the widget's tool removes `pointer-events` from the layers above the widget, and touches reach the iframe, while the pen and the eraser take input on the layer of the learner's work. The iframe has its own document: the widget receives the input in it, and the page's `touch-action` is not inherited there — the widget sets its own ([Pointer Events Level 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/REC-pointerevents3-20260630/); sources 3.1). The widget tells of its state only by the protocol from `spec` (Т-24), and what the protocol has still to decide is named by the card of module widgets in spec's `docs/architecture.md`. Its side in the app is `canvas`'s widget host, as canvas's card there and «Purpose and bounds» above say, and for it this is the input:
- **the channel** is a `MessageChannel` port: the host passes it to the widget once, on the iframe's first load, and from then on listens only to it. The widget's origin without `allow-same-origin` is opaque, and the iframe's window is one for all its documents, so neither by origin nor by `event.source` can a document that the widget later navigated to be told apart. How the host notices such a navigation the protocol decides;
- **the state's version** in each message of the widget. A mark on a part of the widget that has changed since then the host does not draw — just as a mark of the sheet whose strokes are erased (under «The sheet's logical coordinates»);
- **the widget's parts.** The tutor's layer lies over the widget, so the widget names its parts and their boxes, and a mark holds to a part — as on the sheet to strokes. The parts' boxes are converted to the screen by the same conversion as all the layers (under «The sheet's logical coordinates»); in what units the widget reports them the protocol decides;
- **the state, not a verdict.** The widget reports the state and the learner's events, and whether it is correct `module-kit` decides; by which state — the reported one or one repeated on the server — Т-24 decides. Neither the host nor the canvas gives a verdict;
- **the conformance tests** are in `spec`, beside the protocol, and the test host in the development environment `widget-template`, which spec's `docs/architecture.md` lists among the later components. Both sides pass them, each in its own CI, and "`app`" in the card of module widgets there is this host.

Whether a version of the widget is withdrawn the host does not learn by itself: it knows neither `api` nor the index, and over the network it calls no one. `app`, which gives the canvas the task, settles it first.

**Not built:** the sketch "how to begin", marks other than the box, tools other than the pen, the eraser, Select and the hand, and the widget host; there is no text canvas. The engine has no scene graph: revisit this choice when tools that move and rotate come — a ruler and a protractor — or the creative canvas, which may need one; the DOM-free part does not change from it.

### 3.2. The sheet's logical coordinates

The handwriting sheet counts in logical units, as canvas-api does: they do not depend on the screen. The sources are MathTrail's canvas-api, [`docs/spec.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/spec.md) and [`docs/decisions.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/decisions.md) at 0901af0, the research of sources 1.4, `tutor`'s format of a sample, [tutor@231bf8b](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/tree/231bf8b), and its marks, [tutor@0f624a3](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/tree/0f624a31d1a01cf02af6fa28816383e9bc3b28aa), and the AI providers' limits of a picture, sources 9.6.

**The page** is W × H = 1000 × 1400 units, `SHEET` of `src/core/sheet.ts`, at the sheet's top left corner, the origin:
- x grows to the right, y downward, one scale on both axes;
- the size is a constant of the canvas kind, not a setting of the module. The task's zones, the workbook's lines and boxes and a worked example's areas lie on it, and `exportPicture()` draws it, with the ink past it;
- the sheet goes on past it, right and down, as far as its layout reaches — the page, the ink and the tracing paper, and one view of the window more across and two down, at the window's own fit and the zoom —, within `REACH`, 10000 × 14000 units, the bound of a history's points and of every box (`extentOf`); a worked example's sheet is its page alone.

In these units:
- the points of strokes, as fractional numbers, as they came: only the recording of a sample rounds them, to 0.1 unit (below, under «The sample file»). A point outside the reach input presses to its edge, as in canvas-api ([canvas-api@0901af0, `docs/spec.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/spec.md));
- the grid: its kind — squares, lines or dots — and its step are set by the task, and the default step is 50 units, like canvas-api's square; a task from paper's is the learner's. It rules the whole extent;
- the zone of an answer slot, a rectangle in the sheet's units: `left` and `top` from the left and top edges, `width` and `height`;
- all the layers of the stack (above, under «The drawing engine and the layers»).

**Who counts in what:**

| What | In what units | Who converts |
|---|---|---|
| strokes, the grid, a slot's zone, the area of a demo picture, the layers | the sheet's units | — |
| the workbook's lines and boxes | the page's units, 1000 × 1400, as for the sheet, integers | — |
| the sheet on the screen | CSS px: `k` per unit | `canvas` |
| the statement above the sheet | CSS px, without `k` | — |
| the crop | the crop's px: `s` per unit; its place on the sheet is `sheet_box`, in the sheet's units | `canvas` |
| a group's rectangle | the sheet's units, `[ymin, xmin, ymax, xmax]` | — |
| the group boxes and the place in the observation | fractions 0–1000 of the crop, `[ymin, xmin, ymax, xmax]`, integers | into fractions, `canvas` and `tutor`'s adapter; from fractions into the sheet, `canvas` |
| a mark of the sheet | `{stroke_ids, snapped_box}`, the box in the sheet's units | `canvas` |

**The screen** has one conversion for all the layers, as in canvas-api ([canvas-api@0901af0, `docs/spec.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/spec.md)). The page is fit to the width of its area — A CSS px —, but no wider than 1000 CSS px: on a wide screen it is a pixel a unit, at the sheet's left, and the sheet goes on to its right. There is no lower limit: there are no phones in the first slice. The page's zoom z, from 0.5 to 2, multiplies it: Zoom in and Zoom out on the bar, and a pinch of two fingers; the browser's zoom narrows A. What does not fit scrolls, down and across; `vx` and `vy` are the left edge and the top of the visible part of the sheet, in units:

```
k     = min(A / W, 1) × z
x_css = (x − vx) × k             y_css = (y − vy) × k
```

- the limit is the layout's: `fitScale` in `screen.ts`. The `k` for input, for the eraser's reach and for drawing is by the width of the sheet on the screen, `scaleFor`, without the limit: in an ancestor with a `transform` the sheet on the screen can be wider than 1000 px too;
- the drawing surface of the learner's work is only the visible part, on both axes, and draws in device pixels: the scale is `k × devicePixelRatio`, the offset `−vx × k × devicePixelRatio` and `−vy × k × devicePixelRatio`. Its buffer is no larger than the container, however far the sheet goes;
- for the SVG of the template — the grid or the workbook's lines —, of the demo layer and of the tutor's layer, `viewBox` sets the visible part of the sheet, `vx vy W/k H/k`, and the browser converts by itself. A workbook item that `scrollIntoView` or a screen reader scrolls to comes into the visible part two frames later: the container scrolls at once, and the next layout sets the new `viewBox`. The item is an `<svg>` of its place and size, its parts placed from its origin: Firefox brings an SVG group as a point at its origin, and a box with a size whole (measurements 1.6);
- a workbook box holds a stroke if the middle of the bounding box of its points is in the box widened by 12 units, and that bounding box itself is no bigger than 160 × 160. The rule counts in the page's units, in whole tenths, for each stroke separately, and does not know the screen;
- HTML on the sheet, when it comes, takes the same `k`, as a block of the page, 1000 × 1400 units, with the matrix (k, 0, 0, k, −vx·k, −vy·k). The statement stands above the sheet and does not take `k`;
- input is converted back: `x = (clientX − r.left) / k + vx`, `y = (clientY − r.top) / k + vy`, where `r` is the rectangle of the visible part of the sheet, `getBoundingClientRect()`.

**The scrolling.** The offsets `vx` and `vy` are the same for all the layers. The sheet scrolls by the native scrolling of a container in the element's shadow root — by the wheel, the scroll bar and the keyboard, as everywhere on the page:
- the scene with all the layers stands in the visible part of the container (`position: sticky`) — above, under «The drawing engine and the layers» —, and `vy` is the drawing surface's offset from the top of the sheet, divided by `k`;
- above the sheet, in the same scrolling, is the task's statement, and over the container stands the element's bar of buttons (below, under «Docking with the app»). By default the element is as tall as the statement and the sheet, and the sheet is seen whole. If the consumer gave the element a smaller height, the statement and the sheet scroll in it;
- while the statement is visible, the drawing surface goes below the visible part of the container, and the visible part of the sheet is the drawing surface clipped by it. The eraser takes strokes in it;
- on a new width and on a new height of the statement the top left corner of the visible part, `vx` and `vy`, stays at its place on the sheet, unless the sheet's top, or its left, is in sight, and on a new height of the container itself the element does not touch the scrolling: a scrolling in progress is not cut off;
- the container scrolls sideways too, left to right whatever the page's direction, and the scene sticks to its left edge too. The statement and the example's parts are as wide as the page, `min(A, 1000)`, at the left edge of the container, in the element's own direction: they do not move sideways. The column's padding is the consumer's `--novatrail-canvas-pad-top` and `--novatrail-canvas-pad-bottom`: the statement starts below its bars, and the sheet scrolls up past them;
- with one finger the sheet on the drawing surface does not scroll by the browser, nor does the page over it: the drawing surface covers the visible part, and it has `touch-action: none`. One finger scrolls them on the statement above the sheet, by native scrolling; in the pen mode one finger on the drawing surface drags the sheet by our own code, and so does any pointer with the hand;
- on the drawing surface two fingers scroll the sheet, by our own code. The sheet moves with the midpoint of the fingers, after the first 8 px and without a jump. What to scroll the gesture's first step chooses, and the choice does not change until the gesture's end: the container, if it can move in that direction, otherwise the element's nearest scrollable ancestor — across shadow boundaries —, otherwise the page, if its overflow is not hidden. The movement is divided by the container's scale, and there is no inertia;
- one finger in the pen mode scrolls the same, by its own travel instead of the fingers' midpoint, and so does the hand's pointer, from its first move.

**The crop** is drawn by A3, from the visible strokes only: without the grid, the statement and the tutor's layer ([canvas-api@0901af0, `docs/spec.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/spec.md); H3). In the formulas:
- D is the digit height in px: a canvas setting of the module in its manifest, `canvas.digit_height`, an integer from 1 to 256, by default 24, and 24 without a manifest too. The element takes it from the property `manifest` (below, under «Docking with the app»);
- B is the common rectangle of the visible points. All places are in tenths of a unit, `round(10 × v)`, as for the groups;
- `h_med` is the median height of the bundles:
  - a bundle is strokes no shorter than 15 units whose bounding boxes touch or overlap, by a chain and without time;
  - a shorter stroke is a dash: a dot, a minus, a fraction bar, an underline. It does not count and binds nothing;
  - the median is the lower one, the ⌊(n − 1) / 2⌋-th from the lowest; with no bundles, 15 units;
  - the scale takes it no higher than 100 units: H = min(`h_med`, 100);
- `m` is the margin, 12 px;
- D′ is the height at which the digits are drawn: the largest integer no greater than D at which the crop fits within the limit.

```
s         = D′ / H                              crop px per unit of the sheet
w₀        = ⌈(B.xmax − B.xmin) × s⌉ + 2m        h₀ likewise
w         = max(w₀, 200)                        the crop's width, px; h likewise
mx        = m + ⌊(w − w₀) / 2⌋                  the left margin, px; my the top one, likewise
limit:      max(w, h) ≤ 1568 and ⌈w / 28⌉ × ⌈h / 28⌉ ≤ 1568
sheet_box = [B.ymin − my/s, B.xmin − mx/s, B.ymin − my/s + h/s, B.xmin − mx/s + w/s]
```

`sheet_box` (`sb`) is where the crop, with its margins, lies on the sheet. The coordinates are converted by it:

```
sheet → crop:     X   = (x − sb.xmin) × s                Y = (y − sb.ymin) × s
group box:        fx₀ = ⌊1000 × (X₀ − r) / w⌋            fx₁ = ⌈1000 × (X₁ + r) / w⌉, y likewise
fraction → sheet: x   = sb.xmin + fx / 1000 × (sb.xmax − sb.xmin)
                  y   = sb.ymin + fy / 1000 × (sb.ymax − sb.ymin)
```

Here X₀ and X₁ are the edges of the group's points on the crop, and r is half the line and the pixel of its edge.
- **The line** on the crop is of one width, with no pressure ([canvas-api@0901af0, `docs/spec.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/spec.md)): for a stroke with a style, its width at the crop's scale, 1 px at least, the margin and a group's frame widened to hold the widest line; for one with no style, max(D′, 12) / 12 px: at 24 px, 2, at 32 px, 2.67.
- **A group box** is its ink together with the edge pixel that the raster still paints, rounded outward. Every pixel of the group's ink is inside the box, and min < max for a dot too.
- **The limit** is Claude's standard level: any model of Claude and of OpenAI at `high` takes such a picture without downscaling (sources 9.6).
  - A crop that does not fit is drawn with digits smaller than D, by whole px, and D′ says so.
  - A side shorter than 200 px gets a wider margin, and the scale stays the same.
- **An empty sheet** gives no crop: it is cut off before the AI call (H3).

The numbers are computed in integers. The tenths of the points, H, D′, the margins and the sizes are integers below 2^53, and `s`, `sheet_box` and the line are each one division of two integers. So `tutor`'s runner repeats them to the bit, as with the rule of groups. The formulas in integers are in the header of the test vectors (below, under «The sample file»).

**The crop of a workbook box** is the same rule over the strokes of one filled box, as one group of them, at the manifest's digit height. The draft goes into no crop. At the eval heights a box's crop is at the lower limit, 200 × 200 px, that is 64 tokens, and a page of 10 items with one box each is 640 tokens and about 11 KB of PNG. An empty box gives no crop.

**The raster and the PNG** are our own DOM-free code (above, under «The drawing engine and the layers»):
- the pixels are gray, white 255 and ink 0, when no stroke has a style; when one has, RGB on white, the strokes by their IDs, each laid as one layer at its opacity — a pen or a liner over what lies under it, a marker multiplied with it — and a stroke with no style black;
- a segment of a stroke is a line of one width with round ends, a stroke of one point is a dot. A pixel takes the share that the line covers, by a slope of one pixel at the edge;
- only +, −, ×, ÷ and `Math.sqrt` go into a pixel, and the bytes are the same in any JavaScript engine;
- the PNG is gray or RGB, as its pixels, 8 bits, one IDAT with a zlib stream of one fixed-Huffman block, with no ancillary chunks. A crop of the whole sheet weighs about 10 KB.

**The groups and their boxes.** The groups are built by space and time, as in `tutor` ([tutor@231bf8b, `docs/decisions.md`](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/blob/231bf8b/docs/decisions.md)):
- two strokes are neighbors if there are no more than 2 s between them, and the bounding boxes of their points come closer than 50 units horizontally and vertically, closer than a square of the default grid. The pause is from the end of the earlier stroke to the start of the later one, and for strokes that overlap in time it is 0;
- a group is strokes joined by a chain of neighbors. What is added later beside old writing is a group of its own, and lines written one after another do not merge: under a stroke of a line lies a stroke written a line later;
- the rule takes the visible strokes of the final sheet with the time of their points. It counts in integers — in tenths of a unit, `round(10 × v)`, and in milliseconds, `round(t)`, rounding a half up — and only by subtraction, so `tutor`'s runner repeats it to the bit;
- the group labels are A, B, C…, after Z AA, AB…, by place: by the top of the group's rectangle, then by the left edge;
- a group's rectangle is the common rectangle of the points of its strokes in the sheet's units, `[ymin, xmin, ymax, xmax]`. A group box is its ink in fractions of the crop, its own for each crop.

The table "label → stroke IDs" stays on the device and is valid only for its own submission (C2). The split by time is a rough sign of the process: on the research's scale A4 the group boxes are then closer to (b) than to (a) (Т-31). The crop's scale does not depend on the groups: `h_med` is the median of the bundles, and they are built without time. So the same final strokes give one crop, however they were written, and only the boxes and the labels depend on the order of writing.

**The place from the model:**
- `tutor`'s adapter converts its provider's convention into fractions of the crop that the device sent. The provider's downscaling of the picture and the margins with labels, if the server draws them (Т-31), it takes into account itself;
- `canvas` converts the fractions into the sheet's units by the `sheet_box` of its submission and snaps the box to the strokes of that submission whose points are mostly inside (C2 of sources 1.4; [canvas-api@0901af0, `docs/decisions.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/decisions.md)). The model did not see the strokes added after the submission, and they do not go into the mark. The rule:
  - the box is widened by a quarter of the digit height: D′/4 px on the crop, H/4 on the sheet, at D = 24, 6 px, and at 32, 8 px;
  - a stroke is taken if no fewer than two thirds of its points are in the box, and at least one;
  - it is computed in integers, on the crop itself, as the crop's raster is. A point in tenths stands on it at X = 1000 × ((px − B.xmin) × D′ + mx × H), and the box holds it if (xmin × w − μ × D′) × H ≤ X ≤ (xmax × w + μ × D′) × H, the same for y, with μ = 250 thousandths of D′. The edges are inclusive;
  - the box is first widened, then clipped by the sheet's bounds, and the clipping changes nothing: the points lie on the sheet;
  - the margin and the threshold are parameters of eval;
- no strokes inside, no box, and the question is asked without it ([canvas-api@0901af0, `docs/decisions.md`](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/decisions.md));
- `tutor` does not snap the box to strokes: the board draws it as it is, and the measurement counts it by the line of the first error: "the box with a margin of 8 px holds the most visible points of exactly this line" ([tutor@0f624a3, `docs/architecture.md`](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/blob/0f624a31d1a01cf02af6fa28816383e9bc3b28aa/docs/architecture.md)).

**The marks:**
- on the sheet a mark is `{stroke_ids, snapped_box}`. It is drawn by the current geometry of its strokes; new strokes do not move it, and when all its strokes are erased, it vanishes too. Only a sketch on an empty place needs `snapped_box` (C2);
- from `api` a mark comes with a group label, with an answer slot or with a box in fractions of the crop, not in the sheet's units;
- where a mark is `canvas` decides when the marks have come. All the strokes that the sheet knew go into the reckoning, the erased ones too, but only from the submission's table:
  - a group label: the strokes of its row of the table;
  - a box of the model: the snapping above;
  - an answer slot: by place, `{ item, slot }`, from 1. On the sheet it has no strokes: the sheet takes no zones. On the workbook, the strokes of the table's row of its box; an empty box, which is not in the table, the mark circles itself, without strokes, and a box not of the page gives no mark's box. A group label and a box of the model on the workbook are a `TypeError`.

  What is visible is checked at drawing. So a mark that came after a clearing comes back with an undo, together with its strokes;
- the mark's box is around the points of those of its strokes that are visible now and not under an eraser's press, 10 units from them and within the sheet. On the workbook, around the box and these points together. The line is 2 CSS px at any `k`. While new points of the stroke being written come into the box, it fades to a quarter, and when the stroke is laid it comes back.

**What does not leave the device** is `sheet_box`, the table of groups, the strokes and their time — for the check: `app` may send them within a stored work, while the storage of the profile's works is on, and the server gives none to the check. From the workbook only the crops of the filled boxes leave, with their places, item and box, and the table "box → stroke IDs" and the digest stay with `app`. How `canvas` gives them to the consumer together with the crop and takes them back with the marks is below, under «Docking with the app». This is the rule of checking, and a sample for eval has its own path and its own composition, below, under «The sample file».

**The sketch "how to begin" on an empty place** is not addressed by the crop: the crop covers only the work, and an empty sheet has none. Where it stands is decided together with it, with the first slice of `app` and `api`.

### 3.3. Docking with the app

The package enters its consumer as a web component, the element `<novatrail-canvas>`, with no framework. The sources are sources 3.3, [app@7a96676](https://github.com/altnovatrail/app/tree/7a96676) and [skill-map@0295ce8](https://github.com/altnovatrail/skill-map/tree/0295ce8).

**The element:**
- one for all the canvas kinds: it takes the kind from the task's open view. The module chooses the kind, and a new kind is the platform's code, so a new kind comes with a new version of `canvas`, with no change to `app`;
- the markup is in an open Shadow DOM: the page's styles do not touch the canvas's layers, nor the canvas's styles the page. The element sets its own styles by `adoptedStyleSheets` — one `CSSStyleSheet` per document, filled by `replaceSync()` —, not by a `<style>` element in the tree, and writes no `style` attribute in the markup: it works under a content security policy with no inline styles, as `app`'s is. An element moved into another document takes that document's sheet, `adoptedCallback()`;
- the element is registered on the import of its entry point, and only once. The element's name on a page is one, and `app` builds in one exact version of the package;
- the package has two entry points: `@altnovatrail/canvas`, which registers the element, and `@altnovatrail/canvas/core`, the DOM-free part of the drawing engine (above, under «The drawing engine and the layers»), imported in Node too, which registers none (below, under «Code and tools»).

**It takes** by properties, not by attributes. The properties are declared in the element's class, so React 19 and Preact pass their values to the element as properties (sources 3.3).
- `task` is the task's open view: `id`, the statement, the canvas and the items with answer slots, by spec's task schema, with no key, solution or traps, and no skills, band or difficulty. Its type is `Task` of the package `@altnovatrail/spec`: the canvas kind is `handwriting_sheet` or `workbook`, a sheet has the grid `grid` and the step `grid_step`, an item of a sheet the slots `answer_slots`, and an item of a workbook the line `line`. Beyond the schema there is only the temporary field `canvas.tools` (Д5 in [run/DEBT.md](../run/DEBT.md)). The open fields `givens` and `unknown` the element takes and checks, and `app` shows them:
  - the element checks the task strictly, as the schema does: what the schema would not let through is a `TypeError` with the field's path, and so is a field that is not in the open view, be it an old name or a closed field. Only `undefined` omits a field, and `null` is a wrong value. The types of `spec` are flat, and only the check tells the canvas kinds apart: it gives a narrowed task of its own types, by which the rest of the element reads, and the entry points do not export them;
  - the sheet shows the grid, the statement and the tools, and the workbook the item lines on the page, the statement above it and the tools. A workbook whose lines did not fit on the page is a `TypeError` on `task.items[i]` of the first row with no room. A slot in a zone is a `TypeError`. The sheet does not show the options of `choice` or the item's text: `app` shows them;
  - the same task, or one equal to it by its template, changes nothing, and another one changes only the template, and in it only the grid or the lines, or only the statement, if the other stayed as it was. A task of another canvas kind rebuilds the template layer. The work stays, since a new task is a new element;
  - a part of the statement has a text or a picture: the file's path from the module's `media/`, a description, a width and a height in whole CSS px;
  - `canvas.tools` is the list of tools, for both canvas kinds, as in the illustration of olympiad maths for a child in spec's `docs/concepts.md`. The element takes `"pen"` and `"eraser"`, and skips unknown ones: some `app` shows, others will come with new versions of the element. Without the field, the pen and the eraser; without `"pen"`, a `TypeError`. Without `"eraser"` there is no eraser at all: no button, no back end of the pen, `tool = "eraser"` is a `TypeError`, and a new task without the eraser returns the pen;
- `workedExample` is a lesson's worked example, the open view of the segment `worked_example`: the statement, the canvas and the demo's parts. The type is `WorkedExample` of the package `spec`, and the check is strict, as for a task: a `TypeError` with the path from `workedExample`. The element shows a task or an example, not both:
  - an example while `task` is set, while the history is not empty, or while a stroke or an eraser's press is in progress is a `TypeError`. `undefined` removes the example, and the sheet is again without a task. Each property removes only its own: `task = undefined` does not remove the example;
  - an equal example — by content — changes nothing, and another one changes the grid and the statement separately, as a task does;
  - **the example mode:** the statement is above the sheet, in the manifest's language, and the grid is the example's. The learner does not write: the drawing surface takes no input, and a finger and the pen scroll the sheet by native scrolling (above, under «The drawing engine and the layers»). `task`, a history other than an empty one, and marks are a `TypeError`, and `undefined` is allowed for them. `tool` is only the pen, with no event, `exportWork()` and `filled` give `null`, and `undo()` and `clear()` give `false`;
  - **the demo's parts** the learner opens one at a time with the button «Дальше» — as a numbered list between the statement and the sheet, in the manifest's language —, and «Показ» hides the whole demo and shows it again. A paragraph in the list is its text; a picture beside the sheet is a frame of its size, as in the statement: with the description until its file has come and been decoded, and then with the picture, and the item's number stands at its top; and a picture in an area is an item with its description. The picture itself stands on the sheet, on the demo layer: an `<image>` stretched over its area — the picture's corners in the area's corners —, under the learner's work and the tutor's layer, and of those laid over one another, the one opened later on top. Until its file has come and been decoded, the sheet is empty there, and the description is in the item. An item is added at the end and is not rebuilt, and with it neither is the picture on the sheet. A hidden demo is the attribute `hidden` on the list and `display="none"` on the group of pictures on the sheet, and the opened parts stay in it. An empty list and a hidden list take no room;
  - an opened part is scrolled into view at once, with no animation, by its nearest edge, and if the consumer's band `--novatrail-canvas-top` covers it, out from under the band too: what scrolls back goes back by as much as the band covers, and a scrolling container at its start passes the rest to the next one. So the part comes out from under the band both in an element with no height and in an element with a height on a page scrolled below its top. The layout runs at once, before the scrolling: a sheet scrolled into view in an element with a height stays in place when the list grows or hides. In an element with no height it does not, as when the statement grows: the sheet's container has `overflow-anchor: none`, and the page's scroll anchor is the host. The emulators do not check this scroll anchor: it waits for hardware, Д7;
  - a picture in an area comes into view by its area, not by its item: what is scrolled is a block of the area in the sheet itself, outside the sticky scene — `visibility: hidden`, so that it is not drawn and takes no pointer, and its place is given by fractions of the sheet, so the layout has nothing to write. A picture in the sticky scene stands at its old place until the next frame, and what covers it would be computed wrongly. The chain of scrolling and the band are the same as for an item, and an area taller than the visible part comes into view by its top, under the band: from below "nearest" places it so, and from above "nearest" places its bottom, and the band brings the top back;
  - a polite region `role="status"` announces an opened part; it is empty in the shadow root from the element's creation and hidden from sight only: «Часть 2 из 4.», "Part 2 of 4.", in the language of the labels, and after it the part's text or the picture's description in the manifest's language, for a decoration only the number. The focus stays on «Дальше». An assignment to `demoState`, «Показ» and a new or removed example empty the region;
- `demoState` is how many parts of the demo are open and whether the whole demo is hidden: a frozen `{ opened, hidden }`, the same object until it changes. For a new example it is `{ opened: 0, hidden: false }`, and without an example `undefined`. The learner changes it with the buttons «Дальше» and «Показ», and `demochange` tells of it, and `app` can return it by an assignment, with no event, no announcement and no scrolling:
  - it is given after `workedExample`: it is checked against the example's number of parts. A state before an example is a `TypeError`, and `undefined` changes nothing;
  - the state is an object with only `opened`, an integer from 0 to the number of parts, and `hidden`, `true` or `false`, otherwise a `TypeError` with the field's path. One equal by content changes nothing. The learner does not close parts one at a time, but `app` can give fewer opened ones too: the items go from the end;
  - a new or another example returns the demo to its start, and an equal one changes nothing;
- `manifest` is the open view of the module manifest. The element reads three fields from it and skips the others: the format version — any but `FORMAT` of `src/core/manifest.ts` is a `TypeError`, since while the format is v0 the platform reads only its current version —, the digit height on the crop `canvas.digit_height`, whole px from 1 to 256, by default 24, and the explanation language `languages.explanation`, in which the statement and the workbook's lines are. Without a manifest, 24 px and the language of the element itself. The canvas settings in the manifest are its own: one that the element does not know is a `TypeError`, as a task's field is. A new language changes only the attributes of the statement and of the list of lines, and `task` and `manifest` do not depend on each other. The type is `Manifest` of the package `spec`. `api` gives the manifest's open view by the package digest of the attempt, once per package, and `app` caches it and gives it to the element;
- `media` is a function that for a link to a media file gives a promise of a `Blob`. The consumer gives the media, and the canvas itself does not go to the network: `app` takes them from what `api` serves, and the samples page from its build. A picture that did not come, or has not come yet, is replaced by a frame of its size with its description. The files of the pictures of the statement and of the demo are taken by one loading of the element:
  - a link is one file and one call of `media`, however many pictures show it: the task's statement, the example's statement, its parts beside the sheet and in sheet areas. The links are named by what the element shows — the task's statement or the whole example —, and the element asks for all the example's pictures when the example has come, before the learner opens the parts;
  - a file is kept while a task or an example names its link: a link that both the previous and the new ones name the element does not ask for again, and it does not keep the files of links that went. A hidden demo and a `demoState` with a smaller `opened` do not drop files;
  - while the element is on the page, a file has one `blob:` address, and the loading decodes it itself, before showing it: an opened part shows the picture in the same press of «Дальше». A browser that does not keep a decoded address at hand decodes the frame's picture once more, and until then the frame shows the description. The address is revoked when nothing names the link any more, when the file failed to decode and when the element left the page, and when the element comes back a new one is given;
  - a link without a file — it did not come or failed to decode — is asked for again with each new task and each new example, when the element comes back to the page, and on a new function. A new function does not cancel loadings already begun;
- `history` is the writing history with which the sheet opens. `app` keeps it on the device; without it the sheet is empty:
  - the same history changes nothing — the sheet's own array or a history equal to it event by event, by the fields that the sheet keeps, and extra fields are skipped; `undefined` is an empty history. So `app`, which keeps the history itself, can give it to the element on every re-render, and the sheet is not rebuilt by it: the stroke under the pen gets laid, and the marks stay;
  - another history is checked: it must be a history that the sheet could have written itself, by its structure, by the order of time and by the rules of the stack. Otherwise a `TypeError` with the field's path, and the sheet stays as it was. `null` is a `TypeError` too;
  - another history replaces the sheet whole: the strokes, the undo stack and the history. The next stroke takes the largest ID + 1, and the clock runs from its last event + 2001 ms. A stroke and an eraser's press under the pen are reset, the marks are placed anew, and there is no event. A history that is the start of the sheet's history replaces it too, with a warning in the console;
  - `task` and `history` do not depend on each other, and the order of their assignment does not matter;

Marks before their history are a `TypeError`: the consumer gives `history` before `marks`. - `tool` is what the primary button does: `"pen"` writes, as the element starts, `"eraser"` erases, `"select"` takes the strokes of one layer within a lasso or a rectangle, to move, copy or delete them, and `"hand"` moves the sheet under any pointer, the last two on every sheet the learner writes on. The learner switches it with the element's buttons, and so can the consumer. Another value, the eraser in a task without it and Select or the hand on a worked example are a `TypeError`. A press goes on as it began, and the pen's eraser erases with any `tool`, if the task allows the eraser;
- `pens` is the settings of the pen and the eraser, spec's `PenSettings`, frozen: the kind of pen in use, each kind's style, the eraser's mode and size, canvas's as the element comes (`src/core/style.ts`). `app` keeps what `penchange` tells for one visit and gives it to each new element. A value not of the type — a style whose `pen` is not its kind's, a colour not `#rrggbb` in lowercase, a width not in tenths from 0.1 to 50, an opacity not whole from 10 to 100, a size not from 1 to 200 — is a `TypeError` with the field's path; `undefined` changes nothing, and setting it sends no event. With the attribute `unstyled` the element keeps it and draws by none of it;
- a value of `tool`, `pens`, `task`, `workedExample`, `demoState`, `paper`, `tracing`, `grid`, `manifest`, `media`, `history` or `marks` assigned before the element is registered the element takes at its creation, through one shared helper, in this order: `history` before `marks`, the example after the task, and the demo's state after the example. An invalid one leaves the default and writes a warning: an exception in the constructor would leave the element not working;
- the interface language is the attribute `lang` on the element itself: `app` sets it, and the element watches it and labels its buttons and the workbook's boxes by it. In the shadow tree `lang` is inherited from the host, so `:lang` and a screen reader see the same language (sources 3.3). **Gives:**
- the event `historychange` — a `CustomEvent` after every stroke, erasing, clearing, undo and act of the tutor, synchronously. A context that is not a task from paper's, an ID the sheet does not show, a point off the sheet or a colour that is not `#rrggbb` is a `TypeError`, thrown before anything changes, and an act of nothing is no action;
- the event `gridchange`, its `detail` `{ grid }`, frozen, when the learner turns a task from paper's grid by the bar's button;
- the event `penchange`, its `detail` the new `pens`, frozen, at each change the learner makes in the popover of the pen or of the eraser, each step of the slider too; assigning `pens` gives none;
- the method `exportPicture()` — the picture of the work: a promise of a PNG `Blob` of the strokes the surface shows, with no condition, for `StoredWork.picture` of `PutWork` ([the contracts text](../spec/contracts/README.md)): the page, a sheet's or a workbook's with its draft, and the ink past it, right and down, the tracing paper where it lies counted whole, to the ink's edge (`pictureOf` in `src/core/sheet.ts`): a pixel a unit while its rows hold no more bytes than the page's, `PICTURE_BYTES`, scaled down past that, its size in its PNG's header. The strokes are those `exportWork()` takes, by the paths the screen fills, each in its style on white, with no theme, template or marks, and the crop's encoder writes it, grey when every stroke is the palette's black or a grey and RGB otherwise, scaled down so that its rows of three bytes a pixel hold within `PICTURE_BYTES`: 1,576,640 bytes at most. It never gives `null`: an empty surface, and a worked example's, is the white page. It is not for the check: only the crop of `exportWork()` goes there;
- the property `history`, read-only — the whole writing history, frozen, the same array until the next action. The measurement sample and the synthetic samples take it (below, under «The sample file»), and so does `app`'s export;
- the property `filled`, read-only — which answer slots are filled: by item, by slot, in the task's order. A slot is filled by strokes among those `exportWork()` would take: not by the stroke in progress, but by the strokes under the eraser before it is lifted:
  - on a workbook — a box with at least one such stroke in it;
  - on a sheet — the only slot, `anywhere` or `sheet`: `[[true]]` if the sheet has such a stroke, otherwise `[[false]]`. A sheet with only `choice` and a sheet with no task have `null`: `app` marks the options. A worked example has `null` too.

  The value is frozen and stays the same array until a slot changes, and on a sheet both values are the same for all elements. An action changes it before `historychange`, and assigning `task` and `history` changes it with no event. By it `app` learns the exit condition of the step `answer_slots_filled`: for `choice`, its own choice of an option; for the other slots, `filled`, as the solving steps of spec's `format/README.md` define it.

There is no event `speak`: it would voice the "listen" of the text canvas, which `canvas` does not build.

**Commands** — methods the consumer calls:
- `undo()` takes back the last action on the sheet — a written stroke, an erasing or a clearing — and gives `true` if the sheet changed;
- `clear()` removes all the strokes in one action, which undo takes back, and on an empty sheet gives `false`.

The element's own buttons, «Отменить» and «Очистить», call them, and the consumer may call them itself.

**The submission context.** The canvas has no data of its own, so it does not keep the submission context but gives it along with the crop:
- `app` keeps the context and returns it together with the marks of this submission. It keeps it while marks may come; whether to store it on the device longer is for `app` to decide, with what it stores on the device and how much. The context is derived from the learner's work — like the strokes, it is data on the device;
- `sheet_box` and the table "label → stroke IDs" do not go to the server, and the group boxes will go only if Т-31 so decides;
- by the context the canvas finds the strokes of the group labels and converts the boxes into the sheet's units, and it does not show on the sheet the strokes erased since (above, under «The sheet's logical coordinates»);
- the context holds the crop's numbers, as `cropOf` gives them, with `sheet_box` and the group boxes, and the groups with the table "label → stroke IDs". The snapping rule needs the crop's integers, and `sheet_box` and the group boxes are checked against them;
- the context carries a digest: CRC-32 and Adler-32 of a text of the table's strokes — points in tenths of a unit and milliseconds — and of the context's integers. It catches an accidental mismatch, not a forgery. The context returns to a sheet whose history holds its strokes — the same sheet, or one opened anew with the same history. Every sheet numbers its strokes from 1, and another sheet's context with the same IDs, or a changed one, is a `TypeError`;
- on a workbook the context is `{ boxes, digest }`: the table "box → stroke IDs" and the digest of its strokes, of all the page's boxes and of the table's rows. It holds no crop numbers and no groups. It returns to a workbook whose history holds its strokes, and only to a page of the same layout: a context of another layout is a `TypeError`. The table does not go to the server, like the table of labels;
- `app` stores the history and the context as they are, through JSON: a store that rounds numbers changes the digest, and the sheet's own context becomes another's.

It imports the entry point before the first render: before the element is registered, React writes an attribute instead of a property. It does not write `null` into the element's properties (`ReactDOMComponent.js`, lines [1961–1986](https://github.com/facebook/react/blob/v19.3.1/packages/react-dom-bindings/src/client/ReactDOMComponent.js#L1961-L1986) and [1463–1471](https://github.com/facebook/react/blob/v19.3.1/packages/react-dom-bindings/src/client/ReactDOMComponent.js#L1463-L1471), and [`DOMPropertyOperations.js`](https://github.com/facebook/react/blob/v19.3.1/packages/react-dom-bindings/src/client/DOMPropertyOperations.js#L231-L232) of React v19.3.1);

In JSX `workedExample` stands before `demoState`, as `history` before `marks`: React assigns the changed properties in the JSX order, and a state before the example is a `TypeError`. React listens to the event by its exact name — `ondemochange`. As with `tool`, `app` gives the state that `demochange` told, not a new literal on every re-render: that would bring the demo back to its start;

- sets the colours of the bar by `--novatrail-canvas-accent` and `--novatrail-canvas-glass`, and hides the bar while its tutor's sheet is open on a phone by `tools-hidden`;

- does nothing for pen mode: it is one for the whole page and holds until a reload, and a new task does not reset it. It keeps a task from paper's `grid` by `gridchange` in the screen's state and gives it back to a new element. An element without a height scrolls together with the page — with one finger off the drawing surface and with two on it, and in the pen mode with one on it too;
- names 2.5.7 on its page «Доступность»: the sheet is moved by dragging alone with one pointer (below, under «Accessibility»);

The element needs nothing else from the policy: it asks for no inline styles, fonts or network, and it works under the policy `default-src 'none'; style-src 'self'; img-src blob:`;
- listens to `historychange` on the element itself and appends the events to the history on the device, and stores the history and the context as they are, through JSON;

### 3.4. Publication and release

The package comes out on npm — `@altnovatrail/canvas`, in the scope of the organization `altnovatrail`. Everything `canvas` releases is the files of one GitHub release, signed by `.github`'s shared `release.yml`, and the same package file goes to npm. The sources are `.github`'s shared [`release.yml` at v0.1.33](https://github.com/altnovatrail/.github/blob/v0.1.33/.github/workflows/release.yml), GitHub Docs at cca0c9d, [authenticate-packages.md](https://github.com/github/docs/blob/cca0c9d7299f760587d6601bd96acfd2f5cb8929/data/reusables/package_registry/authenticate-packages.md) and [packages-classic-pat-only.md](https://github.com/github/docs/blob/cca0c9d7299f760587d6601bd96acfd2f5cb8929/data/reusables/package_registry/packages-classic-pat-only.md), [npm's documentation at 4e8e03b](https://github.com/npm/documentation/tree/4e8e03b978033529bf8f50d74305b29939eb022c) and sources 7.2.

**The release** comes out by itself, as `module-kit`'s does. After green CI on `main`, `canvas`'s own `release.yml` calls the shared one by the SHA of its release, with the version beside it. The number is the next patch of the line `RELEASE_LINE` of that file; the first release of a line is `X.Y.0`, and the tag comes together with the immutable release. A new line starts when the element stops reading a module format that the line's releases read. A release comes out only if the paths it is built from have changed: the list `paths` of `release.yml` names what the consumer receives, by the template's rule, and what the synthetic samples, the samples page and the generator of a module's eval set are built from. The shared release calls `spec`'s recipes by the script `spec/tools/toolchain/toolchain.sh` from `canvas`'s submodule, and before the build it checks that `spec/` is the submodule `altnovatrail/spec` at a tag new enough for the script; otherwise the run Release on `main` stops and names the reason ([`release.yml` of `.github` v0.1.33](https://github.com/altnovatrail/.github/blob/v0.1.33/.github/workflows/release.yml)).

**The probe and publishing.** The run after CI on `main`, if that passed, and the button publish — `publish` is `true` everywhere but `pull_request` —, and every pull request, Dependabot's too, tries the release without publishing: the key `permissions` raises the write permissions there ([GitHub Docs at 33b1f4a](https://github.com/github/docs/blob/33b1f4a/content/code-security/reference/supply-chain-security/troubleshoot-dependabot/dependabot-on-actions.md)). The probe builds the files, signs them and checks the signatures, and the npm step checks the archive — the name, the version, `repository` and what must not be in `package.json` — and makes a trial publish. So an error of the recipe is seen by a pull request, not by an immutable release. The input `npm-package` is `"@altnovatrail/canvas"` on all events, and the release itself publishes the archive to npm.

**The release files** are written by the recipe `release-files <version>` in the stage `toolchain-core`. It builds them from the tree of the commit being released — without history, without the submodule `spec`, without `git` and without tokens:

| File | What it is | Who takes it |
|---|---|---|
| `altnovatrail-canvas-<X.Y.Z>.tgz` | the package, as `npm pack` builds it, with a `NOTICE` about the icons of the set Material Symbols. The package's version is the release number without `v`: git has no number, the recipe writes it in | npm — the same file |
| `samples-page_v<X.Y.Z>.html` | the build of the samples page as one file: `node scripts/page.ts` writes it with the release's version without `v` and with the text sets in force. The license notices of the third-party code it carries are in the file itself: the volunteer downloads only it | an adult — from the release's page |
| `synthetic-samples_v<X.Y.Z>.tar.gz` | the synthetic samples as one archive, flat: a file `<ID>.json` per sample, as `just synthetic` writes them. `scripts/archive.ts` writes the archive — ustar without time, owner and directories, and gzip without name and time —, so one commit gives the same bytes | `tutor`'s CI — by tag and sum |
| `synthetic-samples_v<X.Y.Z>.tsv` | what each sample of the archive shows: a row per sample — ID, purpose, who wrote it and the scenario — under a row of the column names | `tutor` — for cross-checking |
| `synthetic-module_v<X.Y.Z>.mjs` | the generator of a module's eval set as one ES module for Node 24, with no npm packages beside it: `node scripts/generator.ts` builds `scripts/synthetic-module.main.ts` with Vite, without minification, with the license notices in the header | `modules`'s CI — by exact tag and digest, once `release-verify` has checked it |
| `LICENSE` | Apache-2.0, as in `module-kit`'s release | — |
| `SHA256SUMS` | the sums of all the files | anyone |

Beside every file is its signature `.sigstore.json`: cosign without keys, and the identity is the shared `release.yml`. Three consequences follow:
- `dist/` holds only the release files: the shared release uploads everything that lies there and fails on a subfolder. The package's build lies in `lib/` (below, under «Code and tools»);
- a file cannot be added to an immutable release later, so `release-files` itself builds the page's build for the release — without a token and without `gh`:
  - it downloads no tasks: both the synthetic samples and the page's build take `tutor`'s file and the modules' open views without the network, as copies in `tasks/`, which `ci-licenses` checks by the recipe `release-verify` and whose sums `TUTOR_FILE` and `MODULES_FILE` of `src/eval/tasks.ts` hold. The container does have the network: `docker run` of this step runs without `--network`, and Docker connects such a container to the default network ([`release.yml` of `.github` v0.1.33](https://github.com/altnovatrail/.github/blob/v0.1.33/.github/workflows/release.yml); [Docker's documentation at 226023f](https://github.com/docker/docs/blob/226023fc89bdeb3f051c4c8c697d04ed5f5bcd2c/content/manuals/engine/network/_index.md));
  - it has cosign only if cosign is installed in the stage `toolchain`, with its own sum;
  - that the build holds no closed fields of a task is checked by the same build that writes the release file: `openTasksOf` lets through only the fields of the open view, which the types of `@altnovatrail/spec/formatv0/open` name, at any depth, and the build rejects a picture in the statement — `tutor`'s file carries no media;
  - the tasks of a module version that is later withdrawn stay in a build already released: a file cannot be removed from an immutable release, and the page does not go to the network. So the build takes no version that the index of its release of `modules` marks withdrawn (`scripts/modules.ts`), and a child sample session is run by the build of the latest release of `canvas`; reading the marks in the index of the newest release of `modules`, as spec asks, is debt Д4;
- every release is a new version on npm too, even if the package's code did not change: there is one tag for everything. `app` will take the package only after the measurements of AI reading, and this is reconsidered then.

**The order of releases.** A new canvas kind and a new lesson segment come out before the module that takes them. Only a decision of its own changes the workbook's layout numbers and the rule of the boxes: under new numbers the answers from old histories would land in other boxes (below, under «The workbook»).

**To npm** the package goes by a step of the shared `release.yml`. It is the same run, after the release has come out and been checked, and the same file `.tgz`. The sum of the version on npm is the sum of this file.
- **Without a long-lived token** — trusted publishing by OIDC. From a reusable workflow npm checks the name of the caller, that is, `canvas`'s `release.yml`, and both workflows give `id-token: write` ([npm's `trusted-publishers.mdx` at 4e8e03b](https://github.com/npm/documentation/blob/4e8e03b978033529bf8f50d74305b29939eb022c/content/packages-and-modules/securing-your-code/trusted-publishers.mdx)).
- **Provenance** is created by npm itself. Its certificate names `.github`'s shared `release.yml` at the release's SHA in the SAN, as the cosign signatures do, and the SLSA predicate names `canvas`'s `release.yml` on `main`: the registry accepts provenance from a reusable workflow of another repository ([npm's `trusted-publishers.mdx` at 4e8e03b](https://github.com/npm/documentation/blob/4e8e03b978033529bf8f50d74305b29939eb022c/content/packages-and-modules/securing-your-code/trusted-publishers.mdx); sources 7.2).
- **The OIDC exchange** goes through for `canvas`, though its GitHub token has an immutable subject and the bug npm/cli#9969 — a refused exchange for such repositories — is open (sources 7.2).
- **The step itself** runs on the runner, not in the caller's stage `toolchain`: the tokens do not go there. The other requirements on it are spec's. Before the GitHub release the step checks the archive: the name and the version, `repository` as an object with the repository's url, and that `package.json` has no `private`, no `publishConfig` and no `tag` of its own. Otherwise the run stops and names the reason. Whether the publish went through is decided by the registry, not by npm's exit code. If npm refuses when the GitHub release has already come out, the run is restarted: the retry takes the archive from the release, checks the attestation and the signature, and publishes it. Otherwise the author publishes the same checked file by hand ([`release.yml` of `.github` v0.1.33](https://github.com/altnovatrail/.github/blob/v0.1.33/.github/workflows/release.yml)).

**How they are taken and what is checked.** A file of a NovaTrail release enters code or CI only once `spec`'s recipe `release-verify` has accepted it, and a package from npm, once `release-verify-npm` has accepted the lock file in CI of the pull request that changes it. The recipe accepts `canvas`'s files signed by `.github`'s shared `release.yml`, of a `.github` release the recipe admits, on a call by `canvas`'s `release.yml` from `main`, at the tag's commit, for `canvas` has immutable releases and a ruleset of the tags `v*`.
- **`app`** takes `@altnovatrail/canvas` from npm at an exact version, and on its `main` the registry is the only source. With npm the lock file holds the sum `integrity`, and npm checks it at installation. `npm audit signatures` checks the registry's signature and the provenance, but not by what workflow the package was built (`npm-cache.md`, `npm-audit.md` and pacote 22.0.0 of [npm/cli v12.2.1](https://github.com/npm/cli/tree/ae98c62586b6fea44114f72e249e246a2981de70)). The link to the signed release is held by the signature of the file itself: the archive from the registry is the same bytes as the release's `altnovatrail-canvas-<X.Y.Z>.tgz`, and `app` checks it by the recipe `release-verify-npm` in CI of the pull request that changes the lock file, and the `integrity` is that of the checked bytes. From then on `integrity` holds the version, and `SHA256SUMS` is not needed for this.
- **A working copy** during development is connected by a link. With npm this is `npm link`, and installing a folder outside the project also puts a symlink (`npm-link.md` and `npm-install.md` of [npm/cli v12.2.1](https://github.com/npm/cli/tree/ae98c62586b6fea44114f72e249e246a2981de70)). It does not get into `app`'s `main`: only exact versions go into a release.
- **`tutor`'s CI** takes the archive of the synthetic samples from the release by exact tag and sum, its recipe `canvas-check`. The recipe `release-verify` checks the signature: the SAN is the shared `release.yml` at the SHA of a `.github` release, the repository and the workflow are `canvas` on `main`, the commit is the one the tag is on.
- **An adult** downloads the page's build from the release's page. Nobody expects them to check the signature, and `SHA256SUMS` is open to anyone. The release's description begins with a paragraph about the page: open it from a file on a computer or on Android, it does not work on an iPad, it goes to no network, and GitHub sees the IP address of whoever downloads it. The shared `release.yml` writes the description itself and takes no text from the caller, so the job `notes` of the repository's own `release.yml` puts the paragraph first.

**Recipes for the shared workflows.** The TypeScript wrapper and the shared release expect them in this shape. The wrapper's contract is `.github`'s, and the wrapper itself is `typescript.yml` of the `.github` release that `ci.yml` pins ([`typescript.yml` of `.github` v0.1.33](https://github.com/altnovatrail/.github/blob/v0.1.33/.github/workflows/typescript.yml)). What `canvas` chose where the contract left the choice to it is below, under «Code and tools».
- **The recipes** are five, four named as `go.yml`'s. What to check with and at which versions, `canvas` decides and pins:

An exception — by name and only for a tool that ships nowhere, each with its own decision — is in `licenses.json`, as `lightningcss` under MPL-2.0, which Vite brings. Beyond the contract, it checks what the page's build carries: the release files it takes, by `release-verify`, and the copy of the modules' open views and media against a fresh `module-kit open` (below, under «The sample file»).

  The recipe `ci`, which calls all five, is for the developer: the wrapper does not call it.

It runs as the runner's user, with `HOME=/tmp`, with the network and with the flag `--init`, and in the jobs `test` and `engines`, where the browsers start, also with `--ipc=host`. Of the step's environment only `CI` goes into the container: by it Vitest by default runs the browser headless and does not allow `test.only`. ### 3.5. The sample file

The sample file is the eval protocol: the message `Sample` of the package `novatrail.eval.v1`, its markup is the field `markup`, and the rules of the groups, the crop, the snapping and the boxes are those the device and the runner share ([the text of the contracts](../spec/contracts/README.md)). It is one JSON per sample, by the JSON mapping of proto3, the crops in it PNG in base64. What it holds is set by its purpose and by who wrote, and the sources are MathTrail's canvas-api, [`docs/decisions.md` at 0901af0](https://github.com/mathtrail/canvas-api/blob/0901af0/docs/decisions.md), the research of sources 1.4 and `tutor`'s format of a sample, [tutor@231bf8b](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/tree/231bf8b). What the page asks a person and shows them is below, under «The samples page's steps», and where the build opened from a file works was probed in browsers and on devices (measurements 1.1–1.3).

**How the file is built.** The module `src/eval/` — outside the package, without the DOM, in Node and in the browser — builds `Sample` with the code of `@altnovatrail/contracts`: `sampleOf` from the purpose, "who wrote", the provenance, the writer code, the reference to the task, the sheet or the workbook with the writing history, and the markup in the sheet's stroke numbers. `sampleText` writes the file with every field, zeros too, by the proto names, `readSample` reads it strictly, and `personId`, `writerCode` and `syntheticId` give the ID and the code. The order of computation, such that the runner, from what is recorded, repeats what is recorded:
1. the device builds the groups and the table of boxes by the raw history, by space and time. The labels are placed by position, top to bottom, so as not to give away the order of writing, and the rule computes in tenths of a unit and in whole milliseconds, as the sample records them, so by the recorded history it gives the same groups;
2. the sample's history — time in whole milliseconds, positions to a tenth, without pressure;
3. the final geometry — the strokes in the canonical direction, with the start higher, and when equal, further left; points along the stroke's length every 0.25 units, while the ends and the points that hold the stroke's box stay; the strokes numbered by place, since sequential numbers would give away the order of writing, and the table of groups and the markup moved to the new numbers;
4. the crops — by the file's strokes, at every eval height the protocol names and at the module's height, with h_med in `bundle_median`, and for every crop D, D′, `s`, `sheet_box`, width, height, margins and, by the groups, the boxes, by the formulas of the crop for checking, above, under «The sheet's logical coordinates». They compute in tenths of a unit, as the sample writes, so the crop of a measurement sample from an adult matches `app`'s crop byte for byte. Their numbers for the geometry are the same as for the history, and the pixels differ by no more than a quarter of the gray scale: that is how the step of 0.25 was chosen.

Examples of each composition are in `examples/`: a test writes them, and `ci-licenses` checks them by `spec`'s recipe `sample-check` (below, under «Code and tools»).

**Synthetic samples.** The generator `src/eval/synthetic.ts` draws 16 samples from the seeds 101–116 on the tasks of `tutor`'s file of open views from `tasks/`, the one `TUTOR_FILE` of `src/eval/tasks.ts` names: sheets of lines and of free writing and workbook pages, without essays. The hand is `src/eval/hand.ts`, with its own shapes of digits and signs, through `Recorder` into `Work`, with erasing, clearing and undo. The scenarios are a correct solution, one with an error, an erased one, a cleared one and an undone one, a crossed-out one, a mirrored digit, an illegible answer, two answers and none, an empty sheet, a number line, and pages full, with empty and crossed-out boxes, with a mirrored digit, and empty; the markup says what is drawn, and the seed chooses `first_error`. The set is not in git: `just synthetic <folder>` builds the generator with `tsc` into `build/` and writes the files `<ID>.json`, and prints the rows "ID — purpose — writer — scenario" for the release's description.

**Synthetic samples of a module's eval set.** `just synthetic-module <package> <tag> <folder> <task>:<seed>...` writes samples of a module's eval set on its package — the folder that `module-kit build` wrote at the commit the samples go into. The recipe runs the same file that every release carries, `synthetic-module_v<X.Y.Z>.mjs`, built from the tree with version 0.0.0, and `modules`'s CI takes the release's file, once `release-verify` has checked it, and calls it with the same arguments after `--module`. Samples that will match what the release's file writes in `modules`'s CI are written by that release's file, or by the recipe in a clone of `canvas` at its tag: the recipe at another commit builds other code. A second mode of the same file, `--digest <package> <task>...`, prints a row per task — its ID and the digest of its open view by the same projection as the reference in a sample —, and by it `modules`'s CI checks people's samples against the commit's package. Every task with an open view has a digest, and so do choice, essay and zone, which the generator does not draw; a template has no open view, and a task that is not in the package has no digest: the call prints the rows of the others, names such tasks after them and exits with code 1. The tests hold the digests of the essays and choices of `english-essays`, whose package `tasks/` holds. From the package `inventory.json` and `server.json` are taken: the inventory must be of the format the element reads and name `server.json` by its digest and size, and the package digest is the sha256 of the inventory. `moduleTasksOf` builds a task's open view from the canonical view by the same tables of open fields by which the page's build checks `tutor`'s file, only here the closed fields are dropped; the view and its digest are byte for byte what `module-kit open` gives. The sample is an eval set's, by an adult in their own handwriting or imitating a child's, without a writer code, under Apache-2.0, with the source `modules`: the tag the call named, the module's ID and version, and the package digest. The seed chooses the scenario and the writer, and the ID is `synthetic-<seed>`. The generator draws a sheet with one place `anywhere` and a workbook page; a template, a choice, an essay and a zone it rejects and names. A file of the same seed in the folder is written anew only if it is a sample of the same task of the same module; otherwise the call writes nothing.

**A sample is not a submission for checking.** `sheet_box`, the table of groups, the strokes and their time do not leave the device: that is the rule of checking (above, under «The sheet's logical coordinates»). A sample has a path of its own. It is made for eval, with consent, and the file goes where its purpose directs. So it holds what a submission does not — as much as the eval of this purpose needs.

**Three fields** set what lies in the file and where it may go:

| Field | Values | Why |
|---|---|---|
| purpose | measurement; eval set | a measurement sample from an adult — outside git, at `tutor`; an eval-set sample — a pull request into `modules`, Apache-2.0, cannot be withdrawn |
| who wrote | an adult in their own handwriting; an adult imitating a child's; a child | a child — only with the purpose "measurement", but their sample does not go to `tutor` together with the adults' samples: it lies outside every repository, in the operator's closed bucket, without a pull request, for two years from its session. A runner sends it to no AI provider until the admission table admits to that provider the work of a child under 13 |
| provenance | person; synthetic — with a seed | does not change the composition. For synthetic samples the sample's ID is derived from the seed: one seed gives the same file byte for byte. For a person the ID is random |

By these fields `modules`'s CI accepts only an eval-set sample from an adult, and `tutor`'s runner does not send a child sample to AI. Synthetic samples come out in each of the two compositions below, and `tutor`'s CI checks both.

**Composition:**

| What | measurement by an adult | eval set and child |
|---|---|---|
| strokes | the element's writing history (above, under «Docking with the app»), rounded by step 2 and without pressure: a stroke is an ID, a pointer type, its style if it has one and points of x, y and time, in the order of writing; erasings, cuts with their pieces, clearings and undos with the time and the IDs they removed or brought back (above, under «The drawing engine and the layers») | the final geometry of the visible strokes, each with its style if it has one: the points go along the stroke's length, the stroke's direction is canonical, the strokes are numbered by place; no time, no pressure, no order of writing, nothing erased |
| crops | at every digit height of the protocol; the working one is the module's height | the same |
| groups | the boxes in the fractions of every crop and the table "label → stroke IDs" | the same |
| crop parameters | for every crop — D, D′, `s`, `sheet_box`, width, height and margins; shared — `h_med` and the sheet's size | the same |
| markup and reference to the task | below | the same |
| about the person | purpose, who wrote, the version of the consent text — the version of the page's text set, writer code | for an eval set — without a writer code and with the license, for a child — with the code |
| versions | of the format and of `canvas` | the same |

Time is milliseconds from the first event of the sample, without a date, and coordinates are the sheet's units with a precision of 0.1 (above, under «The sheet's logical coordinates»). By the scale A4 of the study "A child's handwriting on the canvas and the AI tutor" («Рукопись ребёнка на канве и ИИ-наставник»), the writing history is class (c), and the final geometry with groups is (b): the division into strokes and the split into groups by time remain in it (sources 1.4).

**What the file does not hold:** name, email, GitHub account, device, browser, screen, language, date, age and grade. A name written on the sheet itself would get into the strokes and the crops. The page says that it is not written on the sheet, and before a push or a handover a person looks for one in «Открыть образец» (below, under «The samples page's steps»).

**The writer code** is random, the page makes it, and it is linked to no one. The lines of one sheet are «связаны писавшим» ("linked by the writer"), and `tutor` counts the measurement's boundary by sheets: by the code the runner also takes into account the link between the sheets of one writer. For a child, the adult writes the code down beside the consent, and by the code the samples are withdrawn and deleted when their period ends. The page does not remember the code: a person enters their code themselves (below, under «The samples page's steps»).

**The shared rules.** The rule of the groups and the rules of the crop are one for the device and the runner. Samples without a history carry the groups the device built, and the runner does not recompute them. Their test vectors are `spec`'s: four files of `vectors/eval/v1/` of the npm package `@altnovatrail/contracts`, as messages of the protocol — the writing history to the groups, the geometry with groups to the stroke bundles and the crop's numbers, a model's box to the strokes it takes, and a page's strokes to the boxes and the table of the filled ones. They hold no PNG pixels: two implementations may differ by a gray level. `canvas`'s tests check its code against them (below, under «Code and tools»), and the rule changes only in `spec` — `canvas` brings an edit as a row «в реестр spec» of `run/QUESTIONS.md`.

**The crops** are drawn by `canvas` at saving — by the same DOM-free code as the crop for checking (above, under «The drawing engine and the layers»), at every digit height the protocol names. The working one is the module's digit height: at it `app` sends the crop. `tutor`'s runner compares the heights «парно с 32 px на тех же строках», and so the comparison runs on one render. The states — after a line and a pause within a line — are drawn by the runner, from the writing history, by the same rules. So only measurement samples from adults have them.

**A workbook sample** (below, under «The workbook») carries:
- the crops of the filled boxes at the eval heights, with the item and the box, — by the same `cellsForCheck` as the work for checking;
- the table "box → stroke IDs", which the device built by the raw strokes. The runner does not recompute it, as it does not the groups, and in eval-set and child samples it moves to the new stroke numbers, as the table of groups does;
- the rectangles of all the page's boxes in the page's units — those the workbook's digest holds. The runner does not need `canvas`'s layout;
- in eval-set and child samples only the boxes' strokes, and in a measurement sample from an adult the whole writing history.

A workbook sample has no groups and no group boxes: the table of boxes takes their place. So the rows "groups" in the composition above are about the sheet. The workbook's markup is the value in every box. The eval protocol took the rule of the boxes as it is, with its vectors, and both the page and the synthetic samples build such a sample.

**The markup** is what the sample must say: the field `markup` of the protocol ([the text of the contracts](../spec/contracts/README.md)):
- the truth is an observation: every answer slot that AI reads, with its value as it is written, or with the reason why there is none. It holds no outcome and no trap: the runner gets them by the key;
- the notes of the answer slots are "mirrored digit" and "crossed out and written anew";
- on a sheet — the parts of the solution: kind, text, strokes, "crossed out", "illegible" and "final answer", and the number of the first error; for an essay — instead of parts, the assessments of the criteria, which the organizer gives;
- on a workbook — neither parts nor assessments: its places are the boxes.

The page marks up a sample after the writing, before saving, and saves it only when the markup is enough (below, under «The samples page's steps»). The parts of a sheet are the device's groups, which the writer merges, and the sheet's truth is derived from the parts with the result. After saving, the markup does not change. The page calls no AI, so the markup does not adapt to the model's answer. A child sample is marked up by an adult, from the child's words, after the writing.

**The reference to the task** carries no key: the canvas does not get it, and the key lies with the runner, in the task bank or in the module package. The reference holds:
- the source: the release tag and the sha256 of the file from which the task came into the build;
- the module or the task bank, and its version;
- the task's ID and the seed of a template task;
- the digest of its open view: it checks the seed. `canvas` does not match the samples of the task of `tutor`'s file and of a `modules` release: the reference names its source, and whether to match them, and by what, is for `tutor`'s runner to decide (Д4).

**What the page shows and writes with each source.** No page goes without tasks: a task's number from the volunteer's words is not the sheet the learner has.

| Source | For | What it shows | What goes into the reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| the open views of the measurement's tasks from a `tutor` release | the measurement and the child sample session | the statement, the items and the answer slots, from the open view | `tutor`: the release's tag and sha256, the bank and its version, the task's ID, the digest |
| a `modules` release through `module-kit open` | the eval set | the same, and the media files | `modules`: the tag of the release that released the package, the module's ID, the package's version and digest, the task's or the template's ID, for an instance the seed, the digest |

**Modules' tasks in the build.** `module-kit open` writes `open-views.json` — the open view of each task with its digest, for a template an instance for each seed — and the files of the device part. The release's build runs without the network, so the page's build takes a copy of this output in `tasks/modules-<tag>/`, of the release `page_modules_tag` of the justfile with the seeds `open_seeds`: its `open-views.json`, the release's index, and for each module `LICENSE`, `NOTICE` and the files of `media/` that the statements of the taken tasks name. `ci-licenses` writes the output anew — with the binary of `module-kit` at `module_kit_tag` and the archives of the packages that `release-verify` accepted — and compares it with the copy byte for byte (`just modules-open`):
- the page takes the sheets with one place `anywhere` — among them a choice written by hand —, the essays and the workbook's pages, the sheets of the board module `novatrail/check-board` too, the tasks of `tutor`'s check board. A tapped choice is shown and marked by `app`, and the page does not take it;
- the digest of the open view in the sample's reference is byte for byte the one `module-kit` gave: `modules`' `just eval-match` checks the sample against it;
- only the eval set takes the modules' tasks, and the measurement and the child sample session take the tasks of `tutor`'s file (Д4);
- the element gets the module manifest — the digit height — and the media from the build's data, and the file's notices name each module with its `NOTICE`.

**Signatures.** CI takes `module-kit` as the binary of its release by exact tag, not by `go install`, and before the first run checks its signature with `spec`'s recipe `release-verify`. So too the `modules` release and `tutor`'s file. From then on their digest holds the files.

### 3.6. The samples page's steps

What the samples page asks a person and shows them is below, and what lies in the file is above, under «The sample file». The screens are only in Russian; the texts the script writes are in `page/texts.ts`, and the few fixed words in the template `page/index.html`, so that a translation goes into the same place. The texts here are the meaning of the screens, not the final words. The page's sheet has the element's attribute `unstyled`: its strokes have no style and it has no pen's settings, since the notice and the form of a measurement do not yet name a stroke's style, its kind of pen and colour, and a sample holds only what its writer consented to (Д299 spec). The notices and forms of the purposes are text sets of `spec`'s part `legal`: their words are approved by the author on Claude's analysis, and the page takes them from a file of `spec`'s release. The legal texts are in English, and the English text is in force; a screen in Russian shows its gist from the part's passport beside it and says that the English is in force.

**Purpose and text sets.** There is one build. The measurement, the eval set and the child sample session are open only if the build carries a current text set for them: the samples page notice and the consent form under one version, which does not depend on the language. Each purpose has a set of its own — `measure`, `eval-set` and `child-sample` of `spec`'s part `legal` —, and its current one is the version in force with the highest number that the author approved on Claude's analysis. The set's ID and the number joined by a hyphen, `measure-3`, is the "version of the consent text" in the file (above, under «The sample file»). The build takes the sets from `spec`'s release file `legal-<version>.tar.gz` — its copy `tasks/spec-<tag>/legal-<version>.tar.gz` of the tag `spec/` stands on, which `release-verify` checks and `LEGAL_FILE` of `scripts/legal.ts` names —, and reads it by the rules of reading the part in the part's README. The page does not show the translations of the texts that the part carries: the English text is in force.

| | no current set | a current set |
|---|---|---|
| measurement | closed | an adult whose signed form the organizer accepted |
| eval set | closed: the sample is published with the module forever (sources 8.1; [GitHub Docs at efd4783](https://github.com/github/docs/blob/efd4783/content/authentication/keeping-your-account-and-data-secure/removing-sensitive-data-from-a-repository.md)) | an adult whose form was accepted, on the modules' tasks the build carries; the organizer brings the sample into `modules` |
| child sample session | closed | an adult from whom the organizer accepted the form of a parent or guardian |

The page shows a closed purpose and names the reason. Which sets of the release are current, `scripts/legal.test.ts` holds. The test part `page/sets/test/`, in the layout of `legal`, with the sets `test-measure`, `test-eval-set` and `test-child-sample` in force, goes only by the builder's flag `--texts` and only into a page of version `0.0.0`, which a release never has. It is in the page's tests and in the test build for devices, and does not go into a release. The test build is built by the job `android` of the workflow `page.yml` beside the release's build and kept for 7 days as the artifact `samples-page-test`. The page is a barrier of the interface: anyone can download it. The rules keep the acceptance of files and the checks by the fields — the version of the text set and «кто писал» — with the measurement's organizer, `tutor`'s runner and `modules`' CI.

**The writer code** is one per person, not per device. The page makes it but does not remember it: a person enters their code themselves or asks for a new one. So on a shared tablet nobody sees another's code or picks it. The code is a pseudonym, not anonymity. Measurement samples and child samples have it, and an eval-set sample does not (above, under «The sample file»).

**The adult's steps:**
1. **Opening.** The page says what it is: a person writes the solution of a task by hand, and the page saves the sample as a file on this device. Also its version, that it goes to no network and that GitHub saw the IP address of whoever downloaded it. Where the page's script does not run — on the iPad «Файлы» shows it without it (measurements 1.3) — the markup itself says that the page does not work here and that it must be opened on a computer or an Android tablet; the script removes this paragraph. The measurement is on computers and Android, and the page has no hosting.
2. **Purpose**: the measurement or the eval set. It is chosen at every opening, before the writing, and the page does not remember it. The child sample session is a separate button, below.
3. **Code**: for the measurement, and of the adult's purposes only for it: «ввести свой код» or «новый писавший». The code step of a child's session is its own, below. The page shows a new code and asks that it be written down: consent to the measurement samples is withdrawn by it.
4. **The notice of the purpose**, with the version of the text set: the gist in Russian, and below it the text of the set in force, in English. It says:
   - what lies in the file, as above, under «The sample file». A measurement sample has the writing history with time and the writer code, and an eval-set sample the final geometry without time and without a code. Both have crops, markup and «кто писал»;
   - who will see the file:
     - a measurement sample: the organizer and the AI providers that the text set names;
     - an eval-set sample: everyone. It goes into the open `modules` forever, under Apache-2.0, and everyone gets a "perpetual, worldwide … irrevocable" right to copy and change it (sources 8.1). It is also run at AI providers in `modules`' CI. The organizer signs off the commit, and the sample is linked to no GitHub account of the volunteer;
   - the term and how to withdraw consent. Consent to a measurement sample is withdrawn by the writer code, and the notice names honestly what will remain: the AI providers' logs, the summary report without handwriting and the copy of the file on the device. An eval-set sample cannot be withdrawn;
   - that nothing personal is written on the sheet: no name, signature, school or contacts.

   The notice has two statements without which one cannot go on. The first is «мне 18 лет или больше, и по законам места, где я живу, я вправе заключить этот договор». The second is «форма согласия версии … подписана и принята организатором». The statements are the page's words, not the set's: in the part `legal` they stand in the forms.
5. **Writing and markup**, as above, under «The sample file». «Кто писал» is set for every sample: in one's own handwriting or imitating a child's. After the writing, «Разметить»: the page shows the crop that the model will see, and the markup form.
   - On a sheet, the parts of the solution come first, one device group per part. The writer merges neighboring ones, splits and reorders them, and for each enters the text — or a drawing in words — and the flags «зачёркнута», «написана неразборчиво нарочно», «итоговый ответ задачи». «Показать на листе» outlines the part's groups with the element's boxes. Below come the first error, the final answer as written, and the marks «зеркальная цифра» and «зачёркнуто и написано заново».
   - On an essay — a sheet whose only answer place is bound to `sheet` — there are no parts: the writer enters the essay's text as written, with its errors and line breaks, or marks that it cannot be read whole, and a sheet without strokes is an empty essay. The organizer gives the criteria's scores after the writing, and the page writes them empty.
   - On a workbook, each box of the page: an empty one is named so, and a filled one has its crop, its value as written, «неразборчиво» or «можно прочесть по-разному», and the same marks.
   - A new stroke after «Разметить» removes the form: the markup is asked for anew. The page names what is missing, and saving is possible only when the markup is enough and it is confirmed that the sheet holds nothing personal.
6. **Saving:**
   - the page shows the crops — what the model will see — and, briefly, what else lies in the file;
   - the file is named by the sample's ID, `<id>.json`, for any purpose: so it goes into a module's `eval/` without renaming. The ID of a person's sample is 12 characters of `a`–`z` and `2`–`7`, so it will not start with `synthetic-`;
   - the person confirms that the sheet holds nothing personal;
   - the file goes where the browser puts downloads (measurements 1.1–1.3; sources 3.6): on Linux, Firefox and Chrome put it straight into the downloads folder, asking nothing; Chrome on Android, into `/sdcard/Download`, also without asking; Safari on the iPad asks, and puts it into "Downloads" of "On My iPad" or of "iCloud Drive", as its settings choose — with "iCloud Drive" the sample file, a child's too, goes to Apple's cloud. In a child's session, before saving, the page asks to check that Safari on the iPad saves downloads «На iPad», not in iCloud Drive; the page does not see the setting;
   - where to pass the file: a measurement sample and an eval-set sample go to the organizer, by an upload link that works for 12 hours at most, and then the file is erased from the device; the organizer brings an eval-set sample into `modules`.

**The child sample session** is led by an adult, and the child writes. While the set `child-sample` has no current version, the session is closed.
- **Before the first session** the adult opens the page without the child and asks for a new code. They write the code into the consent form, a parent or guardian signs it, and the organizer accepts it with the code already in it. By the code, consent is later withdrawn and the samples are deleted by their term.
- **The session's notice:**
  - the file holds the final geometry without time and pressure, crops, markup, «кто писал» — the child — and the child's code, which links all their samples (above, under «The sample file»);
  - the file goes into no repository, and to no AI provider that the admission table does not admit for a child under 13 (above, under «The sample file»);
  - where to pass the file and who is admitted to it;
  - GitHub saw the IP address of the adult who downloaded the page. The release's description says the same before the download.
- **The adult's statements:** «мне 18 лет или больше, и по законам места, где я живу, я вправе заключить этот договор» and «отдельная письменная форма родителя или опекуна этого ребёнка версии … с этим кодом подписана и принята организатором». The form is accepted before the session.
- **The code** the adult enters at the start of each session: the one written into the form.
- **Writing and markup.** The child writes, and the adult marks up, from the child's words, after the writing (above, under «The sample file»).
- **Saving** is as for an adult, but without a pull request. The notice advises passing the file on and deleting it from the device, and after saving the page says that the file goes to the organizer by their upload link, which works for 12 hours at most, and then the adult erases it from the device.

This is how the session is made:
- in «Начале» it is the last choice, in the nested group «Сеанс образца ребёнка» below the adult's purposes; without a current set `child-sample` it is closed, with the reason;
- the code step is shared with the measurement, but has words of its own: «ввести код из формы согласия» or «новый код для формы — без ребёнка». The page shows a new code with the version of the text set, and «Начать» does not open with it: a session runs only with the code of an accepted form. On a switch between the measurement and the session, the code step is cleared;
- «Кто писал» is not asked in the session, and the part's flag «написана неразборчиво нарочно» is labeled «не прочесть и со слов ребёнка»: only the writer knows whether it was on purpose, and without the flag a child's unreadable part cannot be marked. What the flag means for a child sample is the eval protocol's;
- before saving, the phrase about Safari's downloads on the iPad, and after it, «куда передать файл» from the session's texts.

**«Открыть образец»** is a section under «Начало». An eval-set organizer, a module author, the measurement's organizer or the adult who led a child's session opens a sample file in it and, before a push or a handover, looks for a name, a signature, a school and contacts, both in the strokes and in the texts of the markup, which are published with the sample. Neither an AI nor an agent sees the file: a person looks at it on their own device.
- A sample of any purpose opens. `readSample` reads it, and only `sample-check` checks it by the rules: a file the page did not read is named an error, and nothing is shown.
- The page draws the strokes from the file alone: a sheet by its sizes, a workbook each box with its place. No task is needed for this: the file opens even when its task is not in the build. Of a writing history, what the sheet showed at the end is seen, and the checkbox «показать стёртое» adds, dashed, everything written and not visible at the end — the erased, the cleared and what undo took off: it too lies in the file. The picture does not cut a stroke beyond the sheet's edge. Below come the crops from the file, what the model will see, and the markup in words: the purpose, who wrote, the license, the task, the truth by places, the parts with their texts, the first error and the criteria's scores.
- The file is not changed, not saved and goes nowhere: no network, no downloads, no storage. «К началу» is a new opening of the page. Once writing has begun, the section is not there.

**What the page remembers** of people: nothing, no codes, no purpose, no declarations. All this is asked at every opening. It does not remember an unfinished sheet either. The storage of a page opened from a file differs by browser (sources 3.6; measurements 1.1–1.2): in Chromium all pages from `file:` share one storage, which survives a restart; in Firefox it is per path, and a copy in another folder is another file; Chrome on Android opens a page from the downloads as `content://media/external/downloads/<number>`, and all such pages share one storage, which survives a restart of Chrome. So the storage of a page from a file can be trusted with nothing that would be a pity to lose: a new build, a file in another folder or a browser setting loses it. Were the page to keep an unfinished sheet, in Chromium any local HTML file would read it.

### 3.8. The workbook

The second canvas kind of the first slice is the workbook for school math of grade 3. A task's item is a line of pieces of text and boxes, and the answer in a box is a number or a fraction by hand. The sources are [SVG 2](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/CR-SVG2-20261006/text.html), [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/2024/REC-WCAG22-20241212/), [browser-compat-data v8.1.7](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/v8.1.7/svg/elements/text.json), [tutor@8451728](https://github.com/altnovatrail/tutor/tree/845172878617727fa4da3133b0c9633e8d8ee140), the illustration [`draft/NovaTrail Widget.html` of `spec` v0.2.37](https://github.com/altnovatrail/spec/blob/v0.2.37/draft/NovaTrail%20Widget.html) and sources 9.12 and 9.13, the probes measurements 1.4, 1.12 and 1.13. Here is the workbook whole, and a worked example on it is below, under «The task's fields, filled answer slots and the worked example». Where it is a part of the shared design, the details stand there: the layers under «The drawing engine and the layers», the units, the box rule and the crop under «The sheet's logical coordinates», the element's properties, context and types under «Docking with the app», the sample under «The sample file», the code and the tests under «Code and tools».

**The page** is 1000 × 1400 units, like the sheet: a constant of the kind, without a grid. The statement is above it, HTML, in the same scrolling as on the sheet. The items go in lines, in order, with numbers on the left, and what the lines did not take is the draft.

**The layout**: `pageOf` in the DOM-free part, a function of the task alone, in integers:
- the font size is 30 units, a box 100 × 100, the margins 40, two columns of 440 with a gap of 40, rows 32 apart. A line with a box is 100 high, one without a box 46;
- a short item — a line of one row no wider than 384 — stands two to a row, by lines. A long one takes the whole width, 864, and wraps: by pieces; a piece wider than the line, by words and boxes; a word wider than the line, by clusters. A box holds on to the word after it;
- the number "n." is right-aligned in a column the width of "00.", on the baseline of the item's first line, and the line begins at 56 from the item's left edge;
- a page holds up to 10 rows: up to 20 short items or up to 10 long ones of one line. A row that would go below 1360 is a `TypeError` on `task.items[i]` of its first item: the boxes and the font size do not shrink, and the format refuses such a task by the rule `task.workbook-page`;
- the widths of characters are a table, in hundredths of the font size, the mean of Liberation Sans and Liberation Serif (`widths.ts`), and characters differ only by explicit ranges of codes. So the layout is one in any engine and in Node, and the device's font does not move it: a piece is one `<text>`, fitted to its width by `textLength` and `lengthAdjust="spacingAndGlyphs"`;
- the entry point `core` does not give the layout, and its numbers and rule are `spec`'s: `format/page.yaml` and its test vectors `format/vectors/pages/`, which the package `@altnovatrail/spec` carries and to which the tests hold the layout. [vectors/pages.json](../vectors/pages.json) records `canvas`'s own vectors for this layout, the numbers and cases the decision on it chose: nothing writes it and no test reads it.

**The template layer**: SVG with the same `viewBox` as the sheet's grid: numbers and pieces of text, boxes `<rect role="img">` with a frame of 1 CSS px on whole device pixels. The print is in the ink's color and the consumer's font, and it resets the rest of what is inherited. A screen reader reads the lines as a list, in order, and the boxes by the names from the table of labels: «Окошко», «Окошко 1», in the element's language. The lines' language is from the module manifest, and a value that the example's demo wrote into a box is voiced in it, by a print node of its own right after the box, not in its name (below, under «The task's fields, filled answer slots and the worked example»). The element is horizontal under any writing mode of the page. The print takes `k`: it grows with the browser's zoom only until the page fills the element's width, so 1.4.4 of the lines is a declared nonconformance (below, under «Accessibility»). The page is not laid out anew, and the sheet scrolls sideways: 1.4.10 allows it a two-dimensional layout.

**Boxes and the draft**: the rule `cellOf`, in integers, without DOM and without time, for each stroke separately:
- a stroke is a box's if the middle of the bounding box of its points lies in the box widened by 120 thousandths of its size, and the bounding box itself is no wider and no higher than 1600 thousandths of the box. With a box of 100 this is a margin of 12 and a limit of 160;
- of two boxes, the one with more of the stroke's points, then the earlier by (item, box). On the page of this layout the widened boxes do not overlap, and there is no disputed case;
- the rest is the draft: it is on the page and in the writing history, but does not go to checking;
- the rule takes the same strokes as `exportWork()`: the stroke under the pen is not there, and the strokes under the eraser before lift-off are;
- the test vectors are `vectors/eval/v1/boxes.json` of the package `@altnovatrail/contracts`, and the set of the exhaustive search by which the margin and the limit were chosen is `src/core/workbooks.fixture.ts`.

There are no groups of strokes of the sheet on a workbook: the boxes "47 ÷ 6 = [ ] r [ ]" stand closer than their threshold, and one group would take both. Their place is taken by the table "box → stroke IDs".

**Filled state**: the property `filled`: per item, per box, whether it is filled, in the task's order. On a sheet, the only answer slot `anywhere` or `sheet`, and for a sheet with only `choice` and without a task, `null` (above, under «Docking with the app»). A box is filled if it holds at least one stroke. The frame of a filled box is 3 CSS px, its name «Окошко, заполнено», and in forced colors the frame is Highlight. By `filled`, `app` learns of a "filled answer slot", in the sense of spec's glossary.

**To checking** the whole page goes at once. `exportWork()` gives `{ cells, submission }`:
- `cells`: the crop of each filled box by its strokes, as one group, at the manifest's digit height, with the item and the box, in the task's order. At eval's heights this is 200 × 200 px, 64 tokens (the crop's rule is above, under «The sheet's logical coordinates»);
- `submission`: the context `{ boxes, digest }`: the table of boxes and the digest of its strokes, of all the page's boxes and of the table's rows;
- not a single filled box: `null`, even with a draft.

An empty box gives no crop: its verdict is "unreadable" with the reason `blank`, and `filled` says which boxes are empty. Only the crops with their places leave the device, and the table and the digest stay with `app`.

**Marks** come with the answer slot by place, `{ item, slot }`, from 1, as the contract of `app` and `api` places them, and hold on to the strokes of their submission's table row. The frame stands around the box and the visible ink of its strokes together, with a margin of 10. A group label and a model's box on a workbook are a `TypeError`. A mark outlines an empty box by itself, without strokes, and a box not from the page gives no frame. The consumer gives `task` and `history` before `marks`, and a new page sets the marks anew or takes them off, without an event.

**The workbook's sample** (above, under «The sample file») carries the boxes' crops at eval's heights, the table of boxes that the device built, and the boxes' rectangles in the page's units. It is built by the samples page and by the synthetic samples.

**What is still open:**
- devices, the debt Д7: the emulators passed the devices' checklist, and it did not check the font size and the fonts of the lines on children's tablets, the text, its reset and the frames in Safari, VoiceOver and TalkBack by lines and by touch, a fraction by finger and by pen in a box, and the margin and the limit of the box rule — what the decision log names to revisit. A change of the layout's numbers that such a measure brings is a change of `format/page.yaml` by the format's versions, the rules in `spec`'s `format/README.md`, and `canvas` asks for it by a row «в реестр spec»;
