The NovaTrail canvases. A canvas is the workplace of a task, where the learner writes, draws and types; this UI package holds the canvas kinds — for now two, the handwriting sheet and the workbook — with their layers and tools, captures the learner's strokes with time and pressure, draws the picture of the work that goes to be checked and shows the tutor's marks tied to the learner's strokes, and the app builds it in at an exact version. A separate build of it is the samples page, where adult volunteers make samples for eval on the same canvas that learners use: a sample is saved as a file on the volunteer's device and reaches a module's eval set by pull request, and nothing goes to the platform's server. The same page opens a sample file to be looked at — its strokes, what a history erased too, its crops and its markup — so that a person, not an AI, looks in it for a name or a contact before it goes on.

A component of [NovaTrail](https://github.com/altnovatrail/spec/tree/v0.15.1), a universal open platform for adaptive learning, interactive lessons and AI tutoring. It belongs to stage 2, First slice with learners, with tutor, api, app, skill-map and infra; with tutor it is the stage's first code: first the handwriting sheet, while the samples page waits until a measurement needs volunteers' or children's samples.

On a workbook each stroke falls in one box or in none, by a rule in integers on the middle of its points' box; what falls in no box is draft, which stays on the page and goes to no check, and the property `filled` tells which boxes hold writing, and on a sheet whether its one answer anywhere on it, or the whole sheet, does. The module's manifest, the `manifest` property, gives the height of a digit on the crop, 24 px unless it gives another, as without a manifest, and the language of the statement. The sheet fits the element's width, at most 1000 CSS pixels, and the row's Zoom in and Zoom out make it larger, up to twice that, on every sheet of the page till it is loaded again, so that a workbook's printed lines can be read at 200 %; a zoomed sheet scrolls across as well as down, with the row's Left and Right, two fingers and the arrow keys, while the statement stays as wide as the fit. The consumer themes it by custom properties. The DOM-free part parts the strokes into groups by place and time — two strokes no more than 2 seconds apart whose boxes come within a cell of each other are neighbours, and a chain of neighbours is a group — labels the groups A, B, C… by place and gives each one's rectangle on the sheet, for the crop, the tutor's marks and the samples to come; the rule's test vectors are a proposal for the eval protocol. It also draws the crop for checking, with its own raster and PNG encoder: the ink of the visible strokes, scaled so that the writing comes out at the digit height the module's manifest asks for, within the size every Claude model takes as it is, with the groups' frames in its fractions. Its test vectors go to the eval protocol too. The element takes the tutor's marks of a submission back by its property `marks`, with the context it is to give with the crop: a mark holds on to the learner's strokes — a group's, or those a model's box on the crop takes, two thirds of their points within a quarter of a digit of it — and a frame stands round those of them the sheet shows, over the work, read by its name and fading while the learner writes into it. The history goes out by the property `history` and the event `historychange`, which tells of every action with the events it adds, and comes back by the same property: the same history changes nothing, and another, one the sheet could have written, replaces the sheet, undo and all. The pictures' files come from the consumer's `media`, a function that gives a file's Blob by its reference, the contract's own. `exportWork()` gives the work for checking: the PNG of the crop and the context of its submission, which the app keeps and gives back with the tutor's marks, its digest telling the sheet's own context from another sheet's. On a workbook it gives the crop of each filled box, with its item and slot, and the context of the table of boxes; a tutor's mark there stands on an answer cell by its place and frames the box with its ink. Above the statement and the sheet, outside their scrolling, stands the element's own row of buttons — the pen and the eraser the task allows, undo and clear — a toolbar worked by pointer and by keyboard, labelled in English or Russian by the element's own `lang`, each label after an icon of Material Symbols, Google's set, which the build embeds, so the element asks no network for one and the package's NOTICE names it; a `toolchange` event tells of the learner's choice of tool. On a tablet a pen's touch puts the whole page in pen mode, where a finger writes nothing until the row's Draw with finger lets it write again; a touch wider or taller than 100 CSS pixels is a palm and writes nothing, and two fingers scroll the sheet. The row's Up and Down scroll it the same way with a single pointer, on any device, by four fifths of what is in sight. An element whose content overflows it — of a height of its own, or with a zoomed sheet wider than it — scrolls as a region named by the canvas kind, which Tab goes into from the row and out of, and the keys scroll, in Safari too. What of spec's standard of UI packages, WCAG 2.2 AA with 2.5.5 and 2.3.3, the element does not meet yet — a workbook's printed lines keep their own text spacing (1.4.12), and on an element without a `lang`, the region's English name is spoken in the language it inherits from the page (3.1.2) — section 3.10 of [docs/architecture.md](docs/architecture.md) names, with what stands instead and when it goes, for the app's Accessibility page. A lesson's worked example comes by the property `workedExample`, in place of a task: its statement stands above the sheet and its grid on it, but the learner does not write there — a finger or a pen scrolls the sheet as the browser scrolls the statement, and the row, named Worked example buttons, has Next, Demo, Up, Down and the zoom's buttons. Next opens the parts of the author's demo one at a time, into a numbered list above the sheet that a polite region tells a screen reader of, and Demo hides the whole demo and shows it again; the property `demoState` and the event `demochange` tell the app how far the learner has gone, and the app may give it back to a new element after the example — in React's JSX `workedExample` comes before `demoState`, and `ondemochange` listens. The pictures of the demo come from the consumer's `media` with the statement's, one file for each reference, all of them as the example comes, so that a part opened shows its picture at once; a picture on an area of the sheet stands on the sheet itself, stretched over its area, and the sheet scrolls to it as its part opens. The element now waits for the app, and the samples page is built on it; a test plays the app's part, and `just demo` serves a page to try the element on by hand. A release comes out by itself after CI passes on `main`, when a path it is built from has changed: the package's archive, with its NOTICE, LICENSE and their sums, built, signed and checked by .github's shared release, and the same archive goes to npm: the first version by hand, the later ones by the release's own npm step, through trusted publishing, with no long-lived token. A pull request from a branch of this repository tries the release and publishes nothing.

## Install

The app takes the package from npm at an exact version, and spec's npm package, whose types the element's contract is in, at the version of the tag its own `spec` submodule stands on, not the one in canvas's `dependencies`: the types of the contract are structural, so `tsc` finds a difference of shape where the two meet (R73 spec):

```sh
npm install --save-exact @altnovatrail/canvas@<version>
npm install --save-exact --save-dev @altnovatrail/spec@<the version of your spec tag>
```

A pull request that changes the lock file has spec's recipe check it, as NovaTrail's rules ask of every package of `@altnovatrail`: `just -f spec/justfile release-verify-npm package-lock.json`, from a checkout of spec at a tag. It takes the archive from the registry only if it is the same bytes as the file of canvas's release, signed by .github's shared release at the call of canvas's `release.yml` on `main`.

## The samples page

Every release since 2026-11-10 carries the samples page as one file, `samples-page_v<version>.html`: download it from the release's page and open it from the file, on a computer or on Android. It goes to no network while it runs and remembers nothing between openings; the sample it saves is a file on the device. On an iPad, Files shows the file without its script, and the page does not work there. GitHub sees the IP address of whoever downloads a file of a release, the page too, and each release's notes say so first. The page's own file is checked as any file of a NovaTrail release, from a checkout of spec at a tag:

```sh
just -f spec/justfile release-verify canvas v<version> samples-page_v<version>.html
```

with its bundle, `samples-page_v<version>.html.sigstore.json`, beside it.

## The generator of a module's eval set

Each release carries the generator of a module's synthetic samples as one file, `synthetic-module_v<version>.mjs`: one ES module that Node 24 runs with no package of npm beside it, which writes what `just synthetic-module` writes, byte for byte. A module's CI takes it from a release by its exact tag and digest, checked as any file of a NovaTrail release, and runs it with the arguments of `just synthetic-module` (below):

```sh
just -f spec/justfile release-verify canvas v<version> synthetic-module_v<version>.mjs
node synthetic-module_v<version>.mjs --module <package> <tag> <folder> <task>:<seed>...
node synthetic-module_v<version>.mjs --digest <package> <task>...
```

`--digest` prints a line for each task named, its ID and the digest of its open view, `sha256:` and 64 hexadecimal digits, by the same projection the samples name a task by: a module's CI holds people's samples to the package of its commit with it. A task the generator draws no sample of — a choice, an essay, a zone — has its digest too. A template has no open view, and a task the package does not have no digest: the call prints the other tasks' lines all the same, names each of those after them, and exits 1.

The file opens with a comment that says what it is and holds the license notices of all the code it carries, and its code is not minified, so that whoever runs it can read it.

## Code

- `src/core/` — the DOM-free part, `@altnovatrail/canvas/core`: it runs the same in a browser and in Node, and its entry point gives the sheet's size, the types of the data and the fold of a history into its strokes.
- `src/element/` — the element `<novatrail-canvas>`, `@altnovatrail/canvas`: importing it registers the element. The types of its contract with the app — the element as `CanvasElement` has it, its events, the media, the tutor's marks, the history of writing, the work for checking and its context — are spec's, from `@altnovatrail/spec/canvas`, and this entry point gives them under spec's names, the same types; the DOM-free part gives the data's among them, the history of writing and the context of a submission. The app takes them from spec's package, `import type { CanvasElement } from "@altnovatrail/spec/canvas"`, at the version canvas has in its `dependencies`; canvas's own are only what the contract does not name: the commands `undo()` and `clear()`, and the task's temporary tools.
- `src/eval/` — the sample file of spec's eval protocol, which the samples page and the synthetic samples write: a sample of each composition, on a sheet or a workbook, its final geometry and its crops, written and read by the code of `@altnovatrail/contracts`; and the synthetic samples themselves, which code draws from a seed, with a hand of its own, on tasks of tutor's file of open tasks or, for a module's eval set, of the module's package. It runs the same in a browser and in Node, and is no part of the package.
- `scripts/` — the check of the package as a consumer takes it, and of the archive a release puts out, the script that writes the synthetic samples into a folder and the one that packs them for a release, the generator of a module's eval set and the builder of its file, and the script that writes the row's icons, `src/element/icons.ts`, and `NOTICE` from the set of icons the lock file pins: `just icons` runs it, and `just ci` fails if they are not what it writes.
- `demo/` — a page to try the element on by hand, as the app is to have it: it gives the element a language and its task the tools allowed, keeps the history of writing as the element tells it and opens the sheet again from it, draws the groups of the strokes and the crop for checking under the sheet, gives the tutor's marks back over the crop Check sent, and shows a workbook's pages in the font and the language it gives, how many boxes are filled, the crop of each filled box Check sent and a mark given back on a box, and a lesson's worked example on a new element, on which nothing is written, whose demo opens part by part — a picture on an area of the sheet on the sheet itself — the page keeping how far it has gone and giving it back to the next element. `just demo` serves it on this machine.
- `vectors/` — the test vectors of the workbook's page layout, which its test writes and keeps current. The vectors of the rules the sheet shares with tutor's eval runner — the groups of strokes, the crop for checking, the snapping of a model's box to the strokes and the rule of boxes — are spec's, from its npm package `@altnovatrail/contracts`, and the tests hold the sheet's code to them.
- `examples/` — synthetic sample files, one of each composition, which the test of `src/eval/` writes and keeps current, and which spec's `sample-check` checks in CI.
- `tasks/` — the file of open tasks of a release of tutor that the synthetic samples answer, as the release has it, with its signature, which spec's `release-verify` checks in CI; and the index of a release of modules, signed too, with the inventory and canonical view of two packages it names: grade-3-math, on which CI writes the samples of a module's eval set, and english-essays, whose essays and choices the tests hold to the digests of their open views that module-kit gives.
- `licenses.json` — the packages the license check accepts by name, each with why.

`just build` builds the package into `lib/`, and `just release-files v0.1.0` writes the files of that release into `dist/` — the package's archive, with NOTICE in it, the synthetic samples' archive and the list of what each shows, the samples page, the generator of a module's eval set, LICENSE and SHA256SUMS — and prints the sums, which a build of the same commit gives again. `just synthetic <folder>` writes the synthetic samples into a folder, one file each, and prints what each shows: no tree holds them. `just synthetic-module <package> <tag> <folder> <task>:<seed>...` writes synthetic samples of a module's eval set, by the same file a release carries, built from the tree: to write what a release's file writes in a module's CI, run that file, or the recipe in a clone of canvas at its tag, with the package that `module-kit build` wrote of the module at the commit the samples go into, the tag of the newest release of modules, and the module's `eval/` folder; it writes `synthetic-<seed>.json` for each task and seed named, writes a seed's file anew only when it is a sample of the same task, and refuses a template, a choice, an essay or a zone, writing nothing then. `just ci` runs the checks CI runs: format, lint and types; the tests in Node and in Chromium; advisories; licenses. They run in the devcontainer, whose toolchain stage holds the browser the tests run in. The license check is spec's recipe, run from the `spec/` submodule with the exceptions in `licenses.json`, so it needs `spec/` checked out: see [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md), Environment.
