The NovaTrail canvases. A canvas is the workplace of a task, where the learner writes, draws and types; this UI package holds the canvas kinds — for now one, the handwriting sheet, with the workbook to come — with their layers and tools, captures the learner's strokes with time and pressure, draws the picture of the work that goes to be checked and shows the tutor's marks tied to the learner's strokes, and the app builds it in at an exact version. A separate build of it is the samples page, where adult volunteers make samples for eval on the same canvas that learners use: a sample is saved as a file on the volunteer's device and reaches a module's eval set by pull request, and nothing goes to the platform's server.

A component of [NovaTrail](https://github.com/altnovatrail/spec/tree/v0.2.37), a universal open platform for adaptive learning, interactive lessons and AI tutoring. It belongs to stage 2, First slice with learners, with tutor, api, app, skill-map and infra; with tutor it is the stage's first code: first the handwriting sheet, while the samples page waits until a measurement needs volunteers' or children's samples.

## Status

Code started. The detailing is done — epic E1, archived in [docs/plan/E1.md](docs/plan/E1.md). Epic E2, replanned for the MVP on 2026-10-22, built the handwriting sheet as the element `<novatrail-canvas>`, for tutor's check board to take in place of its own sheet; the board has decided to keep its own. Its last task was the author's try of the sheet by hand on the demo page, which found nothing to fix. E2 is done and archived in [docs/plan/E2.md](docs/plan/E2.md). The text canvas, epic E3, was dropped on 2026-10-25: canvas builds no text canvas. Epic E4, taken the same day, built the sheet for the product: the eraser and the history of writing, the layers and the grid, the groups of strokes and the crop for checking, the tutor's marks tied to the strokes, the package's full interface and the input on tablets. It was done on 2026-10-26 and is archived in [docs/plan/E4.md](docs/plan/E4.md). Epic E6, taken the same day, builds the workbook, the first slice's second canvas kind, for school maths in grade 3: its detailing comes first, then its code. The samples page is epic E5 for later; it waits until a measurement needs volunteers' or children's samples. So far there is the package, `@altnovatrail/canvas`, with its tools and the sheet's input: the element takes a pen, a finger or a mouse, one pointer at a time, and draws the strokes, and its methods `undo()` and `clear()` undo the last action and clear the sheet. Its eraser, E4's first task, takes strokes off whole along the path of a press: the element's `tool` switches to it, and a pen's eraser erases whatever the tool, if the task allows the eraser. The sheet keeps the history of writing — every stroke, erasing, clearing and undo, at the sheet's time — for the app to keep. The sheet is a stack of layers, in the order of spec's canvas layers: its task, the element's `task` property, rules it in squares, lines or dots, and the task's statement stands above the sheet, its pictures from the consumer's `media`. The task comes in the names of the module format's draft 0.1 and is checked as its schema checks it, a field it does not know a TypeError; a workbook's task the element checks, and shows from a later task of E6. The module's manifest, the `manifest` property, gives the height of a digit on the crop and the language of the statement. The sheet is at most 1000 CSS pixels wide, and the consumer themes it by custom properties. The DOM-free part parts the strokes into groups by place and time — two strokes no more than 2 seconds apart whose boxes come within a cell of each other are neighbours, and a chain of neighbours is a group — labels the groups A, B, C… by place and gives each one's rectangle on the sheet, for the crop, the tutor's marks and the samples to come; the rule's test vectors are a proposal for the eval protocol. It also draws the crop for checking, with its own raster and PNG encoder: the ink of the visible strokes, scaled so that the writing comes out at the digit height the module's manifest asks for, within the size every Claude model takes as it is, with the groups' frames in its fractions. Its test vectors go to the eval protocol too. The element takes the tutor's marks of a submission back by its property `marks`, with the context it is to give with the crop: a mark holds on to the learner's strokes — a group's, or those a model's box on the crop takes, two thirds of their points within a quarter of a digit of it — and a frame stands round those of them the sheet shows, over the work, read by its name and fading while the learner writes into it. The history goes out by the property `history` and the event `historychange`, which tells of every action with the events it adds, and comes back by the same property: the same history changes nothing, and another, one the sheet could have written, replaces the sheet, undo and all. `exportWork()` gives the work for checking: the PNG of the crop and the context of its submission, which the app keeps and gives back with the tutor's marks, its digest telling the sheet's own context from another sheet's. Above the statement and the sheet, outside their scrolling, stands the element's own row of buttons — the pen and the eraser the task allows, undo and clear — a toolbar worked by pointer and by keyboard, labelled in English or Russian by the element's own `lang`; a `toolchange` event tells of the learner's choice of tool. On a tablet a pen's touch puts the whole page in pen mode, where a finger writes nothing until the row's Draw with finger lets it write again; a touch wider or taller than 100 CSS pixels is a palm and writes nothing, and two fingers scroll the sheet. The row's Up and Down scroll it the same way with a single pointer, on any device, by four fifths of what is in sight. The element now waits for the app and the samples page; a test plays the app's part, and `just demo` serves a page to try the element on by hand. No release has come out yet.

## Code

- `src/core/` — the DOM-free part, `@altnovatrail/canvas/core`: it runs the same in a browser and in Node, and its entry point gives the sheet's size, the types of the data and the fold of a history into its strokes.
- `src/element/` — the element `<novatrail-canvas>`, `@altnovatrail/canvas`: importing it registers the element.
- `scripts/` — the check of the package as a consumer takes it.
- `demo/` — a page to try the element on by hand, as the app is to have it: it gives the element a language and its task the tools allowed, keeps the history of writing as the element tells it and opens the sheet again from it, draws the groups of the strokes and the crop for checking under the sheet, and gives the tutor's marks back over the crop Check sent. `just demo` serves it on this machine.
- `vectors/` — the test vectors of the rule of stroke groups, of the crop for checking and of the snapping of a model's box to the strokes, which their tests write and keep current.
- `licenses.json` — the packages the license check accepts by name, each with why.

`just build` builds the package into `lib/`, and `just ci` runs the checks CI runs: format, lint and types; the tests in Node and in Chromium; advisories; licenses. They run in the devcontainer, whose toolchain stage holds the browser the tests run in. The license check is spec's recipe, run from the `spec/` submodule with the exceptions in `licenses.json`, so it needs `spec/` checked out: see [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md), Environment.
