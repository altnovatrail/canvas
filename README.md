The NovaTrail canvases. A canvas is the workplace of a task, where the learner writes, draws and types; this UI package holds the canvas kinds — for now two, the handwriting sheet and the workbook — with their layers and tools, captures the learner's strokes with time and pressure, draws the picture of the work that goes to be checked and shows the tutor's marks tied to the learner's strokes, and the app builds it in at an exact version. A separate build of it is the samples page, where adult volunteers make samples for eval on the same canvas that learners use: a sample is saved as a file on the volunteer's device and reaches a module's eval set by pull request, and nothing goes to the platform's server.

A component of [NovaTrail](https://github.com/altnovatrail/spec/tree/v0.6.2), a universal open platform for adaptive learning, interactive lessons and AI tutoring. It belongs to stage 2, First slice with learners, with tutor, api, app, skill-map and infra; with tutor it is the stage's first code: first the handwriting sheet, while the samples page waits until a measurement needs volunteers' or children's samples.

Epic E7, taken the same day, took what spec v0.2.42 brought: the task's new open fields, the exit condition that every answer slot holds something — `filled` on a sheet too — and the lesson's worked example, shown on a sheet the learner does not write on. On the way canvas moved to spec v0.4.1, whose npm package gives the types of the module format. E7 was done on 2026-11-01 and is archived in [docs/plan/E7.md](docs/plan/E7.md). Epic E8, taken the same day, is the first release of the package, which spec's cross-repository plan asks for before the app's code; the tests' coverage and the release's build come first. Then canvas moved to spec v0.6.2, of spec's 0.6 line, and its CI now checks the signature of each package of `@altnovatrail` in its lock file, as spec's rules ask; then the element took the module format 0.2 and the types of its contract with the app from spec's npm package, and the package came out in its first release, v0.1.0; from the next one on, the release puts it in npm itself. The samples page is epic E5 for later; it waits until a measurement needs volunteers' or children's samples. So far there is the package, `@altnovatrail/canvas`, with its tools and the sheet's input: the element takes a pen, a finger or a mouse, one pointer at a time, and draws the strokes, and its methods `undo()` and `clear()` undo the last action and clear the sheet. Its eraser, E4's first task, takes strokes off whole along the path of a press: the element's `tool` switches to it, and a pen's eraser erases whatever the tool, if the task allows the eraser. The sheet keeps the history of writing — every stroke, erasing, clearing and undo, at the sheet's time — for the app to keep. The sheet is a stack of layers, in the order of spec's canvas layers: its task, the element's `task` property, rules it in squares, lines or dots, and the task's statement stands above the sheet, its pictures from the consumer's `media`. The task and the module's manifest come in the types of spec's npm package, `@altnovatrail/spec`; the element reads the module format 0.2, which spec v0.6.0 brought — a manifest of another version is a TypeError — and the task is checked as its schema checks it, a field it does not know a TypeError; what the statement gives and asks, the fields `givens` and `unknown`, the element takes and leaves for the app to show; a workbook's task the element shows too: its lines laid out on a page the size of the sheet — numbers, text fitted to the layout's own widths, and boxes, up to ten rows — which a screen reader reads as a list. On a workbook each stroke falls in one box or in none, by a rule in integers on the middle of its points' box; what falls in no box is draft, which stays on the page and goes to no check, and the property `filled` tells which boxes hold writing, and on a sheet whether its one answer anywhere on it, or the whole sheet, does. The module's manifest, the `manifest` property, gives the height of a digit on the crop, 24 px unless it gives another, as without a manifest, and the language of the statement. The sheet is at most 1000 CSS pixels wide, and the consumer themes it by custom properties. The DOM-free part parts the strokes into groups by place and time — two strokes no more than 2 seconds apart whose boxes come within a cell of each other are neighbours, and a chain of neighbours is a group — labels the groups A, B, C… by place and gives each one's rectangle on the sheet, for the crop, the tutor's marks and the samples to come; the rule's test vectors are a proposal for the eval protocol. It also draws the crop for checking, with its own raster and PNG encoder: the ink of the visible strokes, scaled so that the writing comes out at the digit height the module's manifest asks for, within the size every Claude model takes as it is, with the groups' frames in its fractions. Its test vectors go to the eval protocol too. The element takes the tutor's marks of a submission back by its property `marks`, with the context it is to give with the crop: a mark holds on to the learner's strokes — a group's, or those a model's box on the crop takes, two thirds of their points within a quarter of a digit of it — and a frame stands round those of them the sheet shows, over the work, read by its name and fading while the learner writes into it. The history goes out by the property `history` and the event `historychange`, which tells of every action with the events it adds, and comes back by the same property: the same history changes nothing, and another, one the sheet could have written, replaces the sheet, undo and all. `exportWork()` gives the work for checking: the PNG of the crop and the context of its submission, which the app keeps and gives back with the tutor's marks, its digest telling the sheet's own context from another sheet's. On a workbook it gives the crop of each filled box, with its item and slot, and the context of the table of boxes; a tutor's mark there stands on an answer cell by its place and frames the box with its ink. Above the statement and the sheet, outside their scrolling, stands the element's own row of buttons — the pen and the eraser the task allows, undo and clear — a toolbar worked by pointer and by keyboard, labelled in English or Russian by the element's own `lang`; a `toolchange` event tells of the learner's choice of tool. On a tablet a pen's touch puts the whole page in pen mode, where a finger writes nothing until the row's Draw with finger lets it write again; a touch wider or taller than 100 CSS pixels is a palm and writes nothing, and two fingers scroll the sheet. The row's Up and Down scroll it the same way with a single pointer, on any device, by four fifths of what is in sight. A lesson's worked example comes by the property `workedExample`, in place of a task: its statement stands above the sheet and its grid on it, but the learner does not write there — a finger or a pen scrolls the sheet as the browser scrolls the statement, and the row, named Worked example, has Next, Demo, Up and Down. Next opens the parts of the author's demo one at a time, into a numbered list above the sheet that a polite region tells a screen reader of, and Demo hides the whole demo and shows it again; the property `demoState` and the event `demochange` tell the app how far the learner has gone, and the app may give it back to a new element after the example — in React's JSX `workedExample` comes before `demoState`, and `ondemochange` listens. The pictures of the demo come from the consumer's `media` with the statement's, one file for each reference, all of them as the example comes, so that a part opened shows its picture at once; a picture on an area of the sheet stands on the sheet itself, stretched over its area, and the sheet scrolls to it as its part opens. The element now waits for the app and the samples page; a test plays the app's part, and `just demo` serves a page to try the element on by hand. A release comes out by itself after CI passes on `main`, when a path it is built from has changed: the package's archive, LICENSE and their sums, built, signed and checked by .github's shared release, and the same archive goes to npm: the first version by hand, the later ones by the release's own npm step, through trusted publishing, with no long-lived token. A pull request from a branch of this repository tries the release and publishes nothing.

## Install

The app takes the package from npm at an exact version, and spec's npm package, whose types the element's contract is in, at the version the package has in its `dependencies`:

```sh
npm install --save-exact @altnovatrail/canvas@<version>
npm install --save-exact --save-dev @altnovatrail/spec@<its version>
```

A pull request that changes the lock file has spec's recipe check it, as NovaTrail's rules ask of every package of `@altnovatrail`: `just -f spec/justfile release-verify-npm package-lock.json`, from a checkout of spec at a tag. It takes the archive from the registry only if it is the same bytes as the file of canvas's release, signed by .github's shared release at the call of canvas's `release.yml` on `main`.

## Code

- `src/core/` — the DOM-free part, `@altnovatrail/canvas/core`: it runs the same in a browser and in Node, and its entry point gives the sheet's size, the types of the data and the fold of a history into its strokes.
- `src/element/` — the element `<novatrail-canvas>`, `@altnovatrail/canvas`: importing it registers the element. The types of its contract with the app — the element as `CanvasElement` has it, its events, the history of writing, the work for checking and its context — are spec's, from `@altnovatrail/spec/canvas`, and this entry point gives them under spec's names, the same types; the DOM-free part gives the data's among them, the history of writing and the context of a submission. The app takes them from spec's package, `import type { CanvasElement } from "@altnovatrail/spec/canvas"`, at the version canvas has in its `dependencies`; canvas's own are only what the contract does not name: the tutor's marks, the media and the task's temporary tools.
- `scripts/` — the check of the package as a consumer takes it, and of the archive a release puts out.
- `demo/` — a page to try the element on by hand, as the app is to have it: it gives the element a language and its task the tools allowed, keeps the history of writing as the element tells it and opens the sheet again from it, draws the groups of the strokes and the crop for checking under the sheet, gives the tutor's marks back over the crop Check sent, and shows a workbook's pages in the font and the language it gives, how many boxes are filled, the crop of each filled box Check sent and a mark given back on a box, and a lesson's worked example on a new element, on which nothing is written, whose demo opens part by part — a picture on an area of the sheet on the sheet itself — the page keeping how far it has gone and giving it back to the next element. `just demo` serves it on this machine.
- `vectors/` — the test vectors of the rule of stroke groups, of the crop for checking, of the snapping of a model's box to the strokes, of the workbook's page layout and of the rule of boxes, which their tests write and keep current.
- `licenses.json` — the packages the license check accepts by name, each with why.

`just build` builds the package into `lib/`, and `just release-files v0.1.0` writes the files of that release into `dist/` — the package's archive, LICENSE and SHA256SUMS — and prints the sums, which a build of the same commit gives again. `just ci` runs the checks CI runs: format, lint and types; the tests in Node and in Chromium; advisories; licenses. They run in the devcontainer, whose toolchain stage holds the browser the tests run in. The license check is spec's recipe, run from the `spec/` submodule with the exceptions in `licenses.json`, so it needs `spec/` checked out: see [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md), Environment.
